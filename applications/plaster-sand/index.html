Plaster Sand Applications for Internal, External & Ceiling Plastering

A better plaster finish starts with the right sand. Plaster Sand, commercially known as P-Sand, is a finer grade of manufactured sand processed and classified for use in plaster mortar.

Unlike ordinary concrete-grade fine aggregate, plaster sand is selected against the grading and quality requirements relevant to plastering. It contributes to mortar workability, application characteristics and the texture of the finished surface.

From internal walls and exterior rendering to ceiling plastering, choosing the correct sand is an important part of preparing a suitable plaster system. Understand how P-Sand works, what IS 1542:1992 requires and how to select the material for your construction project.

      Is Plaster Sand the same as M-Sand?

      No. They belong to the same broad family of manufactured fine aggregates, but their intended uses and grading targets differ. P-Sand is classified for plaster mortar under IS 1542. Concrete-grade M-Sand is evaluated for concrete under IS 383. Select the material for the actual application and its test results.

      Why does plastering need a different sand?

      Concrete is placed as a relatively thick mass with coarse aggregate in it. Plaster is a comparatively thin mortar layer applied across a wall or ceiling. The sand has to support that thin applied layer, and its grading influences workability, cohesion, trowel handling, surface texture, material demand and consistency between batches.

      What makes P-Sand different from ordinary crusher dust?

      Crushing makes fine particles, but fine particles alone do not make plaster-grade sand. Unclassified crusher dust may have an uncontrolled particle-size distribution, fines content and consistency. P-Sand is processed and classified and assessed against IS 1542:1992. Not every material sold as crusher dust is suitable plaster sand.

      Why a different grading

      A Thin Mortar Layer Requires Controlled Particle Distribution

      Plastering is different from structural concrete placement. A plaster layer is applied against a prepared background, then levelled and finished.

      The particle-size distribution influences how the mortar handles. Choose a factor to see how the sand takes part, and what else decides the result.

      Choose a factor

      Factor 01

      The mortar has to spread and shape

      The mortar must be workable enough to spread and shape with the chosen method. Sand grading, particle shape, fines, binder and water content together set how it handles.

      Factor 02

      It has to hold together on the wall

      The sand contributes to the aggregate structure while binder and water form the paste. A material with unsuitable grading, or with excessive unwanted fines, can change the consistency of the mortar.

      Factor 03

      The finish starts in the particle distribution

      The particle distribution influences the texture achievable in trowelling and finishing, but the final appearance also depends on the finishing method and the system. P-Sand does not independently guarantee a perfectly smooth or paint-ready wall.

      Factor 04

      Sizes spread through the whole mass

      IS 1542 requires the various particle sizes to be uniformly distributed throughout the sand. That is why the complete grading is evaluated, rather than one sieve result on its own.

      Factor 05

      Batch to batch

      A plastering contractor needs material that behaves consistently from batch to batch. Variations in grading, fines or moisture can force changes in site practice and affect how uniform the finish is.

      Plaster Sand is not simply finer sand. It is a fine aggregate selected against the specific requirements of plaster mortar. It does not promise a perfectly smooth wall.

      IS 1542:1992 grading requirements

      Understand the Grading Before Selecting the Material

      IS 1542:1992, Sand for Plaster: Specification, covers naturally occurring sand, crushed stone sand and crushed gravel sand for mortar used in internal wall plastering, ceiling plastering and external plastering.

      Its scope includes plaster systems using lime, cement, composite lime-cement, activated lime pozzolana mixture (ALPM) or gypsum, with or without admixtures. That does not mean every manufactured plaster sand suits every proprietary gypsum or special plastering system: the material must meet the specification and the binder system must be compatible with it.

      Which standard applies to Plaster Sand?

      IS 1542:1992 is the Indian Standard specification for sand used in internal wall, external wall and ceiling plastering. A reference to a standard is not the same as a product certification: this page does not claim a BIS licence or Standard Mark for any OM GROUP delivery.

      What is the grading requirement for P-Sand?

      IS 1542:1992 specifies 95–100% passing at 4.75 mm, 95–100% at 2.36 mm, 90–100% at 1.18 mm, 80–100% at 600 microns, 20–65% at 300 microns and 0–15% at 150 microns. For crushed stone sand, the 150-micron passing limit is increased to 20%. The actual grading of any supply must be confirmed from its own test report.

        Why does a grading report matter?

        A grading report gives the distribution of material retained on and passing the specified sieves. It helps the purchaser see whether the sand suits the specified plastering application. A sand that looks fine or smooth is not automatically inside the limits.

        What does the 150-micron allowance change?

        Only the finest row, and only for crushed stone and crushed gravel sand. It lets the passing limit rise from 15 to 20 percent. The other six rows are unchanged, and the allowance does not turn 20 percent into a goal.

        Fineness modulus

        One Index. Not the Complete Grading Story.

        Fineness modulus is a numerical index derived from cumulative sieve-analysis results. It gives a general indication of how coarse or fine the sand is on average.

        A higher value generally means a coarser grading trend, a lower value a finer one. It does not replace the sieve-analysis report: two different grading profiles can have similar fineness modulus values, so the full distribution across the specified sieves must still be reviewed.

        What is the minimum fineness modulus of P-Sand?

        The minimum fineness modulus under IS 1542 is 1.4 for crushed stone sand and crushed gravel sand. For natural sand, the minimum is 1.5. The full particle-size distribution must still be verified through sieve analysis.

        Fines, clay, silt and dust

        Controlled Fines. Clean Material. Consistent Mortar.

        Fine material is a normal part of plaster sand. The important issue is the nature and the quantity of the fines.

        IS 1542 requires sand to be hard, durable and clean, free from adherent coatings and organic matter. Under Clause 4.3, the combined clay, silt and dust is not more than 5 percent by weight, determined by the referenced test method, subject to the standard's provisions for further tests and for specified exceptions.

          Not every fine particle is harmful

          Choose a kind of fine material

          Normal

          A controlled fine fraction is expected

          Plaster sand is not required to contain zero fine particles. IS 1542 sets grading limits and a fineness-modulus minimum, and a controlled fine fraction is part of the expected distribution. Zero fines is not the objective.

          Deleterious

          Clay-bearing particles are not clean mineral fines

          Clay-bearing particles must not be treated as equivalent to clean mineral fines. They are assessed with silt and dust under the combined limit of not more than 5 percent by weight (Clause 4.3, by the referenced method).

          Deleterious

          Counted with clay in the combined limit

          Silt and dust are assessed together with clay. Depending on type and quantity, excess unwanted fines may influence water demand, mortar workability, cohesion, surface quality, drying behaviour and consistency.

          Impurity

          Assessed by colour comparison

          The standard also sets a requirement for organic impurities using a colour comparison method. Organic contamination must not be ignored: it can affect plaster quality and appearance.

          Qualified

          Crushed stone sand with more fine dust

          IS 1542 notes that in particular cases crushed stone sand with a higher proportion of fine dust may be satisfactory, subject to agreement between supplier and purchaser and the technical evaluation that needs. This is not blanket permission to sell unclassified crusher dust as P-Sand.

          P-Sand contributes the aggregate fraction. The binder provides the setting and hardening mechanism, and the water lets the mortar be mixed and applied and takes part in the binder's hardening. No mix proportion is given here.

                          FAQ

                          Plaster Sand Applications: Frequently Asked Questions

                          1. What is Plaster Sand?

                          Plaster Sand is fine aggregate selected for plaster mortar. OM GROUP's P-Sand is manufactured from crushed rock and processed and graded for plastering applications under IS 1542:1992.

                          2. What is P-Sand?

                          P-Sand is the commercial name OM GROUP uses for its Plaster Sand. It is a manufactured fine aggregate intended for internal, external and ceiling plastering.

                          3. What is Plaster Sand used for?

                          Plaster Sand is used for internal wall plastering, external wall plastering and ceiling plastering when it meets the applicable grading and quality requirements.

                          4. Which Indian Standard applies to Plaster Sand?

                          IS 1542:1992 is the Indian Standard specification for sand used in plastering.

                          5. Does IS 1542 apply to manufactured sand?

                          Yes. IS 1542 covers natural sand, crushed stone sand and crushed gravel sand used in plaster mortar, provided the material meets the standard's requirements.

                          6. What is the difference between P-Sand and M-Sand?

                          P-Sand is processed and classified for plaster mortar under IS 1542:1992. Concrete-grade M-Sand is selected for concrete applications under the relevant requirements of IS 383:2016.

                          7. Is P-Sand finer than M-Sand?

                          P-Sand is a plaster-specific grade of manufactured sand. Its grading is selected against the plaster-sand requirements rather than the concrete fine-aggregate grading requirements. The actual particle-size distribution must be confirmed through sieve analysis.

                          8. What grading does Plaster Sand require?

                          IS 1542:1992 specifies grading from 10mm to 150 microns. It requires 100% passing 10mm, 95–100% passing 4.75mm, 95–100% at 2.36mm, 90–100% at 1.18mm, 80–100% at 600 microns, 20–65% at 300 microns and 0–15% at 150 microns. Crushed stone and crushed gravel sands have a special allowance of up to 20% passing the 150-micron sieve.

                          9. What is the minimum fineness modulus for P-Sand?

                          IS 1542:1992 specifies a minimum fineness modulus of 1.4 for crushed stone sand and crushed gravel sand. For naturally occurring sand, the minimum is 1.5.

                          10. What is the permissible clay, silt and dust content?

                          IS 1542:1992 specifies a combined maximum of 5% by weight for clay, silt and dust, determined using the referenced test method, subject to the provisions within the standard.

                          11. Does Plaster Sand contain fines?

                          Yes. A controlled fine fraction is part of the grading requirements. The objective is to meet the specified grading and cleanliness requirements, not to eliminate all fine particles.

                          12. Is stone dust the same as Plaster Sand?

                          No. Unclassified stone dust may have uncontrolled grading and fines content. Plaster Sand is processed and classified for plaster mortar against IS 1542:1992.

                          13. Can P-Sand be used for internal wall plastering?

                          Yes. OM GROUP lists internal wall plastering as one of the primary applications of its P-Sand.

                          14. Can P-Sand be used for external plastering?

                          Yes. P-Sand is intended for specified external plastering applications where its grading and the complete mortar system meet the project requirements.

                          15. Can P-Sand be used for ceiling plastering?

                          Yes. IS 1542 covers sand for ceiling plastering. The selected mortar and application method must also be suitable for overhead work.

                          16. Can P-Sand be used on brick walls?

                          Yes, when the material and plaster mortar meet the specification and the brick background is appropriately prepared.

                          17. Can P-Sand be used on concrete walls?

                          P-Sand may be used where the selected plastering system permits it. Concrete backgrounds may require cleaning, roughening or other preparation to provide adequate key and suction conditions.

                          18. Can P-Sand be used on concrete columns and beams?

                          Yes, for specified plaster finishes on suitable prepared structural surfaces. Junctions and concrete background conditions must be treated according to the project details.

                          19. Can P-Sand be used on AAC blocks?

                          It may be used in a suitable plaster system where the material and mortar formulation are compatible with the AAC background. Follow the block manufacturer's and plaster-system supplier's recommendations.

                          20. Can P-Sand be used for cement plaster?

                          Yes. IS 1542 covers sand used in cement-based plaster mortar when the material meets the standard's requirements.

                          21. Can P-Sand be used for cement-lime plaster?

                          IS 1542 covers sand used in cement-lime plaster mortar. The actual mortar formulation must be suitable for the selected system.

                          22. Can P-Sand be used for gypsum plaster?

                          IS 1542's scope includes sand used in specified gypsum-based plaster systems. However, the sand must be compatible with the selected gypsum system and its manufacturer or project requirements.

                          23. Can P-Sand be used for rendering?

                          Yes, where the specification calls for a plaster sand meeting the relevant requirements. External rendering may involve additional detailing and exposure considerations.

                          24. Does P-Sand guarantee a smooth finish?

                          No. P-Sand provides a plaster-grade fine aggregate, but the finished appearance also depends on the mortar, substrate, application technique, thickness, tools and workmanship.

                          25. Does P-Sand prevent plaster cracking?

                          No plaster sand can guarantee crack-free plaster. Cracks can result from movement, shrinkage, excess water, rapid drying, poor curing, excessive thickness, substrate conditions and workmanship.

                          26. What causes plaster to become hollow?

                          Hollow patches can be related to inadequate bonding, background contamination, insufficient surface key, improper suction adjustment, incompatible materials or application problems. The actual cause should be inspected on site.

                          27. Why is curing important after plastering?

                          Curing supports moisture retention during the development of cement-based plaster properties. IS 1661:1972 provides guidance for damp curing of plaster coats and the completed finish. The actual project specification should govern the procedure.

                          28. Is the 1:6 proportion in IS 1542 the site plaster mix ratio?

                          No. The one-part-cement-to-six-parts-sand proportion is used for the specific laboratory mortar-cube strength requirement under IS 1542. It is not a universal site plaster mix recommendation.

                          29. What compressive strength does IS 1542 require in the mortar test?

                          The standard specifies an average minimum compressive strength of 3 N/mm² at 28 days for the defined laboratory mortar cubes using one part cement and six parts conforming sand, with water adjusted to the specified flow.

                          30. What is the difference between Plaster Sand and River Sand?

                          P-Sand is manufactured by crushing and processing rock. River Sand is naturally deposited. IS 1542 permits both sources when they meet the specified requirements.

                          31. What is the difference between Plaster Sand and Washed Sand?

                          P-Sand is a plaster-grade manufactured product. Washed Sand describes a material that has undergone washing. Washing alone does not establish that a sand meets the plaster grading requirements.

                          32. What is the difference between P-Sand and Concrete Sand?

                          P-Sand is classified for plaster mortar under IS 1542. Concrete Sand is selected for concrete mix requirements, generally under IS 383. They should not be treated as interchangeable.

                          33. How is P-Sand tested?

                          The relevant tests include sieve analysis, fineness modulus, clay/silt/dust assessment, organic impurity testing and the specified mortar-strength test. Other test information may be requested according to the project requirement.
