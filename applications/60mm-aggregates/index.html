60mm Crushed Stone Aggregate: Applications in Heavy Foundations, Drainage and Infrastructure Construction

60mm crushed stone aggregate is a large-size commercial crushed-stone grade used in selected heavy-duty construction, drainage, infrastructure fill and specialised foundation applications. Its larger particle size makes it relevant to projects where substantial stone fractions, specified void characteristics and bulk material placement are required.

Unlike conventional 20mm and 40mm concrete aggregates, 60mm is a commercial trade-size description rather than a separately defined nominal coarse-aggregate size under IS 383:2016. Its suitability must therefore be established through actual sieve-analysis results and the grading requirements of the project.

From engineered drainage layers and large foundation-related works to selected mass-concrete and infrastructure applications, 60mm aggregate must be selected according to material grading, placement conditions, structural requirements and the applicable project specification.

Understanding the material

What Is 60mm Crushed Stone Aggregate?

60mm crushed stone aggregate is a large-size crushed-rock material commercially identified by a 60mm trade-grade designation.

It is produced through the mechanical crushing, processing and screening of source rock to obtain larger aggregate fractions suitable for specified construction and infrastructure requirements. It is commonly considered for heavy foundation-related works, selected mass-concrete applications, large drainage systems, infrastructure fill, specialised construction and project-specific large-aggregate requirements.

The exact suitability of the material depends on its actual grading, particle shape, fines content and the project specification.

Is 60mm a Standard Nominal Aggregate Size Under IS 383?

Short answer

No. 60mm is not a separately listed nominal coarse-aggregate size under IS 383:2016. The largest listed single-sized coarse aggregate is 63mm, and IS 383:2016 also has separate mass-concrete classes, including a large class of 80mm to 40mm.

60mm should therefore be treated as a commercial trade-grade description whose actual sieve distribution is confirmed against the applicable project or tender specification. This prevents a costly procurement mistake: assuming that a product named 60mm automatically conforms to the 63mm single-sized classification, or to the large mass-concrete classification.

How Is 60mm Aggregate Manufactured?

The general production sequence runs from source rock to dispatch. The 60mm fraction is the one highlighted in the diagram below: source rock is crushed, and a screen separates it into a larger fraction that returns for further crushing, the selected 60mm fraction, and finer fractions.

  1. Source rock extraction

    Hard rock is extracted from the quarry and prepared for processing. The geological characteristics of the source material contribute to the properties of the resulting crushed aggregate.

  2. Primary size reduction

    Large rock pieces enter the crushing system, where mechanical forces reduce them into smaller fragments.

  3. Further crushing and processing

    The material is processed according to the desired aggregate fractions and production requirements.

  4. Screening and separation

    Screening equipment separates the processed material according to the required particle-size ranges.

  5. Stockpiling

    The large-size material is kept separately from smaller fractions to reduce contamination and unwanted mixing.

  6. Dispatch

    The finished material is loaded for transportation according to quantity, delivery location and project requirements.

Why Is 60mm Considered a Large-Size Aggregate?

60mm is larger than the conventional coarse-aggregate sizes commonly used in ordinary RCC construction. Its larger particle size may be relevant where the project requires large stone fractions, greater particle spacing, bulk material placement, specified large-size drainage media, large-aggregate infrastructure fill or other specialised construction materials.

Larger particles also create limitations. They require sufficient space for handling and placement, and may be unsuitable for narrow sections, congested reinforcement or systems requiring a finer grading distribution.

Positioning

60mm crushed stone aggregate for heavy-duty infrastructure, large foundation applications, specified drainage systems, mass-concrete-related works and specialised construction where the project specification requires a large-size crushed-stone fraction.

01

Large-size drainage media and drainage trenches

Primary · important application

Drainage systems use appropriately selected granular materials to facilitate water movement through or away from a particular area. Large-size crushed stone can be used in selected drainage installations where the design requires substantial particle sizes and an interconnected void structure. A commercial 60mm fraction may be considered where its grading and hydraulic characteristics meet the project requirements.

Why are larger particles used in selected drainage systems?

Larger particles can create relatively large spaces between adjacent stones. When the particle grading and placement are appropriate, these spaces can provide pathways for water to move through a drainage layer. The actual drainage performance depends on the entire granular system.

Potential applications

  • Selected subsurface drainage trenches.
  • Large drainage media installations.
  • Specified pipe-surround drainage arrangements.
  • Certain site-development drainage systems.
  • Selected landscape drainage applications.
  • Engineered drainage layers around infrastructure.
  • Certain soakaway systems, where designed appropriately.

What must the drainage designer verify?

  1. Required particle-size distribution.
  2. Fines content.
  3. Hydraulic conductivity or permeability requirements.
  4. Surrounding soil characteristics.
  5. Filter compatibility.
  6. Geotextile requirements, where applicable.
  7. Pipe diameter and perforation arrangement.
  8. Layer thickness.
  9. Long-term resistance to clogging.
Drainage media is not filter material

A coarse drainage aggregate is not necessarily a suitable filter material. A filter layer must be designed to retain the surrounding soil while allowing water to pass. A 60mm stone fraction may provide a drainage function but may be unsuitable as the only filter layer if it permits migration of surrounding fines. That is why a properly designed system may use separate granular layers or geotextile.

02

Heavy foundation-related works and large foundation bases

Project-specific application

Large industrial and infrastructure projects often involve substantial foundation-related construction work. Depending on the design, this may include large foundation excavations, foundation preparation, engineered granular fill, selected non-structural bedding, foundation-related drainage and compacted stone layers. 60mm aggregate may be considered for selected foundation-related fill or granular applications where the project specification calls for a large-size material.

Why might a project require large-size aggregate?

Some large construction projects need a stone fraction that contributes to the required granular material arrangement. An appropriately specified large aggregate can offer a coarse granular structure, a reduced proportion of fine particles if the grading supports it, potentially large interconnected voids and suitability for selected bulk placement operations. These advantages are conditional on the actual grading and construction method.

Foundation fill is not structural foundation concrete

Foundation fill or granular preparation: a specified stone layer beneath or around a foundation may use large aggregate, subject to the geotechnical and construction specification.

Structural reinforced foundation concrete: governed by structural concrete design and aggregate-size restrictions. A commercial 60mm product should not be recommended as a routine aggregate for reinforced footings, pile caps or heavily reinforced foundation members. IRC:112-2020 identifies 20mm as preferred for reinforced and prestressed concrete, with sizes up to 40mm permitted in special cases where there is no restriction to concrete flow.

Potential applications

  • Selected large granular foundation bases.
  • Engineered foundation-related fill.
  • Certain industrial site foundation preparation works.
  • Specified non-structural large-stone layers.
  • Project-specific mass-fill applications.

Understanding the Role of 60mm

60mm should not be treated as a complete mass-concrete grading on its own. An engineered mixture can benefit from an aggregate distribution designed around particle packing, paste requirements, water demand, workability, placement, consolidation, thermal performance and durability. Larger fractions influence the granular structure of the concrete but must work together with the remaining aggregate fractions, binder, water and admixtures in an approved mix.

What must the engineer assess?

  1. Required mass-concrete grading.
  2. Individual aggregate sieve distributions.
  3. Maximum particle size.
  4. Concrete placement equipment.
  5. Ability to consolidate the mixture.
  6. Mix workability.
  7. Water and cementitious-material requirements.
  8. Heat of hydration and temperature differences across the section.
  9. Construction sequencing, curing and temperature management.
Does 60mm aggregate prevent thermal cracking?

No. Thermal cracking is influenced by the heat generated in concrete, temperature differences, restraint, material properties and construction conditions. Larger aggregate may contribute to an engineered mixture, but it does not independently eliminate temperature-related cracking.

Particle size, voids and grading

Why Does 60mm Aggregate Size Matter in Infrastructure and Heavy Construction?

The size of a crushed-stone particle influences more than its physical appearance.

In large-scale construction, particle size affects how material is transported, placed, packed and consolidated. For drainage, the size distribution influences void spaces and water movement. For concrete, it influences aggregate grading, paste requirements and placement restrictions. For fill, it influences compaction, settlement and the interaction between the material and the underlying ground. These are different engineering functions and are explained separately below.

01Particle-size distribution

Particle-size distribution describes the proportion of material falling within different sieve ranges. A 60mm trade designation alone does not establish the actual distribution. The grading report should identify the upper and lower particle-size limits, the percentage passing and retained on each specified sieve, the finer-material content, any oversized particles and the project acceptance limits.

A consistent grading is important for controlling material behaviour.

02Void spaces between large particles

When large stones are placed together, spaces remain between adjacent particles. Their size, connectivity and distribution depend on particle geometry, particle-size distribution, packing arrangement, compaction and the finer particles occupying the available spaces.

In a suitably designed drainage layer, connected voids can facilitate water movement. Not every large-size aggregate creates a satisfactory drainage layer, and a material containing excessive fines may obstruct the available flow pathways.

03Drainage and hydraulic performance

Drainage systems use aggregate to create pathways through which water can move, and the performance of the system depends on its permeability and its compatibility with the surrounding material.

  • Aggregate grading and particle shape.
  • Fines content and layer thickness.
  • Soil characteristics and filter fabric.
  • Drainage pipe arrangement.

A 60mm product should be specified for drainage only after these requirements have been established.

04Particle size and surface area

For otherwise comparable particle systems, larger aggregate generally has less total surface area per unit volume than smaller aggregate. This can influence the amount of cement paste required to coat the particles and fill the spaces within a concrete mixture, and is one reason larger aggregate is considered in selected large-volume concrete applications.

This does not establish that 60mm material automatically reduces cement consumption. The exact grading, aggregate combination, binder content and concrete design determine the actual result.

05Packing and material settlement

In large fill or foundation-related construction, the arrangement of particles affects the granular structure. An appropriately designed particle-size distribution can contribute to stable packing, whereas an inadequately designed single-size layer may contain significant voids or may not meet the required compaction and settlement criteria.

The construction specification should establish layer thickness, placement procedure, compaction method, particle-size limits, settlement tolerance and the testing and acceptance conditions.

06Compaction and consolidation

Large particles require suitable handling and placement equipment, and the compaction process must be appropriate for the particle size and the intended layer. A method that works for ordinary soil or smaller graded aggregate cannot automatically be transferred to a large stone fill.

The engineer or geotechnical specialist must establish whether the specified material is compacted granular fill, open-graded drainage media, rockfill, foundation bedding or another engineered layer.

07Concrete placement limitations

The 60mm trade grade must not be treated as a routine aggregate for reinforced concrete. Large particles can obstruct movement through restricted reinforcement spaces and complicate consolidation. The issue is not merely the nominal size: the actual particle distribution and the available clearances matter.

Where concrete is involved, the structural designer must verify the maximum permissible aggregate size, the placement method and the approved mix.

08Segregation and material separation

Segregation can occur when different particle sizes separate during handling or placement. In granular fill it can create areas with different grading characteristics, and in concrete it can produce inconsistent material distribution.

Controlled loading, transport, stockpiling and placement help maintain the intended material distribution.

09Fines migration and filter compatibility

Large drainage particles may create connected voids, but those spaces can allow surrounding soil fines to move into the drainage material. This may progressively reduce drainage performance.

An appropriately designed filter arrangement should retain the surrounding soil while allowing water to pass. A geotextile or a graded granular transition layer may be required, depending on the soil, the aggregate and the hydraulic design.

10Durability and long-term performance

The durability of a large-size crushed-stone material depends on the source rock and the requirements of the application. Relevant characteristics can include resistance to weathering, particle strength, resistance to abrasion where applicable, water absorption, source-rock composition, deleterious materials and resistance to breakdown during placement.

A commercial 60mm designation is not evidence of a particular durability rating.

Engineering principle

The 60mm grade is useful where the project requires a large crushed-stone fraction. Its selection must be based on the required particle-size distribution and the function of the material: drainage, fill, mass-concrete grading or specialised infrastructure.

Choosing the right material

40mm vs 60mm vs 63mm Aggregate: Understanding the Difference

The difference between 40mm, 60mm and 63mm is not merely the number displayed against the material.

Each description has a different classification and specification context. 40mm is a recognised nominal aggregate size under IS 383:2016. 63mm is the largest nominal single-sized coarse aggregate category listed under Table 7. 60mm is OM GROUP's commercial trade-grade description, and its actual grading must be assessed against the project specification rather than assumed to match 63mm.

When should 40mm be considered?

40mm is commonly considered for larger concrete sections, selected PCC, foundations and mass-concrete applications where the approved design permits it. It has a defined nominal coarse-aggregate classification under IS 383:2016.

When should 60mm be considered?

60mm may be considered when a project calls for a large-size commercial crushed-stone fraction for drainage, foundation-related fill, large infrastructure or specified mass-concrete grading. Its actual grading and intended use must be confirmed before procurement.

When should 63mm be considered?

63mm should be considered where the project specification expressly calls for the recognised 63mm single-sized coarse aggregate classification under IS 383:2016. The supplied material must meet the applicable Table 7 grading.

Is 60mm the same as 63mm?

Not automatically. The difference must be established from sieve-analysis results. A supplier's use of a 60mm trade description cannot replace the project's 63mm material specification.

60mm Aggregate vs GSB vs WMM: Do Not Buy the Wrong Material

An individual 60mm fraction is not automatically a compliant finished GSB or WMM material.

GSB is a complete graded granular sub-base with a particle-size distribution set by the relevant MoRTH specification. WMM is a processed, moisture-conditioned road-base material manufactured from specified aggregate fractions. A standalone 60mm product is a different material, and OM GROUP sells them separately.

Quality, grading and material selection

How to Evaluate the Quality of 60mm Crushed Stone Aggregate

Evaluating large-size aggregate requires special attention to the actual grading and the intended construction application.

The nominal trade description alone cannot establish whether the material is suitable for drainage, mass concrete, infrastructure fill or another specialised use. A supplier's use of the term "60mm" does not independently establish material compliance.

The laboratory stations

Select a station to read what the test measures. The limits shown are standard acceptance provisions. For a 60mm material, the testing laboratory decides which methods suit the particle-size range.

No laboratory findings, certificates or accreditation are shown or implied.

01Project-specific sieve analysis

Sieve analysis is the starting point for establishing particle-size distribution. For a commercial 60mm grade, the buyer should request results against the actual sieve sizes and grading limits stated in the tender or project specification.

  • Sample identification and collection date.
  • Sieve designations, percentage passing and retained.
  • Maximum and minimum particle size, if specified.
  • Fines content and acceptance limits.
  • Testing procedure.

Do not use the 63mm IS 383 grading table as a substitute for the actual 60mm product report.

02Oversized particle assessment

An important concern with large-size aggregate is the presence of particles beyond the permitted maximum size. Oversized stones can interfere with drainage installation, fill-layer placement, handling equipment, compaction arrangements, concrete placement and compliance with the project grading. The acceptable maximum size must come from the project specification.

03Particle-size consistency

Even within a commercially named 60mm stockpile, individual particles can vary. A consistent distribution matters because variation can change void structure, packing behaviour, layer thickness, compaction characteristics and drainage behaviour. For large infrastructure projects, repeated sieve checks may be necessary.

04Fines and dust content

Fine material can occupy the spaces between large particles. This may be acceptable in some graded fill applications but undesirable in certain open-graded drainage systems, so the acceptable fines limit depends on the intended application. A material intended for drainage must be assessed differently from one intended for a compacted granular fill layer.

05Particle shape

Crushed stone particles can be angular, elongated, flaky or more cubical. Shape affects packing, interlocking, void arrangement, compaction and handling, and the relevant testing method must be appropriate for the supplied particle-size range.

06Specific gravity and density

Specific gravity is useful when characterising aggregate for concrete and related applications. Bulk density depends on grading, particle shape, moisture, packing arrangement, compaction condition and test procedure.

A universal conversion of 60mm aggregate into tonnes per cubic metre should not be published without actual measurement and an explanation of the test condition.

07Water absorption

Water absorption describes the quantity of water absorbed by the aggregate under prescribed test conditions. For concrete applications it can be relevant to mix-water corrections and material proportioning. The suitability of particular test methods must be confirmed for the actual large particle-size range.

08Deleterious materials and cleanliness

Large stone material should be examined for unwanted constituents: clay contamination, organic impurities, soft or weathered particles, excessive fines and other harmful materials specified by the project. For drainage and fill applications, cleanliness is particularly relevant.

09Mechanical resistance

Depending on the application, relevant assessments may include aggregate crushing, impact and abrasion resistance, durability and soundness. Standard test methods often have defined specimen-size and applicability conditions.

Do not automatically apply the test procedure or acceptance limits used for 20mm concrete aggregate to a commercial 60mm material. The testing laboratory must determine the appropriate method, sample preparation and acceptance criteria.

10Source-rock characteristics

The nature of the source rock contributes to its physical and mechanical properties. For certain infrastructure applications, the engineer may require additional source-material assessment, petrographic examination or geological documentation.

11Consistency between deliveries

For large construction projects, material consistency is commercially important. Variation in grading across deliveries can change the performance of a granular layer or blended material. An agreed product specification, representative sampling and appropriate incoming-material controls can help manage the risk.

12Drainage-specific quality checks

For drainage applications the material should be assessed against the hydraulic design: particle distribution, maximum and minimum particle size, fines content, permeability, filter compatibility, resistance to soil migration and long-term clogging risk.

A 60mm designation alone cannot establish hydraulic suitability.

Selection guide

How to Choose Between 40mm, 60mm and 63mm Aggregate for Your Project

Selecting a large-size crushed-stone material should begin with the intended application and the project's written specification.

The commercial name alone should never determine material selection. A 60mm trade-grade aggregate, 63mm single-sized aggregate, large mass-concrete aggregate and construction boulders may have different grading, dimensional and performance requirements.

  1. Identify the actual construction application

    Establish whether the requirement is for drainage media, foundation-related fill, engineered granular fill, mass-concrete aggregate, infrastructure fill, embankment, gabion or rock-protection material, or other specialised construction.

  2. Determine whether the project needs concrete aggregate or fill material

    For concrete, the aggregate must comply with the applicable material specification and approved concrete mix design. For granular fill, drainage and embankment, the applicable geotechnical or infrastructure specification determines the required material.

  3. Identify the specified particle-size range

    Check whether the project calls for commercial 60mm trade-grade material, 63mm nominal single-sized aggregate, 80mm-to-40mm large mass-concrete aggregate, a project-specific combined grading, or a larger rock or boulder material.

  4. Review the grading requirement

    Request the specific sieve-size distribution. Do not assume that 60mm is automatically equivalent to 63mm.

  5. Verify application suitability

    For drainage, assess hydraulic and filter requirements. For mass concrete, verify the combined grading and concrete mix design. For foundation-related fill, establish the geotechnical and compaction requirements. For gabions, verify the stone-size compatibility with the mesh and structural design.

  6. Review material quality

    Establish the relevant testing requirements. The testing programme must reflect the material's size and intended application.

  7. Confirm placement and construction methods

    Consider excavation and access, loading and unloading arrangements, machinery requirements, placement-layer thickness, compaction requirements, drainage arrangements and site conditions.

  8. Confirm procurement and dispatch

    Establish the total quantity, unit, delivery location and supply schedule.

Practical selection examples

Example A · A large drainage trench

The project engineer specifies clean, large-size drainage media with defined fines and filtration requirements. The 60mm product may be considered only after the grading report confirms its suitability.

Example B · A large industrial foundation

The project requires a specified coarse granular layer beneath the structural foundation. The geotechnical design establishes the particle-size distribution and compaction requirements. A 60mm fraction may be considered if it satisfies those requirements.

The structural specification requires a conventional concrete mix. A 60mm trade-grade product should not be selected merely because the foundation is large. The aggregate-size restrictions and approved concrete design determine the appropriate size.

Example D · A mass-concrete project

The designer requires a combined large-aggregate grading. The material may involve several fractions, potentially including a 40mm-to-60mm commercial grade, subject to sieve analysis and the approved mix.

These examples show selection logic, not ready-made engineering recommendations.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About 60mm Crushed Stone Aggregate

1. What is 60mm crushed stone aggregate?

60mm crushed stone aggregate is a large-size commercial crushed-stone grade produced through crushing and screening. It is used in selected drainage, infrastructure fill, foundation-related and specialised construction applications according to the actual grading and project specification.

2. What is 60mm aggregate used for?

60mm aggregate is used in selected heavy foundation-related works, drainage media, large-scale infrastructure fill and specialised construction. It may also form part of an engineered mass-concrete grading where its actual particle distribution meets the required specification.

3. Is 60mm an IS 383:2016 nominal coarse-aggregate size?

No. IS 383:2016 lists 63mm as its largest nominal single-sized coarse-aggregate category. 60mm is a commercial trade-grade description, and its actual grading must be confirmed against the project specification.

4. Is 60mm aggregate the same as 63mm aggregate?

Not automatically. 63mm is a recognised nominal single-sized aggregate under IS 383:2016, whereas 60mm is a commercial trade description. Their equivalence must be established through sieve-analysis results and the project grading requirements.

5. Can 60mm aggregate be used in mass concrete?

It may form part of a specified large-aggregate mass-concrete system where its actual grading complies with the approved specification. IS 383:2016 has separate mass-concrete aggregate classes, including a large class from 80mm to 40mm. A commercial 60mm grade does not automatically qualify as that complete class.

6. Is 60mm aggregate suitable for heavy foundations?

It may be used in selected foundation-related granular fill or large-stone applications where the project permits it. It should not automatically be specified as aggregate in reinforced structural foundation concrete.

7. Can 60mm aggregate be used in RCC?

It is generally not suitable for conventional reinforced concrete members. The large particle size can conflict with member thickness, reinforcement spacing and cover restrictions. Any use in reinforced concrete must be explicitly supported by the applicable structural specification and approved mix design.

8. Can 60mm aggregate be used in PCC?

It should not be assumed to be suitable for ordinary PCC. IRC:112-2020 identifies 20mm to 40mm as preferred nominal coarse-aggregate sizes for plain concrete, with larger sizes permitted only in special cases with supplementary specifications and precautions. A 60mm commercial grade therefore requires explicit project approval.

9. Why are larger aggregate particles considered in specialised infrastructure work?

Larger particles may create a coarse granular structure and relatively large voids. Depending on the grading, they may be relevant to drainage or large-size fill systems. Their suitability depends on the intended engineering function, not size alone.

10. Is 60mm aggregate suitable for drainage?

It may be suitable for selected drainage media applications where the particle-size distribution, fines content, hydraulic requirements and filter compatibility meet the design. Not every commercially supplied 60mm aggregate automatically qualifies as drainage filter material.

11. Does 60mm aggregate improve drainage?

Large particles can create relatively large void spaces, but drainage performance depends on the connected void structure, grading, cleanliness and surrounding soil. Excessive fines or an unsuitable filter arrangement can cause clogging and reduced drainage performance.

12. Can 60mm aggregate be used in drainage trenches?

Yes, where the project specification calls for an appropriate large-size drainage material. The design must consider the trench dimensions, pipe arrangement, soil gradation, filtration requirements and possible use of geotextile.

13. Can 60mm aggregate be used for road sub-base?

It may contribute to specified large-granular fill or sub-base-related work where the project grading allows it. However, a standalone 60mm product is not automatically a compliant GSB material. The complete road-material grading must be established.

14. Is 60mm aggregate the same as GSB?

No. 60mm aggregate is a commercial large-size crushed-stone fraction. GSB is a complete graded granular sub-base material with specified proportions of different particle sizes and finer material. A 60mm fraction alone cannot be treated as complete GSB.

15. Can 60mm aggregate be used in WMM?

Only as an input if the relevant approved road specification and production formulation permit it. WMM is a finished moisture-conditioned and graded road-base material. A standalone 60mm product is not WMM.

16. What is the difference between 60mm and 40mm aggregate?

40mm is a recognised nominal single-sized and graded aggregate under IS 383:2016. 60mm is a commercial large-size grade. The 60mm product is used for more specialised large-stone applications, while 40mm has broader established concrete and infrastructure applications.

17. What is the difference between 60mm aggregate and construction boulders?

60mm aggregate is processed and screened into a large-size crushed-stone fraction. Construction boulders are larger individual stones supplied for applications such as soling, pitching and gabion work. They are different products and should not be substituted automatically.

18. Can 60mm aggregate be used in gabions?

It may be used only where the gabion design, mesh aperture and stone dimensions permit it. Many gabion systems require larger individual stones. The project specification must determine the acceptable stone-size range.

19. What tests are important for 60mm aggregate?

Important checks begin with sieve analysis, oversized-particle assessment, fines content, cleanliness and relevant material properties. Depending on the application, specific gravity, water absorption, source characteristics and mechanical resistance may also need evaluation. The testing laboratory must establish methods suitable for the actual particle-size range.

20. What is the difference between 60mm single-sized aggregate and large mass-concrete aggregate?

60mm is a commercial trade-grade description. Large mass-concrete aggregate under IS 383:2016 is a defined 80mm-to-40mm classification with specific sieve requirements. The actual 60mm product must be tested before it can be considered as part of that grading.

21. Can 40mm and 60mm aggregate be supplied together?

Yes. OM GROUP accepts enquiries for combined 40mm and 60mm requirements, subject to availability and the project specification. No fixed blending ratio should be assumed; the quantities must follow the project’s grading requirement.

22. How do I calculate the quantity of 60mm aggregate required?

For an initial geometric volume calculation, multiply length by width by depth in metres (V = L × W × D). This gives the geometric volume, not the exact purchase quantity. The procurement quantity depends on whether the material is being used as a compacted fill, drainage layer or another construction application, and actual density, compaction, placement and project allowances must be established separately.
