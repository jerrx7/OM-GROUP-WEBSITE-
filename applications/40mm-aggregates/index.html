40mm Crushed Stone Aggregate: Applications in Mass Concrete, PCC and Infrastructure Construction

40mm crushed stone aggregate is a large-size coarse construction material used in selected mass-concrete placements, plain cement concrete (PCC), large foundation works and specified infrastructure applications. Produced by crushing and screening stone into a defined particle-size fraction, it is particularly relevant to concrete sections and construction environments where larger aggregate particles can be accommodated by the design and placement requirements.

Its selection depends on more than nominal particle size. Aggregate grading, section dimensions, reinforcement clearance, concrete flow, placement methods and the approved mix design determine whether 40mm aggregate is suitable for a particular application.

From large concrete foundations and mass-concrete placements to selected road sub-base, embankment and drainage requirements, 40mm crushed stone aggregate serves projects that require an appropriately specified larger aggregate fraction.

Understanding the material

What Is 40mm Crushed Stone Aggregate?

40mm crushed stone aggregate is a large nominal-size coarse aggregate manufactured by mechanically crushing stone and screening it into a specified particle-size distribution.

It is used in selected concrete and infrastructure applications where the project requires a larger coarse-aggregate fraction and where the section dimensions, placement method and grading requirements permit its use.

Under IS 383:2016, 40mm is recognised in both single-sized and graded coarse-aggregate classifications. It is also used in a separate mass-concrete grading framework. The designation 40mm describes a nominal aggregate size, not a guarantee that every stone particle measures exactly 40mm.

Understanding Mass-Concrete Aggregate

Mass concrete has different aggregate-size classifications from the ordinary single-sized coarse-aggregate categories. Under IS 383:2016 Table 8 the four classes are separate material classifications, and the medium class is the one directly relevant to 40mm.

For example, the medium class requires 90–100% passing the 40mm sieve and 0–10% passing the 20mm sieve. The standard also identifies maximum finer fractions for the small class. The percentage limits for the other classes are in the standard and are not reproduced here.

It does not mean that every product sold as single-sized 40mm aggregate automatically qualifies as the medium mass-concrete fraction. A 40mm single-sized aggregate is not automatically equivalent to the medium class of 40mm to 20mm.

Technical takeaway

Three different things carry the number 40: single-sized 40mm aggregate, graded 40mm aggregate, and the 40mm-to-20mm medium mass-concrete aggregate. They must not be presented, or ordered, as interchangeable. The actual sieve-analysis report must be checked.

BIS issued a draft revision to IS 383 in July 2025. It is a draft for comments and is not treated here as a replacement standard. The edition and amendments that apply to a project should be checked before specifying.

How Is 40mm Aggregate Manufactured?

The general manufacturing process runs from source rock to dispatch. The 40mm fraction is the one highlighted below.

  1. Source rock extraction

    Suitable rock is extracted from the source and prepared for processing. The characteristics of the source material influence the physical properties of the finished aggregate.

  2. Primary crushing

    Large rock is reduced into smaller fragments using mechanical crushing equipment.

  3. Secondary crushing and processing

    The crushed material is processed further according to the required output fractions.

  4. Screening and grading

    The processed material passes through screening equipment that separates particle-size fractions.

  5. Stockpiling

    The separated aggregate is stored in dedicated stockpiles to reduce contamination and unwanted mixing.

  6. Loading and dispatch

    The finished material is loaded for delivery according to quantity, grading requirement and destination.

The Role of 40mm Aggregate in Concrete

40mm aggregate forms the larger coarse-particle component of a concrete mixture. It can be relevant to larger concrete sections where the particle size can be accommodated without compromising placement, reinforcement coverage or compaction.

  • Aggregate packing.
  • Combined particle distribution.
  • Cement-paste requirements.
  • Water demand.
  • Concrete workability.
  • Segregation resistance.
  • Placement and consolidation.
  • The performance of the designed concrete mixture.

The suitability of 40mm depends on the complete concrete mix, not the nominal size alone.

Why Can Larger Aggregate Be Relevant in Large Placements?

In a properly designed concrete mixture, larger aggregate particles generally have less total surface area per unit volume than a comparable system of smaller particles. This can influence the amount of cement paste required to coat the particles and fill the available spaces. For some large-volume applications, a suitable larger aggregate size may help achieve an economical mixture.

This does not mean that using 40mm automatically reduces cement consumption or improves concrete performance. The actual proportions must be established through the relevant concrete mix-design procedure and trial batches.

Mass Concrete Applications

Mass concrete involves placing relatively large volumes of concrete in structures or elements where controlling the heat generated during cement hydration, and the resulting temperature differences, may become important.

04

Selected large RCC sections: why 40mm is not suitable everywhere

Special-case application

Although 20mm remains the preferred nominal aggregate size for ordinary reinforced and prestressed concrete under IRC:112-2020, 40mm can be permitted in special reinforced-concrete situations. The critical condition is that the aggregate must be able to flow through the section and comply with the maximum-size restrictions.

Choose a member. The same member is drawn twice: with generous clearances on the left and congested reinforcement on the right. A 40mm stone is drawn at the same scale in both and tries to pass between the bars.

Open sectionGenerous member size and wide clear spacing leave a path for the stone. Concrete can reach the formwork and flow around the steel.
Congested sectionThin member, closely spaced bars and small cover leave a gap narrower than the stone. The stone lodges on the bars, and concrete placement and compaction suffer.
  1. Member thickness. The nominal maximum size should not exceed one-fourth of the minimum member thickness.
  2. Clear bar spacing. Concrete must pass between the bars; in heavily reinforced members the limit is tied to the clear distance between bars.
  3. Concrete cover. The size is also checked against the minimum cover to the reinforcement.
  4. Concrete flow, placement and compaction. The mix must reach every corner and consolidate around the steel.
IS 456 / IRC:112

IS 456:2000, Clause 5.3.3, says the maximum nominal aggregate size should be as large as possible within the specified limits, should not exceed one-fourth of the minimum member thickness, and should allow concrete to be placed around reinforcement and into formwork corners. 20mm is suitable for most work, and where concrete flow is unrestricted 40mm or larger sizes may be permitted. Clause 5.3.3.1 adds a restriction for heavily reinforced members, based on clear bar spacing and cover.

IRC:112-2020 restricts the maximum size to the most restrictive of: one-fourth of the minimum member thickness; 10mm less than the minimum lateral clear distance between individual bars; and 10mm less than the minimum clear cover. It identifies 20mm as preferred for reinforced and prestressed concrete and permits sizes up to 40mm in special cases where there is no restriction to concrete flow.

Concrete behaviour

Why Does 40mm Aggregate Size Matter?

Selecting 40mm aggregate has implications for the entire concrete-production and placement process.

Its larger particle size affects grading, surface area, paste requirements, reinforcement clearance and the ability of fresh concrete to flow through a section. For larger concrete placements, these considerations become particularly important.

01Maximum nominal aggregate size

The maximum nominal aggregate size is an important structural and mix-design parameter. It determines the largest specified coarse-aggregate category permitted for the particular concrete application.

Under IS 456:2000, nominal maximum aggregate size is restricted by member thickness and the ability to place concrete correctly. For reinforced members, additional reinforcement-clearance restrictions apply. Under IRC:112-2020, sizes up to 40mm may be permitted in special reinforced-concrete cases where there is no restriction to concrete flow.

02Particle-size distribution

Larger aggregate does not mean that all particles are identical. Grading describes the distribution of different particle sizes within the material. It influences the way particles combine and how much of the available volume is occupied by solid material.

For 40mm aggregate, the distinction between single-sized and graded classification is especially important.

03Aggregate packing and void content

Concrete contains spaces between its aggregate particles. A suitable combination of sizes can help particles pack more efficiently, and in mass-concrete design this is one reason several aggregate fractions may be combined. A properly designed mixture can reduce unnecessary voids and support an economical paste requirement. Poor grading can have the opposite effect.

04Surface area and cement-paste demand

For otherwise comparable material systems, larger particles generally have less total surface area per unit volume than smaller particles. This can influence the cement paste needed to coat the particles and fill the granular spaces, and it is one factor that can make larger aggregate relevant to large-volume mixes.

A reduction in cement use cannot be claimed simply because the nominal aggregate size is 40mm. Actual proportions must be established through mix design and trials.

05Concrete flow and placement

A concrete mixture must move into the intended section and surround reinforcement where present. Larger aggregate requires adequate space through which the particles can pass. If the section is too restricted, particles can obstruct the flow of concrete.

This is why 40mm is more appropriate for selected large and less congested sections than for conventional reinforced building elements.

06Compaction and consolidation

Concrete must be consolidated to reduce unwanted air voids and ensure proper contact with formwork and reinforcement. Aggregate size can influence how the particles move and rearrange during consolidation. The workability, placing method and vibration or other approved compaction arrangement must be compatible with the aggregate grading.

07Water demand and workability

The water required for concrete is influenced by several factors:

  • Aggregate size, particle shape and surface texture.
  • Aggregate moisture and fine-aggregate grading.
  • Cement content and water-cement ratio.
  • Required slump, admixtures and environmental conditions.

IS 10262:2019 recognises the influence of aggregate size and other material characteristics on water demand and recommends appropriate trial-batch evaluation. Larger particles do not automatically make a concrete mixture workable.

08Segregation and bleeding

Segregation occurs when the constituents of fresh concrete separate during handling or placement. Bleeding is the movement of water towards the concrete surface. Both can be influenced by material proportions, grading and concrete handling.

An unsuitable 40mm mixture may create placement difficulties if the concrete is poorly designed or used in sections with insufficient clearance.

09Mass-concrete heat management

Large concrete sections may generate significant heat as cementitious materials hydrate, and temperature differences between the interior and exterior can produce thermal stresses. Mix design and construction planning may require attention to:

  • Heat of hydration and cementitious material selection.
  • Concrete placing temperature and pour dimensions.
  • Cooling arrangements, construction joints and insulation.
  • Temperature monitoring and curing.

A larger aggregate fraction may contribute to the designed mix, but it does not independently control temperature or eliminate thermal cracking.

10Durability

Concrete durability depends on the quality of the entire mixture and construction process. Important factors include aggregate source characteristics, deleterious material limits, water-cement ratio, concrete cover, compaction, curing, exposure conditions and mix-design compliance.

A 40mm nominal size is not evidence of higher durability.

Key engineering principle

40mm aggregate is useful where the larger particle size can be accommodated by the concrete section and the approved material specification. Its value lies in being the correct aggregate for the intended grading and application, not in simply being larger than 20mm.

When should 20mm aggregate be considered?

20mm is commonly appropriate for conventional RCC slabs, beams, columns and foundations when the project requirements permit it.

When should 40mm aggregate be considered?

40mm may be considered for mass concrete, PCC, large foundation sections and special reinforced-concrete applications where section geometry and placement arrangements permit the size.

When should 60mm aggregate be considered?

60mm may be considered in specialised large-aggregate or infrastructure applications where the applicable specification allows it. It should not be automatically specified for conventional RCC.

Why can larger aggregate be economical?

Larger aggregate can have lower specific surface area relative to an otherwise comparable smaller aggregate system. This can influence the paste required in the designed mixture. However, the actual economic benefit depends on the complete mix, placement, processing and transportation costs. A larger fraction is not automatically cheaper per unit of concrete placed.

Quality, grading and material selection

How to Evaluate the Quality of 40mm Crushed Stone Aggregate

Because 40mm is a larger aggregate fraction, quality assessment should consider both its individual grading and its compatibility with the intended concrete or infrastructure application.

A supplier’s use of the term “40mm” does not independently establish material compliance.

The laboratory stations

Select a station to read what the test measures. The limits shown are standard acceptance provisions.

No laboratory findings, certificates or accreditation are shown or implied.

01Sieve analysis

Sieve analysis establishes the particle-size distribution. For 40mm coarse aggregate, the analysis should distinguish between 40mm single-sized aggregate, 40mm graded aggregate, a project-specific mass-concrete fraction and, where applicable, a road-material fraction.

The report should include:

  • Sample identification and sampling date.
  • Sieve sizes, percentage retained and percentage passing.
  • The applicable grading envelope.
  • Test procedure and testing laboratory identification.

02Oversized material

For 40mm aggregate, the proportion of particles retained on the upper specified sieve is important. Oversized particles can interfere with the intended grading and placement requirements.

03Fines and dust content

The presence of finer material can influence combined grading, paste requirements and drainage performance. Acceptable fines content depends on the intended material specification: an open-graded drainage material, for example, may have different fines requirements from a concrete aggregate.

04Particle shape

Crushed-stone particles can be angular, flaky, elongated or relatively cubical. Shape can influence packing and internal friction. For larger aggregate, particle shape should be assessed against the appropriate size-related testing method and specification.

05Aggregate crushing value

This test evaluates resistance to crushing under a prescribed laboratory procedure. It is important where the relevant concrete or infrastructure specification requires a mechanical-property assessment.

06Aggregate impact value

Impact value assesses resistance to impact under the prescribed method. Acceptance limits depend on the application.

07Los Angeles abrasion value

Abrasion testing evaluates the aggregate’s resistance to surface wear under the specified test. It may be important for materials exposed to abrasion and repeated mechanical action.

08Specific gravity and bulk density

Specific gravity is used in concrete mix calculations. Bulk density is the mass of material occupying a defined bulk volume, including the spaces between particles, and it can vary with grading, particle shape, moisture, compaction state, stockpile condition and test procedure.

A fixed tonnes-per-cubic-metre conversion should not be assumed for all 40mm material.

09Water absorption and moisture

Water absorption and actual aggregate moisture affect batching calculations. In concrete production, the mix designer must account for stockpile moisture when correcting the water addition.

10Deleterious material assessment

The material should be checked for unwanted constituents such as:

  • Clay lumps and soft fragments.
  • Organic impurities and excessive dust.
  • Coal and lignite.
  • Other harmful constituents specified by the applicable standard.

11Soundness and source-rock characteristics

Soundness and petrographic properties may be important for particular exposure and infrastructure requirements. The applicable tests and acceptance criteria should be selected based on the project’s requirements.

12Batch-to-batch consistency

For large infrastructure projects and concrete plants, grading consistency is important. Changes between stockpiles or deliveries may affect the aggregate blend and concrete mix performance. Incoming-material checks and an agreed product specification help manage this risk.

Selection guide

How to Choose Between 20mm, 40mm and 60mm Aggregate for Your Project

Selecting a larger aggregate size requires greater attention to the engineering and placement requirements.

The project should establish the maximum permissible particle size before procurement.

  1. Identify the application

    Establish whether the material is required for mass concrete, PCC, large foundations, reinforced concrete, road sub-base, WMM-related grading, embankment, drainage or other infrastructure applications.

  2. Review the structural geometry

    For concrete sections, identify the minimum member dimension, section thickness, reinforcement arrangement, clear bar spacing, concrete cover and formwork dimensions.

  3. Determine the maximum permitted aggregate size

    Refer to the relevant structural code and project specification. For reinforced concrete, check IS 456 or the applicable IRC code. For mass concrete, check the applicable mass-concrete requirements and aggregate classification.

  4. Identify the required aggregate classification

    Determine whether the project requires single-sized 40mm, graded 40mm, the medium mass-concrete fraction, a complete combined mass-concrete grading or a specified granular road material. These are not interchangeable.

  5. Review the complete grading

    Obtain individual sieve-analysis results and the target combined grading, if aggregate blending is required.

  6. Review the concrete or road-material design

    For concrete, consider the concrete grade, mix design, paste and water requirements, workability, placement and compaction and thermal considerations in mass concrete. For road materials, consider the approved grading, plasticity and fines requirements, compaction, bearing capacity or CBR requirements where specified and the applicable acceptance tests.

  7. Confirm material quality

    Check particle shape, strength, cleanliness, absorption and other applicable characteristics.

  8. Confirm procurement and logistics

    Establish the quantity, unit, delivery location, dispatch arrangements and documentation requirements.

Practical example: choosing aggregate for a large concrete foundation

Suppose a project requires a large concrete foundation base. The engineer begins by reviewing the foundation dimensions, reinforcement arrangement, concrete volume, whether mass-concrete considerations apply, the required concrete performance and the approved maximum aggregate size.

If the section is large and placement is unrestricted, 40mm may be considered. If the section is heavily reinforced, 20mm may be more suitable. If the project is a mass-concrete element requiring a wider aggregate-size distribution, the engineer may specify multiple aggregate fractions. The final decision must come from the approved design and specification.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About 40mm Crushed Stone Aggregate

1. What is 40mm crushed stone aggregate?

40mm crushed stone aggregate is a large nominal-size coarse aggregate manufactured by crushing and screening stone. It is used in selected mass-concrete, PCC, large foundation and infrastructure applications where the required grading and project specifications permit it.

2. What is 40mm aggregate used for?

40mm aggregate is used in mass concrete, selected PCC, large foundation elements, specified concrete sections, road sub-base and embankment applications. Its suitability depends on grading, member dimensions and the approved project specification.

3. Is 40mm aggregate recognised under IS 383:2016?

Yes. IS 383:2016 recognises 40mm as a nominal size for both single-sized and graded coarse aggregate. The standard also provides separate size classifications for aggregates used in mass concrete.

4. What is the difference between 40mm single-sized and graded aggregate?

Single-sized 40mm aggregate follows a defined single-size grading envelope, while graded 40mm aggregate follows a broader distribution containing specified proportions of smaller particles. Both have a 40mm nominal designation but different sieve requirements.

5. Is 40mm aggregate suitable for mass concrete?

Yes. It can be used as an aggregate fraction in mass-concrete applications where the approved grading and mix design permit it. Certain mass-concrete mixes use multiple aggregate fractions to achieve the required combined grading.

6. What is the difference between 40mm aggregate and medium mass-concrete aggregate?

40mm single-sized or graded coarse aggregate is a nominal aggregate classification under IS 383 Table 7. Medium mass-concrete aggregate is a separate classification ranging from 40mm to 20mm under Table 8. The two should not automatically be treated as identical products.

7. Can 40mm aggregate be used in PCC?

Yes. It may be suitable for selected plain cement concrete sections where the layer thickness, placement conditions and approved mix design permit the size. Thin PCC and blinding layers may require smaller aggregate.

8. Can 40mm aggregate be used in RCC?

It can be used in special reinforced-concrete cases where member dimensions and reinforcement clearances permit it. IRC:112-2020 allows sizes up to 40mm in special cases where there is no restriction to concrete flow. For conventional RCC, 20mm remains the preferred nominal size under that code.

9. Can 40mm aggregate be used in foundations?

Yes. It can be considered for selected large foundations, foundation bases and large concrete sections, subject to section dimensions, reinforcement spacing, concrete cover and approved mix design.

10. Is 40mm aggregate suitable for raft foundations?

It may be considered for certain large raft foundation mixes where the structural and concrete design permits it. The reinforcement arrangement, member thickness, placement method and possible mass-concrete temperature considerations must be evaluated.

11. Why is 40mm aggregate used in large concrete pours?

Larger aggregate can influence the granular structure and paste requirement of a designed concrete mix. In suitable large-section placements, it may contribute to an economical mixture. Its selection must be supported by appropriate grading, mix trials and construction requirements.

12. Does 40mm aggregate reduce cement consumption?

Not automatically. A properly designed larger-aggregate mixture may require less paste than a comparable smaller-aggregate system, but cementitious material requirements depend on grading, concrete performance, water-cement ratio and project conditions. A fixed cement saving should never be assumed.

13. Does 40mm aggregate produce stronger concrete than 20mm?

No. Aggregate size alone does not determine concrete strength. Strength depends on the entire concrete mix, material properties, water-cement ratio, compaction, curing and quality control.

14. Can 40mm aggregate be used in ready-mix concrete?

Yes. It may be used in selected larger-size concrete formulations where the approved RMC mix design calls for it. Its use depends on the placing method, member geometry and required combined grading.

15. What is the difference between 40mm and 20mm aggregate?

40mm is a larger nominal coarse aggregate size, generally relevant to mass concrete, PCC, large foundations and specified infrastructure applications. 20mm is commonly used for conventional RCC slabs, beams, columns and RMC. Selection depends on structural geometry and the concrete specification.

16. What is the difference between 40mm and 60mm aggregate?

Both are larger aggregate sizes that may be relevant to mass concrete and heavy infrastructure. 40mm is a recognised single-sized and graded coarse aggregate category under IS 383, while 60mm is a recognised single-sized category. The correct size depends on the project mix and specification.

17. Can 40mm aggregate be used for road sub-base?

It may be used in specified sub-base or granular material blends where the project grading allows it. However, a standalone 40mm aggregate product is not automatically equivalent to a complete GSB specification.

18. Is 40mm aggregate the same as GSB?

No. 40mm aggregate is an individual nominal-size coarse aggregate fraction. GSB is a complete graded granular sub-base material containing multiple particle sizes and fines within a defined specification.

19. Can 40mm aggregate be used in WMM?

It may be a component of an approved WMM aggregate blend where the specified grading permits it. A standalone 40mm product is not the same as finished Wet Mix Macadam.

20. Can 40mm aggregate be used in drainage?

It may be appropriate for selected drainage applications where the grading, fines content and hydraulic characteristics meet the design requirements. Not every 40mm aggregate stockpile is suitable for an open-graded drainage layer.

21. What tests are important for 40mm aggregate?

Important assessments can include sieve analysis, fines and oversized-particle assessment, particle-shape testing, aggregate crushing value, impact value, abrasion value, specific gravity, water absorption, deleterious-material analysis and soundness testing. The relevant testing programme depends on the intended application.

22. How do I calculate the quantity of 40mm aggregate required?

For a basic geometric volume, multiply length by width by depth, all in metres (V = L × W × D). This calculates the geometric volume, not the final purchase quantity. For concrete, the required aggregate mass must be determined from the approved mix design. For road sub-base or embankment work, the quantity should be calculated using the specified compacted volume and appropriate material density and construction allowances.
