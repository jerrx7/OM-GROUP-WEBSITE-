Its suitability comes from the relationship between particle size, concrete mix design, member dimensions and reinforcement spacing. Understanding the material

What Is 20mm Crushed Stone Aggregate?

20mm crushed stone aggregate is a coarse construction material manufactured by mechanically crushing stone and screening it into a specified nominal-size category.

It is widely used in concrete production, especially in conventional reinforced concrete construction where its nominal size is permitted by the member dimensions, reinforcement spacing and approved concrete mix design.

Under IS 383:2016, 20mm is a recognised nominal size for both single-sized and graded coarse aggregate. The term 20mm describes the aggregate's nominal grading classification. It does not mean that every individual stone particle measures exactly 20mm.

Nominal aggregate size is a grading designation established through sieve analysis. For a 20mm single-sized coarse aggregate, the relevant IS 383:2016 Table 7 grading limits include:

Single-sized 20mm aggregate follows a defined grading envelope in which the material is predominantly within a specified particle-size band. Graded 20mm aggregate follows a broader prescribed particle distribution, including a specified proportion of smaller particles.

This difference has practical implications for batching, blending and concrete mix design. An RMC plant may procure separate nominal aggregate fractions and blend them according to an approved mix design rather than buying one product described simply as "graded concrete aggregate". The required classification must be specified before ordering.

The designation 20mm alone does not establish whether the delivered material is single-sized or graded. The actual sieve-analysis report must be checked.

How Is 20mm Aggregate Manufactured?

The production process generally involves six stages, from source rock to dispatch. The 20mm fraction is the one highlighted below.

  1. Source rock extraction

    Suitable rock is extracted from the quarry and prepared for crushing.

  2. Primary crushing

    Larger stone is reduced into smaller fragments through crushing equipment.

  3. Secondary crushing and shaping

    The material is processed further to achieve the required aggregate fractions and particle characteristics.

  4. Vibrating screening

    The crushed stone passes through screening equipment that separates different particle sizes.

  5. Grading and stockpiling

    The required aggregate fraction is separated and stored to reduce unwanted mixing or contamination.

  6. Transportation and dispatch

    The material is loaded and transported to the receiving construction site or concrete production facility.

Major applications of 20mm crushed stone aggregate

Where Is 20mm Aggregate Used?

20mm aggregate has a broad range of applications in conventional construction and concrete production.

It is particularly relevant to reinforced concrete structures, foundations, ready-mix concrete and selected infrastructure projects. Each application below explains the role of the material and the conditions that decide whether it is suitable.

01

Reinforced cement concrete (RCC) structures

Reinforced cement concrete is a construction system in which concrete works together with steel reinforcement to provide the required structural performance. RCC is widely used in residential, commercial, industrial and infrastructure construction.

20mm crushed stone aggregate is commonly specified as the coarse aggregate in conventional RCC when member dimensions, reinforcement arrangements and placement requirements permit its use.

Why is 20mm aggregate commonly considered for RCC?

Its nominal size is suitable for many conventional structural concrete members. In appropriately designed mixes, it can provide a suitable coarse-aggregate component without requiring the smaller nominal size needed by certain thin or heavily reinforced sections. Its use must still be assessed according to:

  • Member thickness.
  • Minimum clear reinforcement spacing.
  • Concrete cover.
  • Maximum nominal aggregate-size restrictions.
  • Concrete workability.
  • Compaction requirements.
  • Approved mix design.

Potential RCC applications

  • Residential building frames.
  • Commercial building structures.
  • Industrial concrete structures.
  • Reinforced concrete slabs, beams and columns.
  • Footings.
  • Selected retaining and support structures.

When should 20mm be replaced with a smaller size?

Where a concrete member is particularly thin or reinforcement is congested, the available space may restrict the use of larger aggregate particles. Under IS 456:2000, 10mm nominal maximum aggregate should be considered in certain thin sections, closely reinforced members or situations involving small cover. Therefore 20mm should not be recommended for every RCC member simply because it is commonly used in structural concrete.

IS 456:2000

Clause 5.3.3 is the main selection framework. The nominal maximum aggregate size should not exceed one-fourth of the minimum member thickness, and should allow the concrete to surround reinforcement and fill the formwork properly. The code states that 20mm nominal maximum aggregate is suitable for most concrete work, subject to member dimensions and reinforcement arrangements.

Clause 5.3.3.1 adds a restriction for heavily reinforced members: the nominal maximum aggregate size should usually be at least 5mm less than the smaller of the minimum clear distance between main reinforcement bars or the minimum cover to reinforcement.

IS 383:2016 governs aggregate classification and quality. IRC:112-2020 also identifies 20mm as the preferred nominal aggregate size for reinforced and prestressed concrete road bridges, subject to its specified limits.

Application verdictPrimary application. 20mm aggregate is an established coarse aggregate choice for conventional RCC where the structural specification permits it.

02

Reinforced concrete slabs

Concrete slabs are horizontal structural elements used in floors, roofs, foundations and other building components. They transfer loads through the supporting structural system and must satisfy the specified structural and durability requirements. 20mm aggregate is commonly used in conventional reinforced concrete slab construction where slab thickness and reinforcement spacing permit the nominal size.

Why is aggregate size important in slabs?

Concrete has to pass through reinforcement, fill the slab formwork and consolidate uniformly. The nominal maximum aggregate size must be compatible with the slab geometry, and a suitable aggregate grading also influences fresh concrete handling and compaction.

Typical applications

  • Residential, commercial and industrial floor slabs.
  • Roof slabs.
  • Selected foundation and raft slabs.
  • Reinforced concrete decks, subject to the applicable design specification.

What should the engineer verify?

  1. Minimum slab thickness.
  2. Reinforcement spacing.
  3. Concrete cover.
  4. Reinforcement layers and congestion.
  5. Placement method.
  6. Required concrete workability.
  7. Approved aggregate grading.

For very thin slabs or heavily reinforced sections, 10mm aggregate may be more suitable.

Application verdictCommonly considered. 20mm is commonly considered for conventional slab construction, subject to the approved structural design.

03

Reinforced concrete beams and columns

Beams transfer loads from supported structural elements to columns, walls or other supporting systems. Columns transfer structural loads through the building frame. These elements often contain substantial reinforcement, making aggregate selection an important construction consideration.

20mm aggregate is commonly used in conventional beam and column concrete where reinforcement clearance and member geometry permit it. When the reinforcement layout provides adequate clearance, concrete can be placed and compacted around the steel using an appropriate mix and placing method.

Technical considerations

  • Clear distance between reinforcing bars.
  • Concrete cover.
  • Number of reinforcement layers.
  • Member cross-section.
  • Formwork arrangement.
  • Concrete placing and vibration.
  • Concrete grade and mix design.

The smaller cross-sections of some columns and heavily reinforced beams can make 20mm unsuitable. This must be resolved through the structural drawings and approved concrete mix rather than by a general product recommendation.

Application verdictPrimary application. Selected conventional beams and columns where code and project requirements allow 20mm.

04

Building foundations and footings

Foundations transfer loads from the structural system to the supporting soil or rock. Different foundations have different depths, reinforcement arrangements and construction conditions. 20mm aggregate is commonly specified for suitable reinforced foundation concrete and conventional footings.

Typical applications

  • Isolated and combined footings.
  • Strip foundations.
  • Raft foundations.
  • Selected pile caps.
  • Other designed foundation members.

Many conventional foundation members have sufficient dimensions to accommodate 20mm aggregate, provided reinforcement clearance and placement conditions are satisfied. Aggregate selection also influences the workability and compaction requirements of the concrete.

What must be checked?

  • Foundation thickness and reinforcement arrangement.
  • Required concrete cover.
  • Soil and groundwater conditions.
  • Concrete grade, exposure and durability requirements.
  • Formwork and placing conditions.
  • Project specifications.

20mm should not be automatically specified for every foundation. Some heavily reinforced or geometrically restricted members require a different aggregate size.

05 · Ready-mix concrete (RMC) production

Why a Batching Plant Blends Several Aggregate Fractions

Ready-mix concrete is manufactured in a batching facility according to an established mix design and delivered to the construction site in a fresh state. RMC plants use controlled batching and mixing to achieve the intended concrete composition.

20mm aggregate is commonly used as the principal coarse-aggregate fraction in many conventional RMC formulations. It is suitable for many conventional structural concrete applications where the mix and placement requirements permit this nominal maximum size, and it forms an important constituent in combined aggregate grading.

Aggregate blending · actual proportions must follow the approved mix design

The importance of aggregate blending

An RMC plant should not simply combine available aggregate stockpiles in arbitrary quantities. The objective is to achieve the target combined grading and the required concrete performance. IS 10262:2019 provides guidelines for concrete mix proportioning, including the selection of coarse-aggregate volume according to nominal size and fine-aggregate grading. Its values are starting points in a mix-design process, not universal proportions to copy into every concrete batch.

What an RMC plant may stockpile

  • 20mm aggregate.
  • 10mm aggregate.
  • Manufactured sand.
  • Other specified materials.

The proportions are established by concrete mix design.

Factors the plant considers

  • Individual sieve-analysis reports.
  • Target combined aggregate grading.
  • Specific gravity and water absorption.
  • Moisture correction.
  • Concrete grade and workability.
  • Pumping or placing method.
  • Required strength and durability.
  • Trial mix results.
Relevant Indian standards: IS 383:2016 (coarse and fine aggregate), IS 10262:2019 (concrete mix proportioning) and IS 4926:2003 (ready-mixed concrete). BIS identifies IS 4926 as the standard governing the production and supply of ready-mixed concrete.

Concrete pavements and wearing surfaces

Concrete pavements use concrete as the structural or wearing surface of a road, industrial yard or other traffic-bearing area. Unlike a conventional building slab, a pavement must also account for traffic loading, fatigue, abrasion, environmental exposure and pavement design requirements. 20mm aggregate may be specified in certain concrete pavement mixes where it complies with the approved design and material specification.

In concrete wearing surfaces the aggregate is exposed to demanding mechanical conditions, such as repeated loading and surface wear, so its mechanical properties become important. IS 383:2016 includes separate mechanical property limits for coarse aggregate used in concrete wearing surfaces, including roads and pavements: maximum values of 30% for aggregate crushing value (or the ten-percent fines alternative) and 30% for aggregate impact value and Los Angeles abrasion value, subject to the applicable clauses and alternatives. These are standard acceptance provisions.

09

Specified road and infrastructure work

Road and infrastructure projects use different aggregate sizes according to the pavement layer and applicable specification. 20mm crushed stone may form part of specified infrastructure works, but it should not be confused with complete road-material products such as GSB or WMM.

Understanding the difference

  • 20mm crushed aggregate is an individual coarse aggregate fraction.
  • GSB is a specified graded granular sub-base material used in road pavement foundations.
  • WMM (Wet Mix Macadam) is a processed and graded aggregate material used in specified road base and pavement construction.

These materials have separate grading and acceptance requirements. A 20mm fraction can be used in an engineered aggregate blend when the project specification permits it, but it is not a direct substitute for the complete GSB or WMM material.

Bituminous concrete distinction

Bituminous concrete uses a combined grading of coarse aggregate, fine aggregate and filler. MoRTH specifications distinguish nominal bituminous mixture sizes such as 19mm and 13.2mm. A 20mm crushed-stone product must therefore not automatically be advertised as a complete 19mm or 20mm bituminous concrete mixture. The actual material fraction must meet the approved combined grading and job mix formula.

Application verdictSpecification-dependent. Use 20mm aggregate in road projects only when the project grading and specification permit the supplied fraction.

10

Selected drainage applications

Certain drainage systems require clean, low-fines aggregate with an open particle arrangement. The purpose is to provide pathways through which water can move, while the selected system manages soil separation and drainage. 20mm crushed stone may be appropriate where the drainage design specifies a suitable particle-size distribution.

Potential applications

  • French drains and selected soakaway systems.
  • Pipe surrounds and specified drainage trenches.
  • Certain landscape drainage arrangements.

Critical material requirement

Not every material sold as 20mm aggregate is automatically suitable for drainage. The drainage specification should establish the particle-size distribution, fines content, hydraulic requirements, compatibility with the surrounding soil, filter fabric or separation arrangements where required, and the pipe perforation and installation details. A material with excessive fine particles may obstruct drainage pathways, and GSB, WMM and crusher-run materials should not be substituted for an open-graded drainage aggregate simply because they contain crushed stone.

Application verdictConditional. Use only when the actual supplied material satisfies the drainage specification.

11

Selected mass concrete and larger concrete placements

Mass concrete involves placing relatively large volumes of concrete where heat generation, temperature gradients and dimensional changes may need special consideration. Aggregate-size selection is an important part of mass-concrete mix planning. Larger coarse aggregate sizes may be advantageous in certain mass concrete mixes because they can reduce paste requirements, but the allowable size depends on the structure and placement conditions.

IS 383:2016 includes a separate classification of coarse aggregate sizes for mass concrete in Table 8. Its classes include a small category spanning 20mm to 4.75mm and larger categories extending to 150mm. 20mm may therefore form part of an approved mass-concrete grading, but it should not be presented as the universal or preferred size for every mass concrete project.

Application verdictProject-specific. Larger sizes such as 40mm may be considered where the section and specification permit them.

Concrete behaviour

Why Does Aggregate Size Matter in Concrete?

The selection of coarse aggregate is an important part of concrete mix design.

20mm aggregate is commonly used because it can satisfy the material requirements of many conventional concrete applications while remaining compatible with the dimensions and reinforcement arrangements found in standard structural members. However, particle size influences more than the physical dimensions of individual stones. It also affects aggregate grading, surface area, packing, water and paste requirements, concrete placement and compaction.

01Nominal maximum aggregate size

Nominal maximum aggregate size is an important parameter in concrete specification. It establishes the size category used to evaluate the material against the permitted grading and construction requirements.

Under IS 456:2000, the nominal maximum size should be as large as possible within the relevant limitations. The code specifies that it should not exceed one-fourth of the minimum thickness of the member, while still allowing adequate placement around reinforcement and formwork. For heavily reinforced members, additional clearance restrictions apply.

This is one of the main reasons 20mm is commonly specified for conventional concrete but smaller sizes may be required for restricted sections.

02Particle-size distribution

Aggregate grading describes how particles are distributed across different sieve sizes. A suitable grading can help establish an efficient granular structure.

When aggregate grading is inappropriate, the concrete mix may require adjustments to the paste content or proportions to achieve the required workability. This is why sieve analysis is essential.

03Aggregate packing and void content

The particles in a concrete mix occupy space, and the spaces between individual particles are referred to as voids. A suitable combination of aggregate sizes can help particles pack together more efficiently.

The resulting paste requirement depends on the overall particle distribution, particle shape and other mix constituents. 20mm aggregate should therefore be evaluated alongside the fine-aggregate grading and any other coarse fractions in the mix.

04Particle shape

Crushed stone can contain angular, flaky, elongated or more cubical particles. Particle shape can affect packing characteristics, internal friction, fresh concrete workability, compaction and paste requirements.

A nominal 20mm designation does not establish particle shape. The actual material must be evaluated using the relevant particle-shape tests.

05Surface texture

The surface texture of coarse aggregate influences the interaction between the particle and cement paste. Rough and smooth surfaces may produce different interfacial characteristics.

However, surface texture alone cannot be used to establish concrete strength. The complete material combination and concrete mix design remain important.

06Workability and placement

Workability describes how easily fresh concrete can be mixed, transported, placed and compacted. Aggregate grading and particle shape influence the fresh concrete's behaviour.

A 20mm aggregate may be suitable for a conventional member with adequate reinforcement clearance, while the same size may be unsuitable for a thin, congested section. The required workability should be achieved through an approved mix design rather than by adding uncontrolled quantities of water.

07Reinforcement clearance

In reinforced concrete, the aggregate must be able to move through the available space and surround reinforcement properly. If the particle size is too large for the available clearances, proper placement and compaction may become difficult.

This is why concrete member geometry is central to aggregate selection.

08Water and cement-paste requirements

Larger aggregate generally has less total surface area per unit volume than a comparable smaller-particle aggregate system. This can influence how much cement paste is needed to coat and fill the granular structure.

However, it does not establish a guaranteed cement saving. The actual paste and water requirements depend on the complete grading, particle shape, workability target and designed concrete proportions.

09Segregation and honeycombing

Segregation occurs when concrete constituents separate during transport, placing or handling. Honeycombing refers to voids and poorly consolidated regions in hardened concrete. Potential contributing factors include:

  • Unsuitable grading.
  • Poor placement technique.
  • Inadequate compaction.
  • Congested reinforcement.
  • Inappropriate workability.
  • Poor formwork arrangements.

Using 20mm aggregate does not automatically prevent these defects.

10Durability and concrete performance

The durability of concrete depends on the entire material and construction system: aggregate properties, cementitious material selection, water-cement ratio, concrete cover, compaction, curing, environmental exposure and quality control.

The aggregate size alone cannot establish resistance to weathering, corrosion or structural deterioration.

Engineering principle

The purpose of selecting 20mm aggregate is to satisfy the concrete design and project specification. A suitable size must also allow the concrete to be placed, compacted and cured properly.

When should 10mm aggregate be used?

10mm may be considered for thin concrete sections, closely spaced reinforcement and situations where the structural design calls for a smaller nominal maximum aggregate size. IS 456 specifically advises consideration of 10mm in such circumstances.

When should 20mm aggregate be used?

20mm is commonly suitable for conventional reinforced concrete members and many standard concrete production applications where member dimensions, reinforcement spacing and project specifications permit it.

When should 40mm aggregate be used?

40mm can be considered in selected mass concrete, PCC and larger-section applications when the member geometry and approved specification allow the larger size. It requires adequate space for placement and consolidation.

Why might 20mm be economical?

In otherwise comparable concrete systems, a larger aggregate fraction can have lower surface area per unit volume than a smaller one. This can reduce the paste requirement in an appropriately designed mixture.

However, the actual economics depend on aggregate grading and quality, cementitious materials, water-cement ratio, mix design, concrete placement, and material and logistics costs. A 40mm aggregate should never replace 20mm merely to reduce the material cost if the structural design does not permit it. Equally, 20mm is not automatically stronger than 10mm.

How to Evaluate the Quality of 20mm Crushed Stone Aggregate

A 20mm nominal designation does not guarantee that an aggregate meets every project requirement.

The material needs to be assessed against the relevant grading, physical, chemical and mechanical properties. This is particularly important for structural concrete, public infrastructure and RMC production.

The laboratory stations

No laboratory findings, certificates or accreditation are shown or implied.

01Sieve analysis and grading

Sieve analysis determines the distribution of aggregate particles across specified sieve sizes. For 20mm coarse aggregate, it helps establish whether the material complies with the required single-sized or graded classification.

The report should contain:

  • Sample identification and collection date.
  • Individual sieve designations.
  • Percentage retained and percentage passing.
  • Grading acceptance limits.
  • Testing method and laboratory details.

02Single-sized and graded classification

The buyer should explicitly confirm whether the project requires single-sized or graded 20mm aggregate. The two classifications have different limits under IS 383:2016. For example, the amount passing the 10mm sieve is permitted to be higher in the graded 20mm classification than in the single-sized classification, which can affect the combined grading used in concrete production.

03Particle shape and flakiness

Particle shape influences packing, friction and fresh concrete behaviour. IS 383:2016 addresses the combined flakiness and elongation index. The standard's stated limit for the combined index is 40% for crushed or uncrushed aggregate, subject to the applicable clause and permitted engineering considerations.

04Aggregate crushing value

Aggregate crushing value assesses resistance to crushing under a specified laboratory procedure. For concrete wearing surfaces, IS 383:2016 specifies an aggregate crushing value limit of 30% maximum or the applicable ten-percent fines alternative. For other concrete applications, the standard includes an alternative ten-percent fines requirement when the crushing value exceeds 30%.

05Aggregate impact value

Aggregate impact value assesses resistance to impact. IS 383:2016 specifies:

  • 30% maximum for aggregate used in concrete wearing surfaces.
  • 45% maximum for other concrete applications.
  • 22% maximum for concrete of M65 grade and above, under the stated requirement.

These limits must be read with the applicable provisions and alternatives.

06Los Angeles abrasion value

Abrasion testing evaluates resistance to wear under the prescribed procedure. IS 383 specifies maximum values of 30% for aggregate used in concrete wearing surfaces and 50% for aggregate used in concrete other than wearing surfaces. The relevance of the test and acceptance limit depends on the application and applicable specification.

07Specific gravity and density

Specific gravity is used in concrete mix calculations and material characterisation. Density and bulk density can help estimate the quantity of material associated with a given volume, but actual delivered weight depends on moisture, grading, packing and source characteristics. A nominal 20mm designation alone does not establish an exact tonnes-per-cubic-metre conversion.

08Water absorption and moisture

Water absorption affects the amount of water that an aggregate can take up under specified conditions. RMC plants need to consider both absorption and actual stockpile moisture when correcting batching water. Failure to account for moisture conditions can change the effective water-cement ratio of the concrete mix.

09Deleterious materials and cleanliness

Aggregates must be evaluated for unwanted constituents that can affect concrete quality. Relevant checks may include:

  • Clay lumps.
  • Coal and lignite.
  • Soft fragments.
  • Dust and excessive fines.
  • Organic impurities.
  • Other harmful materials identified by the specification.

IS 383 addresses limits for deleterious constituents and their testing methods.

10Soundness and source characteristics

Soundness testing evaluates resistance to deterioration under specified accelerated test conditions. Source-rock properties and petrographic characteristics may also be relevant depending on the application.

Selection guide

How to Select the Right Aggregate Size for Concrete Construction

Selecting coarse aggregate should begin with the engineering requirement, not simply the availability of a material.

The appropriate size depends on member geometry, reinforcement arrangement, grading, concrete mix design and the project specification.

  1. Identify the concrete application

    Determine whether the material is required for RCC slabs, beams and columns, foundations and footings, RMC production, precast manufacturing, PCC, concrete pavements or other specified concrete work.

  2. Review the structural drawings

    For reinforced concrete, check the minimum member thickness, the clear distance between reinforcement bars, concrete cover, the number of reinforcement layers, placement access and formwork dimensions.

  3. Confirm the maximum nominal aggregate size

    Review the applicable restrictions under IS 456:2000 and the project's structural specifications. Do not assume that 20mm is appropriate for every member simply because it is common in RCC construction.

  4. Confirm the aggregate classification

    Establish whether the specification requires 20mm single-sized coarse aggregate, 20mm graded coarse aggregate, a specified combined aggregate grading or another project-specific grading.

  5. Review the complete concrete mix design

    Confirm the concrete grade, fine-aggregate grading, coarse-aggregate proportions, water-cement ratio, workability requirements, approved admixtures if applicable and the required mix trials.

  6. Evaluate material quality

    Check grading, particle shape, water absorption, specific gravity and any applicable mechanical-property requirements.

  7. Confirm placement and compaction

    The concrete must be capable of moving into the required sections, surrounding reinforcement and consolidating properly.

  8. Establish procurement and logistics

    Confirm the required quantity, unit, delivery location, delivery frequency and documentation.

Practical decision example

Suppose a contractor is preparing concrete for a conventional RCC building. The structural engineer first examines the beam and slab dimensions and checks the reinforcement layout.

Where 20mm satisfies the maximum aggregate-size restrictions and concrete can be placed and compacted correctly, it may be selected as the coarse aggregate. If a thinner member or congested reinforcement restricts the available space, 10mm may be more suitable. For larger unreinforced concrete sections, 40mm may be considered if permitted by the applicable project specification.

The important point is that the engineering requirement determines the product selection.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About 20mm Crushed Stone Aggregate

1. What is 20mm crushed stone aggregate?

20mm crushed stone aggregate is a nominal-size coarse aggregate manufactured through crushing and screening stone. It is commonly used in conventional reinforced concrete, foundations, ready-mix concrete and other specified construction applications.

2. What is 20mm aggregate used for?

20mm aggregate is commonly used for RCC slabs, beams, columns, footings, foundations, RMC production and selected precast and infrastructure applications. Its suitability depends on the structural design, grading and concrete mix requirements.

3. Why is 20mm aggregate commonly used in RCC?

20mm is a recognised coarse aggregate size that is suitable for many conventional reinforced concrete members where adequate reinforcement spacing, cover and member dimensions permit its use. IS 456:2000 identifies 20mm as suitable for most concrete work, subject to the specified restrictions.

4. Can 20mm aggregate be used for concrete slabs?

Yes. 20mm aggregate is commonly used for conventional RCC slabs when slab thickness, reinforcement spacing and concrete placement conditions permit it. Thin or congested sections may require a smaller nominal aggregate size.

5. Is 20mm aggregate suitable for beams and columns?

Yes. It is commonly used in conventional reinforced concrete beams and columns where the member dimensions, reinforcement clearances and approved mix design allow 20mm aggregate.

6. Can 20mm aggregate be used in foundations?

Yes. It is commonly specified for selected reinforced concrete foundations, isolated footings and raft foundations where the member dimensions and reinforcement arrangements permit the nominal size.

7. Is 20mm aggregate suitable for ready-mix concrete?

Yes. It is commonly used as a coarse aggregate in ready-mix concrete production. RMC plants may also combine 20mm with 10mm or other specified aggregate fractions to achieve a designed combined grading.

8. What is the difference between 20mm single-sized and graded aggregate?

Single-sized 20mm aggregate follows a defined single-size grading envelope under IS 383:2016. Graded 20mm aggregate has a different, broader particle-size distribution. The percentage of smaller material permitted within each classification is different.

9. Does 20mm mean every stone particle is exactly 20mm?

No. 20mm is a nominal aggregate-size designation based on sieve grading. Individual particles can have different dimensions within the specified grading limits.

10. What is the difference between 10mm and 20mm aggregate?

10mm is a smaller nominal coarse aggregate size, while 20mm is larger. 10mm may be considered for thin sections and congested reinforcement. 20mm is commonly suitable for conventional reinforced concrete where the structural requirements permit it.

11. What is the difference between 20mm and 40mm aggregate?

20mm is commonly used for conventional RCC and RMC, while 40mm is considered for selected larger concrete sections, mass concrete and other specified applications. Larger aggregate requires sufficient section dimensions and clearance for placement and compaction.

12. Can 20mm aggregate be used in precast concrete?

Yes. It may be suitable for larger precast components where mould dimensions, reinforcement and the approved concrete mix allow the nominal size. Smaller components may require finer aggregate fractions.

13. Can 20mm aggregate be used in concrete roads?

It may be used in specified concrete pavement mixes where the pavement design and aggregate requirements permit it. Mechanical properties and grading must satisfy the relevant requirements for concrete wearing surfaces.

14. Can 20mm aggregate be used in bituminous road construction?

It may be considered as a material fraction in an approved combined grading where the specification permits it. It is not automatically equivalent to a complete 19mm nominal bituminous concrete mixture. The approved grading and job mix formula must be followed.

15. Can 20mm aggregate be used for GSB and WMM?

A standalone 20mm aggregate product is not a substitute for complete GSB or WMM material. These road materials have their own grading and quality requirements. Individual aggregate fractions may contribute to a specified blend where permitted.

16. Is 20mm aggregate suitable for drainage?

It can be used in specified drainage systems when the actual material is clean, low in fines and meets the required open grading. Not every 20mm aggregate stockpile automatically meets drainage requirements.

17. Does 20mm aggregate produce stronger concrete than 10mm?

No. Aggregate size alone does not determine concrete strength. Mix design, water-cement ratio, aggregate properties, compaction, curing and quality control all affect the final concrete performance.

18. Why do RMC plants combine 10mm and 20mm aggregate?

RMC plants may combine different coarse aggregate fractions to achieve the target particle-size distribution required by a particular mix design. The proportions are established through sieve-analysis data and concrete mix trials, not a universal fixed ratio.

19. What tests are important for 20mm aggregate?

Important tests include sieve analysis, combined flakiness and elongation index, aggregate crushing value, impact value, abrasion value, specific gravity, water absorption and assessment of deleterious materials. The required testing programme depends on the application and project specification.

20. How do I calculate the quantity of 20mm aggregate required?

For an initial volume calculation, volume = length × width × depth, where the length, width and depth are in metres.

For concrete production, the actual quantity of 20mm aggregate must be calculated from the approved concrete mix proportions and total concrete volume. If using multiple aggregate fractions, the designed proportion of each fraction must be considered.
