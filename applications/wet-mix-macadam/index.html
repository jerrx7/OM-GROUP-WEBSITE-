Wet Mix Macadam Applications for Highways, Roads & Infrastructure Construction

The graded aggregate foundation behind a well-engineered road. Wet Mix Macadam is a pavement-construction material produced by blending clean, crushed and graded aggregate fractions with controlled water, followed by spreading and compaction to form a dense road layer.

The final WMM blend is not a single aggregate size. It is a specified combination of coarse and fine fractions designed to achieve the required grading and compaction characteristics.

    Wet Mix Macadam in Fourteen Short Answers

    The questions engineers, contractors and buyers ask first, answered plainly. The sections below explain each one in full.

    What is WMM?

    Wet Mix Macadam is a road-construction material made by mixing clean, crushed and graded aggregate with controlled water and compacting the mixture into a dense granular pavement layer.

    What does WMM stand for?

    WMM stands for Wet Mix Macadam.

    What is WMM used for?

    WMM is used in specified road and infrastructure pavement construction, including highways, rural roads, urban roads, industrial roads and development access roads.

    Which standard governs WMM?

    MoRTH Clause 406 in the applicable revision is the principal specification reference. IRC:109-2015 provides additional design and construction guidance.

    Is WMM a single aggregate size?

    No. WMM is a graded blend of multiple aggregate fractions, combined to meet a specified grading envelope.

    Which aggregate fractions are used for WMM?

    WMM blends may use 40mm, 20mm, 10mm, 6mm and screenings, depending on the required grading and available material.

    What is the difference between WMM aggregate and finished WMM?

    WMM aggregate consists of the individual crushed fractions. Finished WMM is the combined aggregate, proportioned, moisture-conditioned and mechanically mixed for laying and compaction.

    What is OMC in WMM?

    Optimum Moisture Content is the moisture content at which a WMM material achieves its maximum dry density under the specified laboratory compaction method.

    What is the role of a pugmill?

    A pugmill is forced mechanical mixing equipment used to combine graded aggregates and controlled water into a uniform WMM mix.

    Is WMM the same as GSB?

    No. GSB is the granular sub-base, generally governed by MoRTH Clause 401. WMM is generally used as the pavement base course above it where specified and is governed by Clause 406.

    Is crusher-run the same as WMM?

    No. Crusher-run is an aggregate output. WMM requires the appropriate grading, aggregate quality and moisture-controlled mixing.

    What is the difference between WMM and WBM?

    WMM uses mechanically mixed aggregate and controlled moisture. WBM is a separate, older water-bound macadam construction method.

    What is Wet Mix Macadam

    A mechanically mixed, moisture-conditioned aggregate layer.

    Wet Mix Macadam is a granular pavement material created by combining clean, crushed and graded aggregate with controlled water.

    The material is proportioned to meet the required grading envelope.

    It is then mixed, transported, spread and compacted into a dense pavement layer.

    Why is WMM called Wet Mix Macadam?

    "Macadam" refers to a road-construction approach based on graded aggregate layers.

    "Wet Mix" describes the controlled addition of water during the mixing stage.

    The water helps the aggregate blend achieve the moisture condition required for compaction.

    WMM is a mechanically mixed granular material rather than a bitumen-bound or cement-bound pavement layer in its conventional form.

    What makes WMM different?

    The finished WMM layer is formed through:

    • Controlled aggregate grading.
    • Mechanical mixing.
    • Moisture conditioning.
    • Uniform spreading.
    • Compaction.
    • Quality control.

    Is WMM one aggregate size?

    WMM is not simply 40mm aggregate or 20mm aggregate.

    It is a graded blend of multiple particle sizes.

    The final material is designed to meet the grading envelope required by the applicable specification.

        WMM grading and MoRTH requirements

        The Grading Envelope Defines the Combined Aggregate Distribution.

        WMM grading is established through sieve analysis. The final blend must contain an appropriate distribution of larger, intermediate and finer particles, and the combined material must conform to the governing specification.

        Standards update

        MoRTH Sixth Revision · October 2026

        MoRTH Circular No. 407.29, dated 29 October 2026, makes the Sixth Revision of its Specifications for Road and Bridge Works effective for National Highway works from 1 January 2027. Until then, a National Highway tender may still refer to the Fifth Revision, its amendments or project-specific requirements. State PWD, municipal, rural-road, industrial, EPC and other contracts follow their own governing documents.

        WMM material requirements must be confirmed against the MoRTH revision, IRC guidance and project-specific contract applicable to the work. For National Highway tenders governed by MoRTH's Sixth Revision from 1 January 2027, the Sixth Revision and associated contract requirements take precedence.

        No Sixth Revision grading table is shown on this page. The WMM clause, table and acceptance provisions of the published Sixth Revision have to be verified before any replacement table is used, and a Fifth Revision table is not to be reused for a tender the Sixth Revision governs.

        • Moisture sensitivity.
        • Compaction behaviour.
        • Volume stability.

        FAQ

        Wet Mix Macadam Applications: Frequently Asked Questions

        1. What is Wet Mix Macadam?

        Wet Mix Macadam is a pavement material made from clean, crushed and graded aggregate blended with controlled water and compacted into a dense granular road layer.

        2. What does WMM stand for?

        WMM stands for Wet Mix Macadam.

        3. What is WMM used for?

        WMM is used in specified road and infrastructure pavement construction, including highway, rural-road, industrial-road and other approved pavement base or sub-base applications.

        4. Which Indian specification applies to WMM?

        MoRTH Clause 406 in Section 400 is the principal specification reference under the applicable MoRTH revision. IRC:109-2015 provides separate WMM design and construction guidance.

        5. What is MoRTH Clause 406?

        MoRTH Clause 406 governs WMM material requirements and construction operations, including aggregate grading, mixing, spreading and compaction.

        6. What is IRC:109-2015?

        IRC:109-2015 is the Indian Roads Congress guideline for Wet Mix Macadam. It provides guidance on materials, grading, construction and testing.

        7. What is the difference between WMM aggregate and finished WMM?

        WMM aggregate refers to the crushed fractions used to produce WMM. Finished WMM is the combined, proportioned and moisture-conditioned material ready for laying and compaction.

        8. Is WMM one aggregate size?

        No. WMM is a combination of multiple aggregate fractions proportioned to the specified grading envelope.

        9. Is 40mm aggregate the same as WMM?

        No. 40mm aggregate is one coarse fraction that may be used in the WMM blend. It is not finished WMM.

        10. Which aggregate fractions are used in WMM?

        The blend may use 40mm, 20mm, 10mm, 6mm and screenings, depending on the governing grading and available material characteristics.

        11. What is the WMM grading envelope?

        It is the permitted distribution of particle sizes across specified IS sieve openings. The applicable table depends on the governing MoRTH revision and project specification.

        12. Is WMM always a 0–53mm grading?

        A 0–53mm grading description is commonly used for conventional WMM. The actual sieve requirements must be confirmed against the applicable specification and contract.

        13. What is the difference between MoRTH Clause 406 and IRC:109?

        MoRTH Clause 406 provides specification requirements for WMM work. IRC:109-2015 provides separate technical guidelines for WMM design and construction. Their published tables and provisions should not be combined without confirming the governing contract.

        14. What is the latest MoRTH revision for WMM?

        MoRTH issued the Sixth Revision of its Specifications for Road and Bridge Works in October 2026. It becomes effective for National Highway works from 1 January 2027. The applicable revision for a particular project must be confirmed against its contract.

        15. What is the 75-micron sieve used for in WMM?

        The 75-micron sieve measures the very fine portion of the aggregate grading. Its permissible percentage depends on the governing WMM specification.

        16. What is the significance of the 600-micron sieve?

        The 600-micron sieve is an important intermediate grading point. It helps establish the distribution of fine aggregate particles within the WMM blend.

        17. What is the Plasticity Index requirement for WMM?

        The established MoRTH Fifth Revision Clause 406 and IRC:109-2015 provisions specify a PI not exceeding 6 for the material finer than 425 microns. The applicable current project requirement must be checked.

        18. What is Aggregate Impact Value?

        Aggregate Impact Value assesses resistance to impact under the specified test method. The governing WMM aggregate quality provisions specify the applicable maximum value.

        19. What is Los Angeles Abrasion Value?

        It measures aggregate resistance to abrasion and degradation in the prescribed testing procedure. The established MoRTH Fifth Revision / IRC:109-2015 WMM provision specifies a maximum of 40 percent.

        20. What is the combined flakiness and elongation requirement?

        The established MoRTH Fifth Revision / IRC:109-2015 provision specifies a combined maximum of 35 percent. Confirm the applicable requirement for the project.

        21. What is Optimum Moisture Content in WMM?

        OMC is the moisture content at which the aggregate material reaches its maximum dry density under the specified laboratory compaction procedure.

        22. What is Maximum Dry Density?

        MDD is the maximum dry density established by the specified laboratory compaction test. Field density is compared with the reference value according to the project requirement.

        23. Why is moisture control important?

        Moisture close to OMC helps achieve the intended compaction. Material that is excessively wet or dry may not reach the required density.

        24. What is a WMM pugmill?

        A pugmill is forced mechanical mixing equipment used to combine the graded aggregate and controlled water into a uniform WMM mixture.

        25. Can a concrete pan mixer be used for WMM?

        The conventional Clause 406 specification framework allows suitable forced-mixing arrangements such as a pugmill or pan-type mixer. The approved plant and equipment must satisfy the governing specification.

        26. What is the purpose of screening in a WMM plant?

        Screening helps remove oversize material and control the particle-size distribution of the aggregate blend.

        27. How is WMM spread on the road?

        The material is transported to the prepared surface and spread uniformly using an appropriate paver finisher or other approved equipment.

        28. What is the role of a motor grader?

        A motor grader can be used to spread and shape WMM in situations where the governing specification and Engineer permit it.

        29. How is WMM compacted?

        It is compacted across the full specified depth using suitable rollers in an approved sequence until the project density and surface requirements are achieved.

        30. Why is lateral confinement required?

        Lateral confinement helps support the edges of the wet mix during spreading and compaction and helps maintain the required layer geometry and stability.

        31. What is the minimum WMM layer thickness?

        Under the MoRTH Fifth Revision Clause 406, a single compacted layer should not be less than 75mm. Other maximum thickness provisions vary by document and application. The current tender and approved design must govern.

        32. Can WMM be placed in a 200mm layer?

        The MoRTH Fifth Revision contains a conditional allowance for a compacted depth up to 200mm in the specified sub-base circumstance using approved compacting equipment. Do not assume that this applies to every WMM base course.

        33. What is the IRC:109-2015 layer-thickness guidance?

        IRC:109-2015 describes an individual layer between 75mm and 250mm. This must be reconciled with the applicable contract and MoRTH provisions before construction.

        34. What density is required for WMM?

        The established MoRTH Fifth Revision Clause 406 text and IRC:109-2015 use different compaction criteria. The contract's governing density requirement must be followed rather than assuming a universal percentage.

        35. How long should WMM dry after compaction?

        The established MoRTH Fifth Revision and IRC:109-2015 documents refer to 24 hours, but the purpose and subsequent operation differ slightly. Follow the applicable project specification.

        36. Is WMM the same as GSB?

        No. GSB is Granular Sub-Base, generally governed by MoRTH Clause 401. WMM is governed by Clause 406 and is commonly used as a base course above GSB.

        37. Is WMM the same as WBM?

        No. WBM is Water Bound Macadam. WMM uses mechanically mixed graded aggregate and controlled moisture, following a different construction method.

        38. Is WMM the same as crusher-run?

        No. Crusher-run is a mixed-size aggregate output. WMM must meet the specified grading and moisture-conditioned production requirements.

        39. Can crusher-run replace WMM?

        Not automatically. The material must meet the applicable WMM grading, aggregate-quality and moisture requirements, and be approved for the specified application.

        40. Is WMM the same as DBM?

        No. WMM is a granular aggregate layer. DBM is a bituminous pavement layer containing a bituminous binder.

        41. Is WMM the same as DLC?

        No. DLC is Dry Lean Concrete, a cement-bound pavement material. WMM is a granular aggregate material.

        42. Where is WMM used?

        WMM may be used in specified national highways, state highways, rural roads, urban roads, industrial roads, access roads and other civil infrastructure projects.

        43. Can WMM be used for industrial roads?

        Yes, where the pavement design and contract specify WMM for the required base or sub-base layer.

        44. Can WMM be used for internal development roads?

        Yes, subject to the pavement design, applicable grading and approved project specification.

        45. Does OM GROUP manufacture WMM?

        OM GROUP manufactures and supplies the crushed aggregate fractions used for WMM production.

        46. Which WMM fractions does OM GROUP supply?

        OM GROUP supplies 40mm, 20mm, 10mm, 6mm and screenings for WMM-related aggregate requirements, subject to current availability and the project specification.

        47. Can OM GROUP supply WMM aggregate in bulk?

        Yes. Bulk aggregate requirements can be discussed based on the quantity, required grading, destination and delivery schedule.

        48. Does OM GROUP supply WMM aggregate across Goa?

        Yes. OM GROUP supplies WMM aggregate fractions across North Goa and South Goa.

        49. Can OM GROUP arrange delivery to a wet-mix plant?

        Aggregate dispatch to a project wet-mix plant can be coordinated, subject to the confirmed destination, quantity and transport arrangements.

        50. What documents can be requested?

        Grading and current aggregate test information can be requested where available for the specific material and project. Not every parameter is tested for every shipment.

        51. How is WMM quantity measured?

        Finished WMM is generally measured as compacted work in position in cubic metres under the established MoRTH Fifth Revision payment provision. OM GROUP aggregate fractions are quoted according to the agreed commercial measurement unit and order terms.

        52. What affects WMM aggregate pricing in Goa?

        Price depends on aggregate fractions, grading requirement, quantity, delivery location, transport distance, documentation requirements, current availability and project schedule.

        53. Can I order GSB and WMM aggregate together?

        Yes. OM GROUP can discuss combined project requirements for GSB material and WMM aggregate fractions. Their specifications and commercial quantities must remain separate.
