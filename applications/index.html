FAQ

Questions about construction material applications

Which construction material is used for RCC?

RCC is commonly made with cement, coarse aggregate, sand, water and steel reinforcement. OM GROUP supplies the aggregate, sand and ready-mix concrete. Aggregate size, sand type and concrete grade are set by the structural design and approved mix, so confirm them against your drawings before ordering.

For conventional members, 20mm aggregate is commonly considered; for congested reinforcement, 10mm aggregate. Fine aggregate is typically M-Sand or Concrete Sand. Ready-mix concrete is delivered to site by transit mixer.

Which aggregate is used for slabs and beams?

20mm crushed stone aggregate is commonly the coarse aggregate for conventional RCC slabs and beams. 10mm is commonly considered where reinforcement is closely spaced or sections are thin. The right size depends on member dimensions, reinforcement spacing and the approved mix design.

If the drawings or mix design name an aggregate size, use that size. Do not substitute one size for another without checking with the project engineer.

What is 10mm aggregate used for?

10mm aggregate is crushed stone of 10mm nominal size, used in RCC with closely spaced reinforcement, thin sections, precast elements, and paver and block manufacture. It is not automatically a replacement for 20mm aggregate; member dimensions and the mix design decide which size is used.

See 10mm aggregate specifications and FAQs.

What is 20mm aggregate used for?

20mm aggregate is typically the default coarse aggregate for RCC slabs, beams, columns, footings and ready-mix concrete. It suits conventional members where reinforcement spacing allows it. Final size depends on the structural drawings and mix design.

See 20mm aggregate specifications and FAQs.

What is the difference between GSB and WMM?

GSB (Granular Sub-Base) is a graded granular material forming the sub-base layer beneath a road's base course. WMM (Wet Mix Macadam) is a separate layer of crushed, graded aggregate. They are different layers with different specifications, commonly MoRTH Clauses 401 and 406, and are not interchangeable.

Which layer a project needs is set by the pavement design and the contract specification. See GSB, WMM and the road base material guide.

Which sand is used for plastering?

Plaster Sand, a finer manufactured sand graded for plaster mortar, is the material intended for plastering. OM GROUP's Plaster Sand is supplied with reference to IS 1542:1992. Other sands should be used only where the project specification allows. M-Sand, river sand and stone dust are not automatically interchangeable with it.

See Plaster Sand for wall and ceiling plastering. Grading and documentation are available on request.

Is M-Sand suitable for concrete?

M-Sand is manufactured sand made by crushing hard rock and screening it to sand size, graded for concrete, RCC and masonry. Suitability for a given mix depends on its grading, the applicable IS 383:2016 requirements and the approved mix design, so confirm current grading before use.

OM GROUP also lists Concrete Sand, which is manufactured M-Sand confirmed against IS 383:2016 and the project mix specification.

Which material is used for paver blocks?

Paver blocks are commonly made with 6mm or 10mm crushed stone aggregate and manufactured sand, along with cement. The exact grading depends on the block design, mould and manufacturing process. OM GROUP supplies the aggregates and sand, and current grading information can be discussed with the team.

See 6mm aggregate and 10mm aggregate.

What information is needed to order construction materials?

Send the material, quantity and unit, application, project location, required delivery date and any specification or grade. For ready-mix, add pour volume, grade and workability. Several materials can be quoted in one enquiry. Pricing depends on material, quantity, location and delivery schedule.

Units can be cubic metres, tonnes, brass or trips. A BOQ or specification extract speeds up the confirmation.
