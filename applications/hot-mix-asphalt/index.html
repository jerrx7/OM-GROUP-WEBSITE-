Hot Mix Asphalt for Road Construction & Infrastructure

Engineered bituminous mixtures for specified pavement construction. Hot Mix Asphalt is a carefully proportioned mixture of crushed aggregates, mineral filler and bituminous binder, produced through a controlled heating and mixing process.

Used in specified road pavement layers, Hot Mix is transported to the construction site, laid with suitable paving equipment and compacted to achieve the required pavement profile and density.

From aggregate selection and binder proportions to plant production, temperature control and field compaction, every stage contributes to the performance of the finished road. Explore how Hot Mix Asphalt is manufactured, tested and used in modern road construction.

Quick answer

What is Hot Mix Asphalt?

What is Hot Mix Asphalt?

Hot Mix Asphalt is a bituminous pavement material manufactured by combining selected mineral aggregates, mineral filler and bituminous binder in controlled proportions. The ingredients are heated and mixed in an asphalt plant to produce a uniform mixture that can be transported, laid and compacted according to the approved road construction specification.

Think of it as engineered stone and bitumen

Stone forms the mineral structure. Fine aggregate fills the spaces between larger particles. Mineral filler contributes the finest fraction of the aggregate-binder system. Bitumen binds and coats the mineral particles. Controlled mixing makes the material consistent, and compaction on the road creates the pavement structure.

The proportions come from the approved mix design, not from visual estimation.

Bitumen is not asphalt

When a contractor orders Hot Mix, they are not ordering pure bitumen. They are ordering a specified mixture with an approved aggregate grading, binder type, binder content, mineral filler content, mix design, and production and construction requirements.

Direct answers

Hot Mix Asphalt in Sixteen Short Answers

The questions engineers, contractors and buyers ask first, answered plainly. The sections below explain each one in full.

What is Hot Mix Asphalt?

A bituminous pavement mixture produced by combining selected mineral aggregates, mineral filler and bituminous binder in controlled proportions. The materials are heated and mixed before being transported, laid and compacted to form a pavement layer.

What are the main ingredients of Hot Mix?

Coarse aggregate, fine aggregate, mineral filler and bituminous binder. Additives or modifiers may also be used when the approved mix design specifies them.

What is DBM?

Dense Bituminous Macadam is a dense graded bituminous mixture commonly used as a binder or base course in flexible pavement construction.

What is BC?

Bituminous Concrete is a dense graded bituminous mixture commonly used as the wearing course in a flexible pavement.

What is the difference between DBM and BC?

DBM commonly forms a structural bituminous layer beneath the wearing course, while BC commonly forms the upper pavement surface. Their actual specifications depend on the project design.

How is Hot Mix Asphalt manufactured?

By proportioning aggregate fractions, drying and heating the aggregates, adding specified binder and filler, mixing the materials, inspecting the finished mixture and discharging it for transport.

What is a Job Mix Formula?

The approved mix design that defines the aggregate grading, binder type, binder content and other specified mixture parameters.

What is Marshall Mix Design?

A method used to evaluate and select bituminous mixture proportions through prepared specimens and measurements such as stability, flow and volumetric properties.

What is the difference between batch and drum-mix plants?

A batch plant produces individual measured batches using separate weighing and mixing stages. A drum-mix plant produces the mixture continuously through an integrated production process.

Why is temperature important in Hot Mix?

Temperature influences binder viscosity, mixing, workability, transportation and compaction. The required operating range depends on the binder, mix design and project specification.

What is prime coat?

A specified bituminous treatment applied to a suitable prepared granular surface before the subsequent pavement layer.

What is tack coat?

A specified bituminous application used to promote bonding between pavement layers.

Why is compaction important?

Compaction helps achieve the required pavement density, aggregate contact, volumetric structure and surface profile.

How is Hot Mix quantity calculated?

For a uniform rectangular road section, theoretical compacted volume is length times width times compacted thickness. Multiplying the volume by the approved compacted density gives the theoretical mass.

What tests are performed on Hot Mix?

Depending on the mix and project: aggregate gradation, binder content, bulk specific gravity, maximum theoretical specific gravity, air voids, VMA, VFB, Marshall properties, density and core testing.

Does OM GROUP supply Hot Mix in Goa?

This page does not state that OM GROUP manufactures or supplies Hot Mix Asphalt. OM GROUP's catalogue covers crushed aggregates, GSB, WMM, boulders and other construction materials. Any Hot Mix supply is confirmed only after the plant, mix types, binder grade, documentation and delivery coverage for a given requirement are verified.

Understand the product

More than crushed stone and bitumen.

Hot Mix Asphalt is an engineered combination of materials. The aggregate structure, binder and filler work together to produce the required pavement properties.

A mix with too much or too little binder can behave differently from one that meets the approved design. A mixture with unsuitable grading, or one that is poorly compacted, can deteriorate early. That is why Hot Mix production follows a controlled process.

Every ingredient is selected and proportioned according to the approved mix design. No percentage is shown here.

Ingredient 01

Coarse aggregate: the mineral skeleton

Coarse aggregate forms a major part of the mineral structure. It is generally produced by crushing suitable rock and processing it into the required size fractions.

What matters

  • Particle size and gradation.
  • Angularity, shape and surface texture.
  • Strength, abrasion and impact resistance.
  • Water absorption, durability and cleanliness.

What it contributes

  • Load distribution and aggregate interlock.
  • Resistance to deformation and stability of the mixture.

Aggregate requirements depend on the mix type and project specification. See the 20mm, 10mm and 6mm aggregate pages for crushed fractions.

Ingredient 02

Fine aggregate: completes the grading

Fine aggregate consists of smaller mineral particles. It helps complete the aggregate grading and contributes to the internal structure of the mix. It may include suitable crushed fine aggregate and other permitted sources.

What matters

  • Particle-size distribution.
  • Cleanliness and particle shape.
  • Mineral properties and moisture content.
  • Compatibility with the mix design.
Ingredient 03

Mineral filler: the finest fraction

Mineral filler is very fine mineral material. It contributes to the finer portion of the aggregate system and interacts with the bituminous binder.

It can influence

  • Filler-binder interaction and mix consistency.
  • The voids structure and workability.
  • Moisture sensitivity.

The type and quantity must comply with the approved mix design. Recovered fines from dust collection are used only where the specification and mix design permit.

Ingredient 04

Bitumen: binds and coats

Bitumen is the binding material. It coats the aggregate particles and helps form a cohesive mixture.

Binder selection considers

  • Climate and pavement temperature.
  • Traffic loading and project requirements.
  • The binder specification and mix-design performance.
Ingredient 05 · where specified

Additives and modifiers are project-specific

Some projects specify them. They are not automatically part of every Hot Mix.

  • Polymer-modified bitumen (PMB).
  • Crumb rubber modified bitumen (CRMB).
  • Anti-stripping additives.
  • Fibres.
  • Reclaimed Asphalt Pavement (RAP).
  • Other approved modifiers.

Their use, type and dosage must be part of the approved mix design.

The result

A uniform, coated mixture

Controlled mixing coats the mineral surfaces with a thin film of bitumen and produces a uniform mixture. Bitumen is not a pool around the stone: it is a coating on every particle.

The exact proportions are determined by the approved mix design and the applicable project specification.

Types of Hot Mix

Different mixes. Different pavement functions.

Hot Mix Asphalt is not one universal mixture. Different bituminous mixes are designed for different pavement layers, traffic conditions and construction requirements.

01DBM

Dense Bituminous Macadam

A dense graded bituminous mixture generally used as a binder or base course within a flexible pavement, depending on the project design. It is the structural bituminous layer beneath the wearing course, not the final surface.

02BC

Bituminous Concrete

A dense graded bituminous mixture commonly used as a wearing course. It forms the upper bituminous layer, provides the finished riding surface and protects the layers beneath. It is a specified mix type, not simply any black asphalt.

03BM

Bituminous Macadam

A bituminous road construction material used in specified pavement applications. It differs from dense graded DBM in grading and intended structural role. Do not use BM and DBM as interchangeable names.

04SDBC

Semi-Dense Bituminous Concrete

A semi-dense graded bituminous mix used in specified road construction and surfacing applications. Its use depends on the governing specification and pavement design.

05SMA

Stone Matrix Asphalt

A gap-graded mixture designed around a stone-on-stone aggregate skeleton with a binder-rich mortar. Used for specialised performance requirements, and may involve stabilising additives.

06WMA

Warm Mix Asphalt

Produced with technologies that allow mixing and placement at reduced temperatures compared with conventional hot-mix processes: chemical additives, foaming or other approved methods. The specification and equipment determine the process.

07Modified

Modified bituminous mixes

Use an approved modified binder or additive system for specified performance, such as PMB or CRMB. The modification must be part of the approved mix design.

08RAP

Reclaimed Asphalt Pavement mixes

RAP is recovered from existing bituminous pavement. Where permitted, it is processed, characterised and incorporated into a new mix design. Its percentage, binder contribution and grading follow the approved design.

The correct mix type must be selected from the contract specification. Open-graded premix carpet and other surface treatments are different materials and should not be confused with dense graded DBM or BC. Not every mix is suitable for every application, and this page does not state which mixes OM GROUP can supply.

    Bitumen grades and binder selection

    Paving-grade bitumen is chosen for the road, not for the name of the mix.

    In India, IS 73:2013 specifies viscosity-graded paving bitumen.

    The commonly referenced grades are VG-10, VG-20, VG-30 and VG-40. Selection depends on climatic and project conditions, not simply on the mix name.

    VG-10

    A lower-viscosity paving grade, generally considered for relevant cooler-climate or specified applications.

    VG-20

    A paving grade considered for specified climatic and pavement conditions.

    VG-30

    A commonly used paving-grade binder for many road applications, subject to the project specification and climatic selection.

    VG-40

    A higher-viscosity grade that may be selected for highly stressed pavement locations, subject to engineering design and specification.

    A higher VG number is not automatically better

    Selection must consider:

    • High pavement temperatures.
    • Low pavement temperatures.
    • Traffic and loading.
    • Mix design and construction conditions.
    • The applicable specification.

    What IS 73:2013 checks

    The standard classifies paving bitumen by viscosity at 60°C and includes requirements such as:

    • Absolute and kinematic viscosity.
    • Flash point and solubility.
    • Penetration and softening point.
    • Tests on aged binder residue.

    Check the current standard and amendments for the actual limits.

    Selection note. The binder grade shown on this page is for technical explanation. The actual grade used in a project must be specified by the Engineer or approved mix design and must comply with the governing contract.

    Aggregate grading

    Grading decides how the stone packs.

    Aggregate grading describes how the mineral particles are distributed across sizes. A well-designed grading is needed for the required aggregate skeleton, density, stability, workability, air-void structure, durability and resistance to deformation.

    Coarse fraction

    The larger particles that carry the mineral skeleton.

    Intermediate fraction

    Completes the grading and contributes to the packing arrangement.

    Fine fraction

    Fills the smaller spaces between the larger particles.

    Mineral filler

    The finest mineral component, which interacts with the binder.

    The grading is shown as a curve of percentage passing against sieve size. The approved mix design identifies the required grading, with an upper and a lower specified limit.

    Mix design

    The Job Mix Formula defines the mixture.

    The Job Mix Formula (JMF) is the approved mix design that defines the material proportions and production requirements for the specified mixture.

    It establishes

    • Aggregate fractions and grading.
    • Binder type and binder content.
    • Mineral filler and additives, where applicable.
    • Relevant mix properties.

    Why it matters

    Without an approved mix design the producer has no properly established recipe for the specified material. The JMF is the reference for production control: it helps control proportions, consistency, binder content, grading and quality acceptance.

    Optimum binder content

    Optimum binder content is the proportion chosen through the specified mix-design procedure to meet the volumetric and performance requirements. It is not chosen by how the mix looks.

    Too little binder can lead to

    • Poor coating and reduced durability.
    • Ravelling and moisture-related damage.

    Too much binder can contribute to

    • Bleeding and reduced air voids.
    • Reduced stability, and rutting or deformation risk.

    Marshall Mix Design

    A recognised method for designing and evaluating specified bituminous mixes. Specimens are prepared at different binder contents and their properties evaluated. IRC:SP:135-2022 gives guidance on hot bituminous mix design, including Marshall design and volumetric properties.

    Interactive 04 · Asphalt plant explorer

    A plant is an industrial system, not a single machine.

    A Hot Mix Plant is an industrial production system for bituminous asphalt mixtures. It receives and proportions aggregate fractions, dries and heats them, manages dust and exhaust, heats and doses bitumen, adds filler, mixes, and discharges or stores the finished mix. IRC:90-2010 gives guidance on selection, operation and maintenance, for both batch-type and continuous drum-mix configurations.

    Choose a component (batch-type plant shown)

    Stage 3 · feeding

    Cold-feed bins

    Each bin holds one aggregate fraction. Feeders set how fast each fraction leaves its bin, so the combined stream matches the mix design.

    Material flow

    Stockpile → loader → bin → feeder → collecting conveyor

    Stage 5 · conveying

    Conveyor

    Belt conveyors move the combined aggregate through the plant, through transfer points and drives, towards the dryer.

    Material flow

    Cold feed → collecting belt → charging belt → dryer

    Stage 6 · drying and heating

    Rotary dryer and burner

    A rotating drum with internal lifting flights cascades the aggregate through hot gases, removing moisture and heating it to the production condition. The controlled burner sits at the entry end of the drum and heats the gas stream; the flame stays inside the intended combustion arrangement.

    Material flow

    Cold wet aggregate in → burner flame and hot gases → drum rotation and cascading → hot aggregate out

    Stage 7 · dust collection

    Dust collector

    The dryer exhaust carries dust. Ducting takes it to filter bags, which capture fine particles, and cleaner exhaust leaves through the stack. A baghouse supports environmental control; it does not by itself make a plant pollution-free.

    Material flow

    Dryer exhaust → ducting → filter bags → stack; collected fines to hoppers

    Stages 8–9 · batch plants only

    Hot elevator and screening

    In a batch plant, heated aggregate is lifted by an enclosed elevator to the screening section. Vibrating screens then separate it into specified size fractions, which helps control the grading used in each batch. A conventional drum-mix plant does not have these stages.

    Material flow

    Dryer → hot elevator → vibrating screens → individual hot bins

    Stages 10–11 · batch plants only

    Hot bins and weighing

    Each size fraction is stored in its own hot bin until the batch is weighed. The size ranges depend on the plant screens and the approved mix. The fractions are then weighed to the approved mix formula, a key control stage: the system must hold the specified proportions.

    Material flow

    Screens → separate hot bins → weigh hopper → pugmill

    Stage 12 · binder storage

    Bitumen tank

    Insulated tanks hold the binder at a temperature suitable for pumping and mixing. Circulation pumps, insulated pipelines and temperature instruments complete the system.

    Material flow

    Tanker → heated tank → circulation pump → insulated line → dosing

    Stage 15 · batch mixing

    Pugmill

    Twin shafts with paddles combine aggregate, binder and filler until the particles are coated and the batch is uniform.

    Material flow

    Weighed aggregate + binder + filler → paddles → finished batch

    Production control

    Control panel

    The control system and operator watch proportions, temperatures and the production sequence against the approved mix formula.

    Material flow

    Sensors → control system → operator and records

    Stage 17 · discharge

    Discharge

    The finished mix is discharged into a tipper, or into a surge silo or other controlled storage, depending on the plant.

    Material flow

    Mixer → discharge gate → tipper or storage

    A typical batch-type plant, not an OM GROUP facility. The size ranges shown on the hot-bin and screening pictures are examples: real fractions come from the plant screens and the approved mix design.

    Manufacturing process · 26 stages

    From raw material to finished Hot Mix, and on to the road.

    The manufacturing process is a controlled sequence. Step through it from the aggregate stockpile to the completed pavement. Stages 8 to 11 belong to a batch plant only.

    Stage 01 of 26

    Raw material procurement and inspection

    The process begins with suitable raw materials: coarse and fine aggregates, mineral filler, paving bitumen and approved additives. Each is checked against the approved mix design and the applicable specification.

    Inspect

    • Source and documentation.
    • Particle-size distribution and shape.
    • Strength and cleanliness.
    • Moisture, and the binder grade.
    Stage 02 of 26

    Aggregate stockpiling

    Different aggregate fractions are stored separately, which keeps the material separation that controlled proportioning relies on.

    Stockpile management prevents

    • Cross-contamination and uncontrolled mixing.
    • Excessive moisture and foreign material.
    • Unnecessary segregation.
    Stage 03 of 26

    Cold feed bins

    Aggregates are loaded into separate cold-feed bins, each holding one fraction. The feeder system controls the rate at which each fraction enters the conveyor. Depending on plant design, feeders use variable-speed controls and monitoring.

    Why it matters

    • The fractions must be proportioned to give the intended combined grading.
    Stage 04 of 26

    Aggregate proportioning

    The fractions are combined in controlled proportions into one stream, intended to give the grading the approved mix design requires.

    What is controlled

    • Aggregate feed rate and fraction proportion.
    • Combined grading.
    • Feed consistency.
    Stage 05 of 26

    Aggregate conveying

    The combined aggregate moves towards the dryer on conveyors: belt conveyors, transfer points, drive rollers and, where applicable, elevators.

    Stage 06 of 26

    Drying and heating

    Aggregate commonly contains moisture. The dryer reduces it and heats the aggregate to the production condition. A rotary dryer uses a controlled burner and internal lifting flights to expose the material to hot gases.

    Why drying matters

    • Excess moisture affects coating and interferes with mixing.
    • It can create production problems and affect the finished mix.
    Stage 07 of 26

    Dust collection and pollution control

    Dryer exhaust carries dust and fine particles. Dust collection captures material and manages exhaust emissions. A plant may include primary dust collection, cyclones, baghouse filters, ducting, a stack and collected-fines handling.

    A baghouse supports environmental control. It does not by itself make a plant pollution-free.

    Stage 08 of 26 · batch plants only

    Hot aggregate elevator

    In a conventional batch plant, heated aggregate is lifted by an enclosed hot elevator towards the screening and storage section.

    This stage is part of the batch sequence only. It does not appear in a conventional drum-mix plant.

    Omitted from the conventional drum-mix sequence, where this equipment is not present.

    Stage 09 of 26 · batch plants only

    Hot aggregate screening

    Hot aggregate passes over vibrating screens that separate it into specified size fractions. This helps control the grading used in each production batch.

    Omitted from the conventional drum-mix sequence, where this equipment is not present.

    Stage 10 of 26 · batch plants only

    Hot aggregate storage

    Separated fractions are stored in individual hot bins until the batch-weighing operation.

    Omitted from the conventional drum-mix sequence, where this equipment is not present.

    Stage 11 of 26 · batch plants only

    Aggregate weighing

    The required aggregate fractions are weighed according to the approved mix formula. This is a key control stage: the system must hold the specified proportions.

    Omitted from the conventional drum-mix sequence, where this equipment is not present.

    Stage 12 of 26

    Bitumen storage and heating

    Bitumen is stored in suitable heated tanks. The storage and transfer system keeps the binder in a condition suitable for pumping and mixing: insulated tanks, heating, circulation pumps, insulated pipelines, temperature instruments and flow controls.

    Why temperature matters

    • Bitumen viscosity changes with temperature.
    • Too cold: handling and mixing become difficult.
    • Overheated: the binder can age and degrade.
    Stage 13 of 26

    Bitumen dosing and weighing

    The required quantity of binder is measured and introduced into the mixing system. The amount comes from the approved JMF.

    Wrong binder content affects

    • Coating, air voids and stability.
    • Durability and resistance to deformation.
    • Moisture susceptibility.
    Stage 14 of 26

    Mineral filler addition

    The specified mineral filler is added according to the mix design, often through a dedicated filler system. Recovered fines, where permitted, follow the governing specification and mix design.

    Stage 15 of 26

    Mixing

    The central manufacturing stage. In a batch plant, aggregate, binder and filler are combined in a pugmill mixer. In a continuous plant, mixing happens in the drum-mix arrangement. The aim is a uniform mixture in which the particles are coated with bitumen.

    What must be controlled

    • Material proportions and binder addition.

    Interactive 06 · Temperature journey

    Temperature decides whether the mix can still be worked.

    Temperature affects the viscosity and workability of the bituminous mixture. The mix must remain within the specified production, transport and construction conditions. If it becomes too cool before placement and compaction it is difficult to spread and compact properly.

    There is no single temperature that is correct for all Hot Mix. The range depends on the binder grade, the mix design, the plant arrangement, weather, transport distance, the construction specification and the approved rolling procedure. No temperature is built into this page.

    Why too cool, or too hot, is a problem

    Too cool before laying and compaction: hard to spread, hard to compact to the required density. Overheated during production: the binder can age or degrade.

    Common pavement defects

    Understand the problems that good design and construction help control.

    This section helps explain why asphalt mix design and construction quality matter. Each card shows a technical cross-section of the defect, its common contributing factors and the considerations that help control it.

    01

    Rutting

    A longitudinal depression in the wheel paths.

    Common contributing factors

    • Inadequate mix stability and excessive deformation.
    • Poor compaction and weak underlying layers.
    • Heavy traffic and high pavement temperatures.

    Prevention and control

    Mix design that suits the traffic and climate, a suitable binder, and compaction to the specified density.

    02

    Fatigue cracking

    Interconnected cracks that develop under repeated traffic loading.

    Common contributing factors

    • Repeated loading and structural inadequacy.
    • Insufficient pavement thickness.
    • Material deterioration and moisture-related damage.

    Prevention and control

    Adequate pavement design and thickness, a sound foundation and drainage.

    03

    Thermal cracking

    Temperature changes create tensile stresses in the bituminous pavement.

    Common contributing factors

    • Binder properties and the local climate.
    • Pavement conditions.

    Prevention and control

    Binder selection suited to the climate, as the approved mix design requires.

    04

    Ravelling

    Progressive loss of aggregate particles from the pavement surface.

    Common contributing factors

    • Poor aggregate coating and insufficient binder.
    • Poor compaction, ageing and moisture damage.

    Prevention and control

    Correct binder content, good coating and compaction.

    05

    Bleeding or flushing

    Excess binder at the surface gives a shiny or sticky appearance.

    Common contributing factors

    • Excess binder and low air voids.
    • Mix design issues, binder migration and construction problems.

    Prevention and control

    A mix designed through the approved procedure and controlled binder content in production.

    06

    Stripping

    Loss of adhesion between bitumen and aggregate in the presence of moisture.

    Common contributing factors

    • Moisture and unsuitable aggregate-binder interaction.
    • Inadequate drainage and material contamination.
    • Inadequate anti-stripping measures where needed.

    Prevention and control

    Compatible materials, moisture-susceptibility testing where required, good drainage and anti-stripping measures where the design needs them.

    07

    Segregation

    An uneven distribution of coarse and fine aggregate in the mixture or the placed mat.

    Common contributing factors

    • Material handling and stockpile management.
    • Truck loading and discharge.
    • Paver operation.

    Prevention and control

    Careful handling, stockpiling, loading and paver operation.

    08

    Potholes

    Pavement material deteriorates and is displaced.

    Common contributing factors

    • Water ingress and traffic.
    • Cracking and loss of material.
    • Underlying structural failure.

    Prevention and control

    Sealing cracks early, good drainage and sound construction. Repairs follow the contract and the repair method.

    No single mix characteristic prevents every defect, and a defect is not always caused by the mix alone. Pavement design, drainage, the subgrade, traffic and construction quality all matter. The drawings are simplified cross-sections, not to scale.

    FAQ

    Hot Mix Asphalt: Frequently Asked Questions

    Definition and ingredients

    1. What is Hot Mix Asphalt?

    A bituminous pavement mixture produced by combining selected mineral aggregates, mineral filler and bituminous binder in controlled proportions. The materials are heated and mixed, transported, laid and compacted to form a pavement layer.

    2. What is Hot Mix used for?

    Specified bituminous pavement layers in road and infrastructure construction: highways, state and urban roads, industrial roads, resurfacing, widening and rehabilitation.

    3. What does HMA stand for?

    Hot Mix Asphalt.

    4. What is the difference between bitumen and asphalt?

    Bitumen is the petroleum-derived binder. An asphalt mixture is mineral aggregate plus bituminous binder. When a contractor orders Hot Mix they order a specified mixture, not pure bitumen.

    5. What are the ingredients of Hot Mix?

    Coarse aggregate, fine aggregate, mineral filler and bituminous binder, with approved additives or modifiers where specified.

    6. What is the function of aggregate in asphalt?

    It forms the mineral skeleton: aggregate interlock, load distribution, stability and resistance to deformation.

    7. What is the function of bitumen?

    It coats the aggregate particles and binds them into a cohesive mixture.

    8. What is mineral filler?

    Very fine mineral material that forms the finest part of the aggregate system and interacts with the bitumen. Its type and quantity follow the approved mix design.

    9. What is the difference between coarse and fine aggregate?

    Coarse aggregate forms the larger-particle skeleton. Fine aggregate completes the grading and fills the smaller spaces between larger particles.

    10. How is Hot Mix Asphalt manufactured?

    Aggregate fractions are proportioned, dried and heated, binder and filler are added, the materials are mixed, the finished mix is inspected and discharged for transport. Batch and drum-mix plants do this differently.

    Mix types

    11. What is DBM?

    Dense Bituminous Macadam: a dense graded bituminous mix commonly used as a binder or base course in flexible pavements.

    12. What is BC?

    Bituminous Concrete: a dense graded bituminous mix commonly used as the wearing course.

    13. What is the difference between DBM and BC?

    DBM commonly sits beneath the wearing course as a structural bituminous layer; BC commonly forms the top surface. Specifications depend on the project design.

    14. What is Bituminous Macadam?

    A bituminous road construction material used in specified applications. It differs from DBM in grading and structural role, and the two names are not interchangeable.

    15. What is SDBC?

    Semi-Dense Bituminous Concrete: a semi-dense graded mix used in specified surfacing applications, depending on the governing specification.

    16. What is Stone Matrix Asphalt?

    A gap-graded mix built around a stone-on-stone aggregate skeleton with a binder-rich mortar, used for specialised performance requirements.

    17. What is Warm Mix Asphalt?

    An asphalt produced with technologies that allow mixing and placement at lower temperatures than conventional hot mix.

    18. What is modified bitumen?

    Bitumen whose properties are changed with an approved modifier to achieve specified performance. The modification must be part of the approved mix design.

    19. What is polymer-modified bitumen?

    Bitumen modified with a polymer (PMB), used where the specification or approved design calls for it.

    20. What is the difference between a binder course and a wearing course?

    The wearing course is the top surface layer that takes traffic and weather. The binder course sits beneath it as part of the structural bituminous layers.

    Manufacturing

    21. What is a Hot Mix Plant?

    An industrial system that receives and proportions aggregate, dries and heats it, manages dust, heats and doses bitumen, adds filler, mixes and discharges the finished Hot Mix.

    22. How does a batch-type hot mix plant work?

    It makes the mix in individual measured batches: cold feed, drying, hot elevator, screening, hot bins, weighing, bitumen dosing and pugmill mixing.

    23. How does a drum-mix plant work?

    It makes the mix continuously: controlled aggregate feed into a rotating dryer-mixer, where the aggregate is dried and heated and binder and filler are added at designated points.

    24. What is the difference between batch and drum plants?

    Batch plants weigh and mix batch by batch, with hot screens and hot bins. Conventional drum-mix plants produce continuously and do not use the same hot-bin arrangement.

    25. What is a rotary dryer?

    A rotating drum with a controlled burner and internal lifting flights that cascade the aggregate through hot gases to dry and heat it.

    26. Why are aggregates heated?

    To remove moisture and bring the aggregate to the production condition so that it can be coated properly with bitumen.

    27. What is a baghouse filter?

    A dust collector in which filter bags capture the fine particles in the dryer exhaust. It supports environmental control but does not by itself make a plant pollution-free.

    28. What are hot bins?

    Insulated bins in a batch plant that store the screened, heated aggregate fractions separately until they are weighed.

    29. What is a pugmill mixer?

    A mixer with twin shafts and paddles in which weighed aggregate, binder and filler are combined until the particles are coated.

    30. How is bitumen added to aggregates?

    It is heated, pumped through insulated lines, measured, and either introduced into the pugmill (batch) or added at a designated point in the drum (continuous).

    Mix design and quality

    31. What is a Job Mix Formula?

    The approved mix design that defines aggregate fractions and grading, binder type and content, filler and other specified parameters.

    32. What is Marshall Mix Design?

    A method that prepares compacted specimens at different binder contents and evaluates stability, flow and volumetric properties to select the mix.

    33. What is optimum binder content?

    The binder proportion selected through the specified mix-design procedure to meet the volumetric and performance requirements.

    34. What are air voids?

    The volume of air spaces within the compacted mixture. They matter for performance and durability.

    35. What is VMA?

    Voids in Mineral Aggregate: the void space between the aggregate particles in the compacted mix, including air voids and effective binder volume.

    36. What is VFB?

    Voids Filled with Bitumen: the proportion of the VMA occupied by effective bitumen.

    37. What is Marshall stability?

    The maximum load a prepared specimen supports under the prescribed Marshall test method.

    38. What is Marshall flow?

    The deformation of the specimen associated with the Marshall stability test.

    39. Why is aggregate gradation important?

    It decides the aggregate skeleton, density, stability, workability, air-void structure, durability and resistance to deformation.

    40. Which tests are performed on Hot Mix?

    Depending on the mix and project: gradation, binder content, bulk and maximum theoretical specific gravity, air voids, VMA, VFB, Marshall properties, moisture susceptibility, field density and core testing.

    Construction

    41. How is Hot Mix transported?

    In suitable tipper vehicles, with covering where required, coordinated with the paving operation so that the mix arrives while it can still be laid and compacted.

    42. Why is asphalt temperature important?

    It affects binder viscosity, workability and compaction. If the mix is too cool it is hard to spread and compact; an overheated binder can age or degrade. The ranges come from the specification and approved procedure.

    43. What is prime coat?

    A specified bituminous treatment applied to a prepared granular surface before the next pavement layer.

    44. What is tack coat?

    A thin, uniform bituminous application that promotes bonding between specified pavement layers.

    45. How does an asphalt paver work?

    The mix is received in the hopper, carried back by a conveyor, spread across the width by an auger and levelled by the screed into a mat.

    46. Why is compaction necessary?

    To achieve the required density and volumetric structure, reduce excessive air voids, improve particle contact and reach the surface profile.

    47. What is the function of a road roller?

    It compacts the freshly laid mat, in the sequence and with the equipment set by the approved rolling procedure.

    48. How is asphalt density measured?

    By field density testing and by testing core samples, using the method and acceptance limits set in the contract.

    49. How are asphalt core samples collected?

    A core-drilling machine cuts a cylindrical sample through the compacted layer, which is then tested, for example for thickness and density, as the quality plan requires.

    50. How long before asphalt pavement can be opened to traffic?

    There is no universal time. The pavement is opened only when the applicable specification permits and the Engineer approves.

    Applications and procurement

    51. Where is Hot Mix used?

    Highways, state, district, urban, rural and industrial roads, logistics and commercial areas, widening, resurfacing, rehabilitation, bridge approaches, junctions, parking areas and repair works, with the mix chosen by the project designer.

    52. Is Hot Mix suitable for highways?

    Yes, where the pavement design and specification call for DBM, BC or another specified bituminous mix in the highway layers.

    53. What is Hot Mix used for in road resurfacing?

    To place a new bituminous surface over a suitably prepared existing pavement.

    54. How much Hot Mix is needed per kilometre of road?

    It depends on the road width, the compacted thickness and the approved compacted density. Use the calculator on this page with your own values; no figure is assumed.

    55. How is Hot Mix quantity calculated?

    Compacted volume = length × width × compacted thickness. Theoretical mass = volume × the approved compacted density. Final quantities follow the BOQ and measurement method.

    56. What affects Hot Mix prices?

    Aggregate, binder, filler and additive costs, mix type, plant and fuel costs, quality control, transport distance, delivery schedule, quantity and the contract conditions.

    57. Who supplies Hot Mix in Goa?

    This page does not name a supplier. For works under the Goa PWD circular, the plant must be installed within Goa and meet the plant conditions. OM GROUP does not state on this page that it supplies Hot Mix; if you need it, share the requirement and availability will be confirmed.

    58. How do I request a DBM quotation?

    Share the mix type, governing specification, grading, binder grade, approved JMF, quantity, delivery location and schedule using the enquiry builder above.

    59. How do I request a BC quotation?

    The same way: mix type, specification, JMF, quantity, delivery location and schedule. Availability and the commercial response are confirmed against your requirement.

    60. What documents are required before ordering Hot Mix?

    Where required: the approved JMF, binder certificate, aggregate test results, production records, temperature records, mix test results and the delivery challan.
