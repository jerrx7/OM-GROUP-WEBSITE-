    What is Ready-Mix Concrete?

    Concrete Produced at a Batching Plant and Delivered Fresh

    RMC is made by combining measured quantities of concrete-making materials under controlled batching and mixing conditions. The fresh concrete is then carried to the site, generally by transit mixer, for discharge and placement.

    RMC is a process, not just a product. The difference is where and how the concrete is produced.

    Which Indian Standard governs RMC?

    IS 4926:2003 is the Indian Standard code of practice for Ready-Mixed Concrete, covering its production and supply. IS 10262:2019 covers mix proportioning and IS 456:2000 covers plain and reinforced concrete: the three are not interchangeable.

    The two responsibility areas

    What goes into RMC

    • Cementitious materials: cement as the main hydraulic binder, and supplementary materials where the mix design specifies them.
    • Fine aggregate: natural sand or manufactured fine aggregate.
    • Coarse aggregate: crushed stone, forming the coarse skeleton.
    • Water: supports hydration and sets workability.
    • Chemical admixtures: where specified, to modify workability, setting or other characteristics.
    • Mineral admixtures: where the mix designer selects them.

    What RMC is not

    • Not simply wet cement.
    • Not merely concrete transported in a truck.
    • Not a guaranteed higher-strength alternative to all site-mixed concrete.
    • Not a substitute for structural mix design.
    • Not a guarantee of perfect site execution.
    • Not automatically a complete concrete-pumping service.
    • Not a guarantee of structural performance independent of placement and curing.

    RMC vs site-mixed concrete

    Two Production Methods. The Same Requirement for Engineering Quality.

    RMC and site-mixed concrete can both give a specified grade when their materials, proportioning and quality control meet the relevant requirements. The key difference is where and how the concrete is batched and mixed.

    What is the difference between RMC and site-mixed concrete?

    RMC is produced at a batching plant and delivered fresh to the site. Site-mixed concrete is prepared at the construction site. Both can meet a specified grade when their materials, proportioning and quality-control procedures satisfy the applicable requirements.

    Does RMC automatically give better concrete?

    No. A properly designed and controlled site-mixed process can meet its specified requirements. RMC puts storage, proportioning, batching and mixing in a controlled plant, but quality still depends on the materials, mix design, testing, delivery, placement and curing. RMC is particularly useful for larger volumes, repeated pours, limited site storage and projects with defined quality and documentation requirements.

    Concrete grades explained

    Understand What M20, M25, M30 and Higher Grades Mean

    The grade is the characteristic compressive strength, in MPa (N/mm²), at the specified age: commonly 28 days for grade designation. The grade is the target. The mix design develops the proportions.

    Choose a grade. Grade selection follows structural design. M20 to M45 are the standard supply range; M60 stands apart as an on-request grade.

    What does M20 mean?

    M20 denotes concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 20 MPa at 28 days.

    What does M25 mean?

    M25 denotes concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 25 MPa at 28 days.

    What does M60 mean?

    M60 denotes high-strength concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 60 MPa at 28 days. It requires project-specific mix-design and technical confirmation.

    What grades of RMC does OM GROUP supply?

    OM GROUP supplies M20 to M45 as standard grades. High-strength grades such as M60 are available on request, subject to project-specific confirmation.

    Why is design mix so important?

    A mix is not chosen by choosing an M-grade. Two projects needing the same grade can need different mix designs because of differences in aggregate characteristics, exposure, workability, pumping requirements, member geometry, construction method and durability.

    Can a mix ratio be copied from another project?

    No. The mix designer establishes proportions using representative materials and the actual requirements.

    How concrete mix design works

    The Grade Is the Target. The Mix Design Develops the Proportions.

    IS 10262:2019 gives guidelines for concrete mix proportioning. The process balances strength, workability and durability for the actual materials and placement.

    Step through the nine stages. No universal recipe is shown, and no proprietary proportions are published.

    Which standard is used for RMC mix design?

    IS 10262:2019 provides guidelines for concrete mix proportioning. IS 456:2000 provides the broader code of practice for plain and reinforced concrete.

    Stage

    1. Characteristic strengthThe structural design specifies the required characteristic strength.
    2. Target mean strengthThe mix aims for a mean above the characteristic value, to allow for variation in production and testing.
    3. Water-cementitious ratioChosen from strength, durability and exposure requirements.
    4. Aggregate selectionFine and coarse aggregates are selected and evaluated: grading, particle characteristics, specific gravity and other properties.
    5. Cementitious contentThe type and quantity of cementitious material follow the design requirements.
    6. WorkabilitySet by the placing conditions, reinforcement arrangement, delivery method and finishing.
    7. Admixture selectionWhere required, chemical admixtures are used to give the specified fresh-concrete behaviour.
    8. Trial mixesTrial mixes check whether the proposed proportions give the intended performance.
    9. FinalisationThe mix is finalised on the basis of test results and the project requirements.
    A mix design is project- and material-specific. It is not a calculator that can be replaced with a ratio from a web page, and approved mix designs belong to the plant's technical team.

      RMC for specified foundation concrete

      Application · foundations and footings

      Foundations transfer a structure's loads into the supporting ground. The concrete grade and performance requirements depend on the structural design, the soil, the exposure and the type of foundation. RMC can be used in specified foundation concrete where its grade and mix meet the structural design.

      Why RMC can suit foundation pours

      Foundation work can need substantial volume and coordinated placement. RMC supports planned batching, repeated deliveries, less on-site storage of ingredients, coordinated discharge, documented dispatch quantities and project-specific grades.

      What should be planned

      • Foundation dimensions and required volume.
      • Grade, reinforcement completion and formwork readiness.
      • Access for transit mixers, and a pump where applicable.
      • Placement sequence, compaction equipment and curing.

      RMC does not replace foundation design, soil investigation, reinforcement detailing or formwork inspection. The grade comes from the approved structural design.

      Foundation component

      • Isolated footingsConcrete under individual columns, to the designed size and grade.
      • Combined footingsOne footing carrying more than one column.
      • Strip foundationsContinuous footings under walls or rows of columns.
      • Column pedestalsShort columns rising from the footing.
      • Foundation beamsBeams tying footings together.
      • Raft foundationsLarge-volume placements: see the next section.
      • Other designed foundation componentsAs the structural drawings specify.

      Raft foundations and large concrete pours

      Ready-Mix Concrete for reinforced floor and roof slabs

      Application · RCC slabs

      RCC slabs form the floors and roofs of buildings. RMC can be used for specified slab concrete where the mix meets the required grade, workability and durability.

      Common applications

      • Residential and commercial floor slabs, roof slabs, balcony and terrace slabs.
      • Industrial floor slabs, where the mix is specified, and structural mezzanines.

      What matters in a slab pour

      • Grade: from the structural design.
      • Workability: suited to the reinforcement, slab depth and placement method.
      • Pump: where concrete is pumped, the mix and the arrangement are confirmed in advance.
      • Formwork and reinforcement: checked, with cover, embedded services and openings inspected.
      • Compaction, finishing and curing: done to the slab specification.

      Follow the slab pour

      1. Formwork and supportsChecked before placement.
      2. Reinforcement and embedded servicesReinforcement, cover, conduits, sleeves and openings inspected.
      3. Concrete dischargeBy chute or pump, close to the final position.
      4. DistributionSpread to level with controlled movement.
      5. VibrationCompaction removes trapped air and surrounds the steel. Not too little, not too much.

      7. FinishingAt the right stage of the concrete's behaviour, never with added water.
      8. CuringStarted promptly and kept up: RMC does not eliminate the need for curing.

        Is higher slump always better?

        No. The correct consistency is the one that suits the mix design and the placing conditions. Adding water to raise slump uncontrollably can change the effective water-cementitious ratio.

        The correct process

        Identify the placement method, establish the required workability, tell the supplier the target, select the mix design to match, check fresh-concrete consistency by the relevant procedure, and avoid unapproved water addition.

        Remember

        Slump is a workability check, not a strength grade.

        Water-cementitious ratio

        One of the Most Important Parameters in Mix Design

        The water-cementitious ratio relates effective mixing water to cementitious material. It influences strength development, permeability, durability, workability, shrinkage behaviour and overall performance.

        Excess water can raise the ratio above the mix design and harm the hardened concrete. Too little water makes mixing, placement and compaction difficult. The ratio is set by mix design and production control, not casually at the point of discharge.

        How to select the right RMC grade

        Follow the Structural Design. Do Not Choose by Number Alone.

        A higher M-grade is not automatically the correct selection. The structural engineer determines the required grade from the design and the project specification.

        These are the questions to settle with your engineer.

        Question

        • What is the structural member?Slab, beam, column, footing, raft, retaining structure or another element.
        • What loads must it resist?The design loading and structural function influence the required concrete properties.
        • What is the exposure condition?Environmental exposure and durability requirements can influence the design beyond the strength grade.
        • What is the placement method?Conventional discharge, pumping, overhead placement or congested reinforcement can influence workability.
        • What aggregate size is specified?The nominal maximum size should suit the reinforcement spacing and the geometry.
        • What testing is required?Sampling, fresh-concrete checks, cube testing and documentation.
        • What special properties are needed?Pumpability, SCC behaviour, high strength, thermal control or another specified requirement.

        Follow the approved structural design. Do not select M30 simply because it is higher than M25, M40 because it sounds more durable, or the highest available grade because it is the highest.

        FAQ

        Ready-Mix Concrete Applications: Frequently Asked Questions

        1. What is Ready-Mix Concrete?

        Ready-Mix Concrete is concrete produced under controlled batching and mixing conditions at a plant and delivered to a construction site in a fresh state for placement.

        2. What does RMC stand for?

        RMC stands for Ready-Mix Concrete.

        3. What is the difference between RMC and site-mixed concrete?

        RMC is proportioned and mixed at a batching plant, then delivered to the site. Site-mixed concrete is produced at the construction site. Both can meet the same specified grade when their materials, proportioning and quality control meet the applicable requirements.

        4. Is RMC always better than site-mixed concrete?

        No. RMC offers plant-controlled batching and coordinated supply, but the most suitable method depends on project scale, access, logistics, quality-control arrangements and construction requirements.

        5. Which Indian Standard governs RMC production and supply?

        IS 4926:2003 is the Indian Standard code of practice for Ready-Mixed Concrete. It covers production and supply requirements.

        6. Which standard is used for concrete mix design?

        IS 10262:2019 provides guidelines for concrete mix proportioning.

        7. Which standard governs plain and reinforced concrete?

        IS 456:2000 is the code of practice for plain and reinforced concrete, read with its applicable amendments.

        8. What grades of RMC does OM GROUP supply?

        OM GROUP lists M20 through M45 as standard supply grades. Higher-strength grades such as M60 are available on request, subject to technical and availability confirmation.

        9. What does M20 mean?

        M20 designates concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 20 MPa at 28 days.

        10. What does M25 mean?

        M25 designates concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 25 MPa at 28 days.

        11. What does M30 mean?

        M30 designates concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 30 MPa at 28 days.

        12. What does M35 mean?

        M35 designates concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 35 MPa at 28 days.

        13. What does M40 mean?

        M40 designates concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 40 MPa at 28 days.

        14. What does M45 mean?

        M45 designates concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 45 MPa at 28 days.

        15. What does M60 mean?

        M60 designates high-strength concrete with a characteristic compressive strength of 60 MPa at 28 days. It requires project-specific mix-design and technical confirmation.

        16. Does a higher concrete grade always mean better concrete?

        No. The grade must follow the structural design. Durability also depends on materials, exposure, water-cementitious ratio, cover, detailing, placement and curing.

        17. What is the difference between nominal mix and design mix?

        Nominal mix uses fixed proportions permitted under the relevant provisions of IS 456, for concrete up to M20. Design mix is proportioned according to the selected materials and project requirements and is mandatory for M25 and higher grades under the IS 456 framework.

        18. What is concrete mix design?

        Concrete mix design is the process of proportioning cementitious materials, aggregates, water and admixtures to achieve the specified strength, workability and durability.

        19. Does OM GROUP publish standard mix ratios?

        No universal proprietary mix ratio should be used for every project. The proportions must be determined by the project-specific mix design.

        20. What is characteristic compressive strength?

        It is the strength value below which no more than 5 percent of the relevant test results are expected to fall, as defined under the concrete design and acceptance framework.

        21. What is target mean strength?

        Target mean strength is the average strength that the mix design aims to achieve above the characteristic strength, accounting for expected variation in concrete production and testing.

        22. What is water-cementitious ratio?

        It is the ratio of effective water to cementitious materials in a concrete mix. It is an important parameter influencing concrete strength and durability.

        23. What is slump in concrete?

        Slump is a test used to assess the consistency of fresh concrete under the prescribed testing procedure.

        24. Does slump measure concrete strength?

        No. Slump measures fresh-concrete consistency. Compressive strength is evaluated through hardened-concrete strength testing.

        25. Is higher slump always better?

        No. The appropriate consistency depends on the placing method, reinforcement, member geometry and mix specification. Excessive water addition should not be used as an uncontrolled way to increase slump.

        26. What is pumpable concrete?

        Pumpable concrete is a mix designed with suitable grading, workability and cohesion for movement through a concrete pumping pipeline.

        27. Is every RMC mix pumpable?

        No. Pumpability depends on aggregate grading, mix design, admixtures, pipeline geometry, distance, vertical lift and other placement conditions.

        28. Does OM GROUP provide concrete pumping?

        Pump requirements can be discussed as part of the concrete enquiry. Availability and any separate pump arrangement must be confirmed. Do not assume that transit-mixer supply automatically includes a pump.

        29. Can RMC be used for foundations?

        Yes. RMC can be used for specified foundation concrete when its grade and mix meet the structural design requirements.

        30. Can RMC be used for slabs and beams?

        Yes. RMC can be used for specified slab and beam concrete, subject to the structural design and project mix requirement.

        31. Can RMC be used for columns?

        Yes. RMC may be used in structural columns when the selected mix is appropriate for the design, reinforcement arrangement and placement method.

        32. Can RMC be used for raft foundations?

        Yes. RMC can be used for raft foundations. Large-volume placement may require additional planning for delivery coordination, temperature control, joints and curing.

        33. Can RMC be used for retaining walls?

        Yes. RMC can be used for specified retaining-wall concrete. The grade and mix must follow the design and exposure requirements.

        34. Can RMC be used for high-rise buildings?

        Yes. RMC can be used for high-rise construction where the mix grade, workability, pumping requirements, durability and delivery arrangements meet the project specification.

        35. Can RMC be used for infrastructure?

        Yes. RMC may be used in specified infrastructure concrete, subject to project specifications and quality-control requirements.

        36. Can RMC be used for precast concrete?

        Yes, where the selected mix and delivery method suit the precast manufacturing process and product requirements.

        37. Can RMC be used for self-compacting concrete?

        SCC requires a separate specialised mix design and fresh-concrete testing. Availability must be confirmed for the particular project.

        38. Does RMC require curing?

        Yes. Cement-based concrete requires appropriate curing and protection after placement. RMC does not eliminate the need for site curing.

        39. Can water be added to RMC at the site?

        Water should not be added without authorisation and consideration of the mix design. Uncontrolled water addition can change the effective water-cementitious ratio. Any permitted adjustment should follow the agreed procedure and be documented.

        40. What is a concrete cube test?

        A cube test measures the compressive strength of a prepared concrete specimen under the specified testing method.

        41. When are concrete cubes tested?

        Concrete specimens are tested at specified ages. Common ages include 7 and 28 days, with the 28-day result commonly used for grade designation.

        42. Does one cube result determine whether an entire load has passed?

        Not necessarily. Concrete acceptance is based on the applicable sampling and acceptance criteria. Test results must be evaluated as required by the relevant standard and project specification.

        43. What is a concrete delivery ticket?

        A delivery ticket is the record accompanying an RMC dispatch. It typically identifies the plant, ticket number, date, truck, grade, workability, quantity, loading time and other applicable mix details.
