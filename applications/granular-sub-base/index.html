Granular Sub-Base (GSB) Applications for Highways, Roads & Infrastructure

The engineered granular layer beneath a well-designed road. Granular Sub-Base is a specified blend of graded granular material placed and compacted over prepared subgrade to form the sub-base layer of a road pavement.

It is not a single aggregate size or an uncontrolled crushing by-product. Its grading, material properties, strength, moisture condition and compaction must meet the requirements of the applicable project specification.

GSB helps distribute traffic-induced loads, protects the underlying subgrade and can perform a drainage or filter function where the selected grading and pavement design require it.

    Granular Sub-Base in Twelve Short Answers

    The questions engineers, contractors and buyers ask first, answered plainly. The sections below explain each one in full.

    What is GSB?

    Granular Sub-Base is a specified graded granular material placed and compacted over prepared subgrade to form the sub-base layer of a road pavement.

    What does GSB stand for?

    GSB stands for Granular Sub-Base.

    What is GSB used for?

    GSB is used in highway, state-road, rural-road, urban-road, industrial-road, access-road and infrastructure pavement construction where the design requires a granular sub-base.

    Which standard governs GSB?

    MoRTH Clause 401 in Section 400 is the principal reference under the applicable revision. The project contract determines the exact grading and acceptance requirements.

    What are the six GSB gradings?

    The traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision Table 400-1 contains Gradings I, II, III, IV, V and VI.

    Which GSB gradings are used for drainage?

    The traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision identifies Gradings V and VI for sub-base-cum-drainage applications. The actual hydraulic performance depends on the pavement and drainage design.

    What is GSB CBR?

    CBR is a penetration-based test used to assess the resistance of compacted sub-base material relative to a reference crushed-stone standard.

    What is the traditional Fifth Revision GSB CBR reference?

    The traditional Fifth Revision Table 400-2 specifies a minimum CBR of 30 at 98% laboratory dry density unless otherwise specified in the contract.

    Is GSB a single-size aggregate?

    No. GSB is a graded blend of permitted granular materials, proportioned to meet a specified grading envelope.

    Is GSB the same as WMM?

    No. GSB is the sub-base layer. WMM is generally the base course above it and is governed by a different specification.

    Is crusher dust the same as GSB?

    No. Unclassified crusher dust does not automatically meet the GSB grading and physical requirements.

    Does OM GROUP manufacture GSB?

    Yes. OM GROUP manufactures and supplies GSB across North Goa and South Goa, with grading matched to the applicable project specification.

    What is Granular Sub-Base

    A specified granular layer beneath the pavement base.

    Granular Sub-Base is a pavement material composed of permitted granular constituents combined to meet a defined particle-size distribution.

    It is placed over a prepared subgrade and compacted to the required line, level, cross-section and density.

    The GSB layer forms part of the pavement system.

    Why is it called Granular Sub-Base?

    Granular refers to the particle-based material.

    Sub-Base refers to its position below the main pavement base course.

    GSB is not the final road surface.

    It is an underlying pavement layer that contributes to the overall structural and functional performance of the road.

    What materials can be used?

    Under the traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision Clause 401 framework, GSB material may include:

    • Natural sand.
    • Crushed gravel.
    • Crushed stone.
    • Crushed slag.
    • Permitted combinations of these materials.

    The Fifth Revision also allows certain materials such as brick metal, kankar and crushed concrete in the lower sub-base under its specified provisions.

    The material must be free from organic and other deleterious constituents and must conform to the applicable grading and physical requirements.

    Important: The materials permitted by a particular project must be checked against the current governing specification. Do not assume every listed material is permitted in every GSB layer or tender.

    What makes GSB different?

    GSB is not selected only by visual appearance.

    Its suitability depends on:

    • Particle-size distribution.
    • Aggregate characteristics.
    • Fines content.
    • Plasticity.
    • CBR.
    • Moisture-density relationship.
    • Compaction.
    • Drainage function where required.
    • Project-specific acceptance requirements.

    GSB is a blend, not a single size

    A 20mm aggregate is a single-size product.

    A 40mm aggregate is a single-size product.

    GSB is a combination of particle sizes proportioned to achieve a specified grading envelope.

    That is why an individual aggregate fraction cannot automatically be called GSB.

        Why GSB matters in road construction

        A pavement foundation is only as effective as its supporting layers.

        GSB contributes to the structural and functional performance of the pavement.

        01

        Load distribution

        GSB helps distribute stresses transmitted through the upper pavement layers towards the subgrade.

        The overall effect depends on:

        • GSB thickness.
        • Material properties.
        • Compaction.
        • Subgrade strength.
        • Pavement design.
        • Traffic loading.
        02

        Subgrade protection

        GSB provides an intermediate granular layer between the upper pavement and the underlying soil.

        It can help reduce direct exposure of the subgrade to construction activity and pavement stresses.

        03

        Separation

        A suitably designed GSB layer can reduce the migration and mixing of subgrade soil into the overlying pavement structure.

        The filter compatibility of the materials is important.

        04

        Drainage

        Certain GSB gradings are used as sub-base-cum-drainage layers.

        The grading and design must permit the intended movement of water.

        05

        Construction platform

        A properly constructed GSB layer can provide a working platform for subsequent pavement construction.

        Its ability to carry construction traffic depends on the material, thickness, subgrade and design.

        06

        Pavement support

        GSB provides support to the specified base course above it.

        07

        Pavement system integration

        The performance of GSB depends on the surrounding pavement layers and drainage system.

        It cannot compensate for an unsuitable subgrade or defective pavement design.

        Important qualification

        GSB does not independently guarantee:

        • Road strength.
        • Pavement life.
        • Load capacity.
        • Drainage performance.
        • Resistance to settlement.

        These depend on the entire pavement system and construction quality.

        GSB grading and MoRTH Clause 401

        Six Traditional Gradings. Different Particle-Size Distributions. Different Functions.

        The traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision Clause 401 framework identifies six GSB gradings. Each defines the percentage of material permitted to pass specified IS sieves. They are not simply different commercial names for the same material.

        Standards note

        Fifth Revision reference · Sixth Revision from 1 January 2027

        GSB grading, physical requirements, CBR, layer thickness and compaction must be confirmed against the MoRTH revision and project specification applicable to the work. The traditional Grading I–VI table shown in this guide is identified as the MoRTH Fifth Revision reference and must not be treated as a universal requirement for every project.

        MoRTH published the Sixth Revision of its Specifications for Road and Bridge Works in October 2026. According to Circular No. 407.29 of 29 October 2026 it becomes effective for National Highway works from 1 January 2027. State PWD, municipal, rural-road and other contracts follow their own adopted specifications and amendments. No Sixth Revision grading table is shown here: the revised Clause 401 and its tables have to be verified from the published document, and the traditional table is not assumed to be unchanged.

                  Maximum Dry Density

                  MDD is the highest dry density achieved by a material under the specified laboratory compaction procedure.

                  Optimum Moisture Content

                  OMC is the moisture content at which that maximum dry density is achieved under the same test procedure.

                  Water helps the granular particles rearrange during compaction.

                  MDD provides the laboratory reference against which field density may be assessed.

                  Section 50 · How to calculate GSB quantity

                  Calculate the compacted layer volume first.

                  For a rectangular or uniform road section:

                  Compacted volume = Length × Width × Compacted thickness

                  Where:

                  • Length is measured in metres.
                  • Width is measured in metres.
                  • Thickness is measured in metres.

                  The result is in cubic metres.

                  Example

                  Consider a hypothetical road section:

                  • Length: 1,000m.
                  • Width: 7m.
                  • Compacted GSB thickness: 0.20m.

                  Calculation:

                  1,000 × 7 × 0.20 = 1,400m³

                  Compacted GSB volume: 1,400m³.

                  Important procurement distinction

                  This is the theoretical compacted layer volume.

                  It is not automatically the loose volume to be ordered.

                  Actual procurement quantity depends on:

                  • Material density.
                  • Moisture.
                  • Loose bulk density.
                  • Compaction.
                  • Handling losses.
                  • Site conditions.
                  • Measurement method.
                  • Contract payment basis.

                  Tonnes conversion

                  If a verified bulk density is available:

                  Estimated mass = Compacted volume × Applicable density

                  However, the density used for conversion must be appropriate to the measurement basis.

                  Do not use an assumed generic density to calculate a final commercial order.

                  For different road sections:

                  Calculate each section separately and add the results.

                  FAQ

                  Granular Sub-Base Applications: Frequently Asked Questions

                  Definition and basics

                  1. What is GSB?

                  GSB stands for Granular Sub-Base. It is a specified graded granular material placed and compacted over prepared subgrade to form the sub-base layer of a road pavement.

                  2. What does Granular Sub-Base mean?

                  Granular Sub-Base refers to the granular pavement layer below the base course and above the prepared subgrade.

                  3. What is GSB used for?

                  GSB is used in specified highway, state-road, rural-road, urban-road, industrial-road, access-road and infrastructure pavement construction.

                  4. What is MoRTH Clause 401?

                  MoRTH Clause 401 is the principal specification reference for Granular Sub-Base in Section 400 of the Specifications for Road and Bridge Works.

                  5. Is GSB a single-size aggregate?

                  No. GSB is a graded blend of permitted granular materials that must meet the specified sieve distribution.

                  6. What materials are used in GSB?

                  Under the traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision, GSB may use natural sand, crushed gravel, crushed stone, crushed slag or combinations, with additional materials permitted in specified circumstances and subject to the governing requirements.

                  Grading and specifications

                  7. What are the six GSB gradings?

                  The traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision Table 400-1 contains Gradings I, II, III, IV, V and VI.

                  8. What is GSB Grading I?

                  Grading I is one of the traditional MoRTH GSB grading envelopes, with specified particle-size limits from 75mm down to 75 microns.

                  9. What is GSB Grading II?

                  Grading II is an alternative grading envelope with different percentage-passing limits from Grading I.

                  10. What is GSB Grading III?

                  Grading III is a separate grading category. The Fifth Revision identifies Gradings III and IV as preferred for lower sub-base applications.

                  11. What is GSB Grading IV?

                  Grading IV is a separate grading category with its own sieve limits. Its use depends on the pavement design and project specification.

                  12. What is GSB Grading V?

                  Grading V is one of the traditional MoRTH gradings used as a sub-base-cum-drainage layer under the Fifth Revision framework.

                  13. What is GSB Grading VI?

                  Grading VI is another traditional grading associated with sub-base-cum-drainage applications.

                  14. Which GSB grading is best?

                  There is no universally best grading. The contract, pavement design and layer function determine the correct grading.

                  15. Which GSB grading is used for drainage?

                  Under the traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision, Gradings V and VI are identified for sub-base-cum-drainage applications. Actual drainage performance must be confirmed through the pavement design and hydraulic requirements.

                  Strength and testing

                  21. What is GSB CBR?

                  CBR is the California Bearing Ratio, a penetration-based test used to assess the resistance of compacted material relative to a reference crushed-stone standard.

                  22. What CBR is required for GSB?

                  The traditional Fifth Revision Table 400-2 includes a minimum CBR of 30 at 98% laboratory dry density unless otherwise specified in the contract. The governing project specification must be checked.

                  23. What is soaked CBR?

                  Soaked CBR is determined after the specimen undergoes the prescribed soaking procedure before testing.

                  24. Why is CBR important?

                  CBR helps engineers evaluate the support characteristics of the compacted sub-base material under the specified test conditions.

                  25. What is Aggregate Impact Value?

                  Aggregate Impact Value measures aggregate resistance to impact under the prescribed test method.

                  26. What is the maximum AIV for GSB?

                  The traditional MoRTH Fifth Revision Table 400-2 specifies a maximum Aggregate Impact Value of 40. Confirm the applicable current project requirement.

                  27. What is Wet Aggregate Impact Value?

                  Wet AIV is an aggregate impact test conducted under the prescribed wet test condition.

                  28. When is Wet AIV required?

                  Under the Fifth Revision Clause 401 provision, aggregate with water absorption exceeding 2% under IS 2386 Part 3 is subject to Wet AIV testing under IS 5640. The exact project provision must be checked.

                  29. What is the Liquid Limit requirement for GSB?

                  The traditional Fifth Revision Table 400-2 specifies a maximum Liquid Limit of 25.

                  30. What is the Plasticity Index requirement for GSB?

                  The traditional Fifth Revision Table 400-2 specifies a maximum PI of 6.

                  31. Why are fines important in GSB?

                  Fines contribute to the overall grading, but excessive or unsuitable plastic fines can increase moisture sensitivity and affect compaction and drainage.

                  32. What is the 75-micron sieve used for?

                  The 75-micron sieve measures the very fine portion of the material. The permitted percentage depends on the applicable grading.

                  33. What is sieve analysis?

                  Sieve analysis determines the particle-size distribution of the material and verifies whether it conforms to the required grading envelope.

                  Construction

                  34. What is OMC?

                  Optimum Moisture Content is the moisture content at which the material achieves its Maximum Dry Density under the specified laboratory compaction procedure.

                  35. What is MDD?

                  Maximum Dry Density is the highest dry density achieved under the specified laboratory compaction test.

                  36. What is GSB compaction?

                  GSB compaction is the mechanical densification of the spread granular material to meet the required field-density and layer-geometry criteria.

                  37. What density is required for GSB?

                  The density requirement depends on the governing specification. A minimum 98% of laboratory MDD is a common Fifth Revision reference, but the project contract must be checked.

                  38. What is the minimum thickness of GSB?

                  Thickness depends on pavement design and contract. The Fifth Revision specifies a minimum 150mm thickness for each layer where the sub-base is laid in two layers.

                  39. Can GSB be used as a drainage layer?

                  Yes, where the specified grading and pavement design assign it a drainage function. Gradings V and VI are traditional MoRTH sub-base-cum-drainage gradings.

                  40. Does GSB prevent waterlogging?

                  Not by itself. Drainage depends on the complete pavement and water-management system, including permeability, filter compatibility and effective outlets.

                  Applications and comparisons

                  16. Is GSB the same as WMM?

                  No. GSB is the sub-base layer, while WMM is generally used as a base course above it. They are governed by different MoRTH clauses and are not interchangeable.

                  17. Is GSB the same as WBM?

                  No. WBM is Water Bound Macadam, a separate road-construction method with its own grading and construction procedure.

                  18. Can 20mm aggregate be used as GSB?

                  Not automatically as supplied. A 20mm aggregate is a single-size fraction, while GSB requires a specified graded blend. It may be used in a permitted blend if the final material meets the required grading and physical properties.

                  19. Can 40mm aggregate be used as GSB?

                  Not automatically as supplied. The final GSB material must meet the specified grading envelope.

                  20. Is crusher dust the same as GSB?

                  No. Crusher dust is a fine crushing output. It is not automatically a specification-compliant GSB material.

                  41. Can GSB be used beneath concrete roads?

                  Yes, where specified in the rigid pavement design. The exact layer arrangement may include GSB, DLC and concrete pavement.

                  42. Is GSB used for industrial roads?

                  Yes, GSB may form the sub-base of industrial roads where required by the pavement design.
