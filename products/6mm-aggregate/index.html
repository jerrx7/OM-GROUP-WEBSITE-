What Is 6mm Aggregate?

6mm aggregate is crushed stone at a 6mm trade size, used mainly in paver block and tile manufacture, blocks, precast products and surface finishes.

Aggregate is the stone fraction of concrete and mortar. In standard aggregate classification, the 4.75mm sieve is the boundary between fine aggregate, such as sand, and coarse aggregate. 6mm crushed aggregate is generally treated as a coarse aggregate fraction because it sits above that boundary, though the actual grading should be confirmed against the product requirement.

6mm is one of the smaller crushed stone fractions traded in construction markets. Because the particles are small, it packs closely, fills voids efficiently and produces an even surface — the properties a paver block, a chequered tile or a rendered finish depends on. Those are different properties from the ones structural concrete needs, which is why 6mm is a finishing and manufacturing material rather than an RCC aggregate.

Where 6mm sits against the standard. IS 383:2016 tabulates nominal coarse aggregate sizes of 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm. 6mm is not one of them. A 6mm product should therefore be specified by its actual grading and sieve requirements rather than presented as an IS 383 nominal-size grade. If your production or client requires a particular sieve range, state it with your enquiry.

What 6mm Aggregate Is Called — Chips, Grit, Kapchi and Mamri

Trade names for smaller crushed stone fractions vary considerably across India, and 6mm sits close to the dividing line between several of them.

Stone chips

Widespread, particularly in eastern and southern India.

Grit

Commonly used for material below roughly 6mm.

Kapchi

Gujarat and western India, commonly used for material above roughly 6mm.

Mamri

Appears in some western India trade usage specifically for 6mm.

Jelly & blue metal

Southern terms for crushed stone generally.

These are not exact synonyms. Usage varies by region, supplier and application — particularly between asphalt road work and cement concrete work.

What Is 6mm Aggregate Used For?

A finishing and manufacturing input.

6mm is not the conventional coarse aggregate size for structural RCC. Structural concrete commonly uses 10mm, 20mm and other project-specified aggregate sizes.

Paver blocks & interlocking blocks

A major application for 6mm aggregate. Aggregate selection is part of the approved paver mix and material requirements — 6mm may form part of a mix where the approved formulation calls for it.

Concrete & chequered tiles

Surface appearance depends heavily on the smaller aggregate fraction, particularly on exposed or polished faces.

Hollow & solid blocks

The finer fraction assists mould fill and produces a closer finish on exposed faces.

Precast products

Used in detailed and exposed-face elements where the approved mix calls for a smaller fraction.

Surface finishing & grit

Used where a specified crushed fraction is required in rendered or textured finishes. Conventional plaster uses sand; confirm what the specification calls for.

Where specified: screeds, roofing, drainage

Tile bedding and floor screeds; roof chippings over waterproofing membranes; decorative and landscaping work; drainage and filter media; fine asphalt layers — all conditional on the membrane system, specification and fines content.

6mm Aggregate, Stone Dust or M-Sand — Which Do You Need?

Not interchangeable — and confused regularly.

These three are confused regularly, and ordering the wrong one is an expensive mistake for block and paver producers.

MaterialWhat it is
6mm aggregateA coarser crushed stone fraction at 6mm trade size
Stone dust / crusher dustThe fine by-product fraction from crushing, with a higher proportion of fines
M-SandManufactured sand — crushed rock processed and graded to sand requirements, with fines controlled

The practical difference is fines content and the degree of grading control. Most paver, tile and block mixes use a combination rather than one material alone, in proportions set by the mix design. If a mix design exists, order to it. Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions, answered directly.

What is 6mm aggregate?

6mm aggregate is crushed stone at a 6mm trade size, used mainly in paver block and tile manufacture, blocks, precast products and surface finishes. It is a finishing and manufacturing material rather than a structural concrete aggregate.

Is 6mm aggregate coarse or fine aggregate?

6mm crushed aggregate is generally treated as a coarse aggregate fraction because it sits above the 4.75mm sieve, which is the boundary between fine and coarse aggregate in standard classification. Actual grading should be confirmed against the product requirement.

Is 6mm aggregate an IS 383 size?

IS 383:2016 tabulates nominal coarse aggregate sizes of 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm, and 6mm is not among them. A 6mm product should be specified by its actual grading and sieve requirements rather than presented as an IS 383 nominal-size grade.

What is 6mm aggregate used for?

6mm aggregate is used mainly for paver blocks, concrete and chequered tiles, hollow and solid blocks, precast products and surface finishing. It is also used, where specified, in screeds and tile bedding, roof chippings over waterproofing, decorative and landscaping work, drainage media and fine asphalt layers.

Can 6mm aggregate be used in RCC?

6mm is not the conventional coarse aggregate size for structural RCC. Structural concrete commonly uses 10mm, 20mm and other project-specified sizes, with the choice set by the member and the approved mix design.

Is 6mm aggregate used for paver blocks?

Yes, this is a major application. Concrete paving block production is governed by its product specification, and aggregate selection forms part of the approved mix and material requirements — 6mm may be included where the approved formulation calls for it.

Can 6mm aggregate be used for plastering?

6mm can be used where a specified crushed fraction is called for in grit or textured finishes. Conventional plaster uses sand, so confirm what the specification requires.

What is the difference between 6mm and 10mm aggregate?

6mm is a finishing and manufacturing material for pavers, tiles, blocks, precast and surface work. 10mm is a coarse aggregate used in structural concrete where sections are thin or reinforcement is congested. They serve different purposes and are not substitutes.

What is the difference between 6mm aggregate and stone dust?

6mm aggregate is a coarser crushed stone fraction at 6mm trade size. Stone dust is the fine by-product fraction from crushing, with a higher proportion of fines. They behave differently in a mix and are not interchangeable.

What is the difference between 6mm aggregate and M-Sand?

6mm aggregate is a coarser crushed stone fraction. M-Sand is manufactured sand — crushed rock processed and graded to sand requirements with fines controlled. Most block and paver mixes use a combination, in proportions set by the mix design.

What is 6mm aggregate called in the trade?

Depending on region and supplier it may be called stone chips, grit, kapchi or mamri. These are not exact synonyms and usage varies, so confirm the sieve size when ordering rather than relying on the trade name.
