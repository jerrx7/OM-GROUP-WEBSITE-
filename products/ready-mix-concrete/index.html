What Is Ready Mix Concrete?

Ready Mix Concrete (RMC) is concrete produced under controlled batching and mixing conditions and supplied to a construction site in a fresh state for placement.

RMC is not simply concrete delivered by truck — it is a controlled production and supply system. Raw materials are stored and proportioned at a batching plant to a specified mix design, mixed under quality control, then transported and delivered ready for placement, with consistency managed at the point of production rather than assembled on site.

The abbreviation RMC is used interchangeably with Ready Mix Concrete in Indian construction trade usage. IS 4926:2003, the Indian Standard governing RMC production and supply, sets out batching, mixing, quality control and delivery-documentation requirements for the plant-and-transport side of the process — it does not cover placement, compaction, curing or protection after delivery, which remain the contractor's responsibility on site.

RMC vs Site-Mixed Concrete

Two ways to arrive at the same grade.

RMC and site-mixed concrete can both meet the same specified grade — the difference is in how consistently that grade is met batch to batch, and what it takes on site to get there.

RMC Materials

What goes into the mix, and what each does.

Cementitious materials

Cement and, where used, supplementary cementitious materials — the primary binder driving strength development and setting behaviour.

Fine aggregate

Sand or manufactured sand, filling the space between coarse particles and affecting workability, cohesion and finish.

Coarse aggregate

Crushed stone carrying the bulk of the mix by volume, with grading affecting workability, packing and finished surface.

Water

Hydrates the cement and contributes to workability — the water-cementitious ratio is a primary driver of strength and durability.

Chemical admixtures

Used where the mix design calls for them, to modify setting time, workability or durability characteristics for the specific requirement.

Mix design

Proportions each of the above against the project's strength, workability and durability requirement — not a fixed recipe applied uniformly.

No proprietary mix ratio is published on this page. Proportions are project-specific, set by the mix design against IS 10262:2019 — publishing a generic recipe without a project-specific basis would be inaccurate rather than merely unhelpful.

How Ready Mix Concrete Mix Design Works

The grade is the target; the mix design gets you there.

Mix design balances characteristic strength, target mean strength, water-cementitious ratio, workability, aggregate grading, cementitious content, admixture use, durability requirement and exposure condition against the project's actual specification, referenced against IS 10262:2019. It is not a calculator this page can substitute for — the requirement should come from the project's structural design.

Which Grade Should I Choose?

Follow the structural design.

The correct RMC grade is determined by the structural design and project specification, not simply by choosing the highest available grade. Structural engineers set the requirement based on design loads, exposure conditions, durability needs, member type and construction method. This page provides technical reference information; it does not substitute for structural design.

Questions worth putting to your engineer if the requirement is still being assessed:

  • What are the design loads and member type? Columns, foundations and heavily loaded members generally point toward a higher grade than slabs or footings.
  • What is the exposure condition? Durability requirements linked to exposure can affect the water-cementitious ratio even at the same grade.
  • Is the concrete being pumped? Placement method feeds into the mix design's workability target.
  • What documentation does the project require? Testing and sampling frequency should be confirmed alongside the grade.

Workability, Pumping, Durability & Quality

What decides how it places, lasts and performs.

Workability & slump

Required workability should be specified according to placement method and project requirement — no universal slump figure is published here, and none is invented.

Pumpable concrete

Pumpability depends on aggregate grading, workability, mix design, admixtures and pumping distance. Not every mix is automatically pumpable — discuss the requirement directly.

Durability

Durability depends on mix design, water-cementitious ratio, materials, curing, exposure, cover, placement and workmanship. A higher M-grade number does not automatically mean more durable.

Quality control

Typical RMC practice includes raw-material checks, batching accuracy, fresh-concrete checks, sampling, cube testing and delivery documentation — stated as typical industry practice, not a specific certified process unless separately confirmed.

Compressive strength

Characteristic strength is the value below which no more than 5% of test results are expected to fall — a single cube result should never be read in isolation against the grade number.

Curing & site responsibility

RMC quality does not end at delivery. Final performance depends on placement, compaction, finishing and curing — all outside IS 4926's production-and-supply scope and outside OM Group's delivery responsibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Definition

What is Ready Mix Concrete?

Ready Mix Concrete (RMC) is concrete produced under controlled batching and mixing conditions and supplied to a construction site in a fresh state for placement, rather than mixed on site.

What is RMC?

RMC is the commonly used abbreviation for Ready Mix Concrete.

How is RMC made?

Raw materials are stored and proportioned at a batching plant to a specified mix design, mixed under quality control, then transported by transit mixer for site delivery and discharge.

What is the difference between RMC and site-mixed concrete?

RMC is batched under controlled plant conditions with batch-to-batch consistency managed at production; site-mixed concrete is prepared on site, with consistency depending on site practice and crew.

Technical

What is characteristic compressive strength?

Characteristic compressive strength is the strength below which no more than 5% of test results are expected to fall — the statistical basis for the grade designation.

What is cube strength?

Cube strength is the compressive strength measured from a standard 150mm cube specimen, typically tested at 28 days.

What is target mean strength?

Target mean strength is the mean strength a mix design aims for, set above the characteristic strength to statistically ensure the characteristic value is met across a batch of results.

What is IS 4926?

IS 4926:2003 is the Indian Standard governing the production and supply of Ready Mixed Concrete — batching, mixing, quality control and delivery documentation.

What is IS 10262?

IS 10262:2019 is the Indian Standard guideline for concrete mix proportioning, used to design the mix for a specified grade.

What is IS 456?

IS 456:2000 is the Indian Standard code of practice for plain and reinforced concrete, and is the source of the concrete grade table used to classify ordinary, standard and high-strength concrete.

What is the difference between nominal mix and design mix?

Nominal mix uses fixed proportions permitted only at M20 and below under IS 456:2000. Design mix is proportioned specifically to the project's materials and requirement, and is mandatory from M25 upward.

Mix Design

What is concrete mix design?

Concrete mix design is the process of proportioning cement, aggregates, water and admixtures to meet a project's strength, workability and durability requirements, referenced against IS 10262:2019.

What is water-cement ratio?

Water-cement ratio is the ratio of water to cementitious material in a mix, a primary driver of both strength and durability.

What factors determine mix design?

Characteristic strength, target mean strength, water-cementitious ratio, workability, aggregate grading, cementitious content, admixture use, durability requirement and exposure condition all feed into the mix design.

Can I get a specific mix recipe from OM Group?

No — mix proportions are project-specific and determined through the mix design process, not published as a generic recipe.

Quality

How is RMC quality controlled?

Typical RMC practice includes raw-material checks, batching accuracy control, fresh-concrete checks such as slump, sampling, cube testing and delivery documentation.

How is concrete tested?

Primarily through cube compressive-strength testing at specified ages, alongside fresh-concrete checks like slump at the point of delivery.

What is a cube test?

A cube test measures the compressive strength of a standard 150mm concrete cube specimen, typically at 28 days, used to verify the mix meets its characteristic strength.

Why shouldn't one cube result be judged in isolation?

Characteristic strength is a statistical value — up to 5% of results are expected to fall below it by design. A single result needs to be read against the full batch of test results, not treated as a pass/fail on its own.
