What Is 40mm Aggregate?

40mm aggregate is crushed stone graded to a nominal size of 40mm, used as the coarse aggregate component in mass concrete, PCC and sub-base work.

IS 383:2016, the Indian standard for aggregates in concrete, specifies coarse aggregate in nominal sizes of 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm, as either single-size or graded material — 40mm is one of these standard nominal sizes.

The figure refers to nominal size, not a guarantee that every particle measures 40mm. Nominal size describes the sieve the bulk of the material passes, with a defined distribution above and below.

Size matters for a practical reason. Larger aggregate presents less surface area per unit volume, so it typically requires less cement paste to coat — meaning a 40mm mix generally carries a lower paste demand than an equivalent 20mm mix, which is why 40mm is generally the more economical choice per cubic metre where the specification permits it. What it gives up is placement: it needs greater clear spacing and section depth, and does not move into congested reinforcement the way smaller aggregate does.

In Indian construction trade usage, terms such as metal or blue metal are commonly used for crushed stone aggregate, and 40mm metal generally refers to this product. Terminology varies by region and application, so confirm the size specification rather than relying on trade names alone when ordering.

40mm Aggregate Size and Grading

Nominal size is the start; grading determines behaviour.

IS 383:2016 sets grading limits for coarse aggregate by sieve analysis, defining the percentage of material expected to pass each sieve for a given nominal size. Well-graded aggregate generally produces better workability and more consistent results than poorly graded material. 40mm is used both as a single-size aggregate and blended with smaller fractions in a graded mix, depending on the mix design and application.

Reinforced-concrete use of 40mm aggregate is governed by member dimensions and reinforcement spacing under IS 456:2000 — larger aggregate needs more generous clear spacing and section depth, so suitability should always be verified against the project specification before ordering for an RCC member. Beyond grading, coarse aggregate for structural or mass concrete is generally assessed on particle shape, cleanliness and freedom from deleterious material, water absorption, specific gravity, and mechanical performance through crushing, impact and abrasion testing, using the relevant IS 2386 methods.

Why 40mm Isn't Automatic for Every RCC Member

One rule decides it — not the concrete grade.

IS 456:2000, Clause 5.3.1 sets the maximum aggregate size for a reinforced-concrete member as the smallest of three checks: one-quarter of the member's minimum dimension, the clear spacing between reinforcement bars minus 5mm, and the clear cover minus 5mm. Whichever of the three gives the smallest figure is the actual ceiling for that member — not the concrete grade, and not whichever aggregate happens to be in stock.

The smallest figure governs — here, 20mm. That specific column requires 20mm aggregate regardless of concrete grade; 40mm would not meet its own geometry. Run this same check against your own member before specifying 40mm.

This example is illustrative only — actual aggregate selection must follow the structural drawings, concrete mix design and project specification prepared by the responsible engineer. 40mm aggregate's natural home is mass concrete and other large, less congested pours, which is precisely the domain IS 383:2016 Table 8 addresses for mass-concrete aggregate sizing.

Used where volume and economy matter most.

How 40mm Aggregate Compares

Three materials it's often confused with.

40mm Aggregate vs GSB

Not Interchangeable

40mm aggregate is a single coarse-aggregate size; GSB (Granular Sub-Base) is a blended, graded sub-base material meeting a sieve/grading curve under MoRTH Clause 401. A 40mm fraction can be one input into a GSB blend, but 40mm aggregate on its own does not meet Clause 401's grading requirement and is not GSB. See the GSB page for the full specification.

40mm Aggregate vs WMM

Not Interchangeable

Wet Mix Macadam (WMM) is a plant-mixed, moisture-conditioned base-course material under MoRTH Clause 406, produced by blending multiple aggregate fractions with water to a specified grading. 40mm aggregate is one of the size fractions that can feed into a WMM blend, but a single 40mm fraction is not WMM on its own. See the WMM page for what goes into the mix.

40mm Aggregate vs Boulders

Different Product

40mm aggregate is processed, crushed and screened to a sized fraction. Boulders are substantially larger individual stones, generally used whole rather than as a sized aggregate fraction, in applications such as pitching, gabion work and soling. They are not interchangeable products. See the Boulders page for boulder types and applications.

Not Sure Which Size You Need?

Follow your engineer's specification first.

If your engineer or mix design has specified the aggregate size, follow that specification. Aggregate size is a design decision and nothing on this page substitutes for it.

If the requirement is still being assessed, these are the questions that usually determine it, and the ones worth putting to your engineer:

  • Is this mass concrete, PCC or an unreinforced/lightly reinforced section? This generally points toward 40mm or larger.
  • Is this conventional reinforced concrete at standard member sizes? Standard slabs, beams and columns commonly use 20mm.
  • How much clear spacing and section depth does the member have? Larger aggregate needs generous clearance, per IS 456:2000.
  • Is the material going into a road sub-base, embankment or drainage layer? 40mm and larger grades are commonly specified for these.
  • Are sizes being blended? Some mix designs combine 40mm with a smaller fraction — the mix design determines the blend.
