What Is 20mm Aggregate?

20mm aggregate is crushed stone graded to a nominal size of 20mm, used as a coarse aggregate in reinforced concrete.

Aggregate is the stone fraction of concrete and accounts for most of a mix by volume. Coarse aggregate is the fraction retained on a 4.75mm sieve; fine aggregate such as sand passes it. 20mm aggregate is a coarse aggregate, and across Indian construction it is among the most widely specified coarse aggregate sizes for structural concrete.

The figure refers to nominal size, not a guarantee that every particle measures 20mm. IS 383:2016, the Indian standard for aggregates in concrete, specifies coarse aggregate in nominal sizes of 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm.

20mm is commonly specified because it balances two competing requirements. Larger aggregate has less surface area per unit volume, so it generally requires less cement paste to coat, which tends to make it more economical per cubic metre than smaller sizes. At the same time it remains small enough to place through the reinforcement spacing found in conventional members. Where that balance does not hold — thin sections, congested steel, or a specification that says otherwise — a different size is used.

In Indian construction trade usage, terms such as metal, blue metal or jelly are commonly used for crushed stone aggregate, and 20mm metal generally refers to this product. Terminology varies by region and application, so confirm the size specification rather than relying on trade names alone when ordering.

Single-Size and Graded 20mm Aggregate

The distinction worth resolving before you order.

This causes more ordering confusion than any other point on this page. Single-size aggregate of nominal size 20mm describes material where the bulk passes the 20mm sieve and is largely retained on the sieve below it — a narrow band of one size. Graded aggregate of nominal size 20mm describes material containing a designed distribution of sizes below 20mm as well, within the limits IS 383:2016 sets for graded material.

Crushing plants generally produce single-size grades, which are then blended in proportion at the mix design stage to reach the combined grading a concrete design requires. This is why RCC work is commonly supplied as separate 20mm and 10mm deliveries rather than one pre-blended product, and why a mix design specifies proportions rather than a single material.

If your specification calls for graded aggregate rather than single-size, state that requirement with your enquiry so the material and quotation can be confirmed accordingly.

Size, Grading and Applicable Standards

Grading limits, particle shape and test methods.

IS 383:2016 sets grading limits for coarse aggregate by sieve analysis, defining the percentage of material expected to pass each sieve for a given nominal size, with separate limit sets for single-size and graded material. The standard also includes requirements relating to particle shape, including flakiness and elongation, cleanliness and freedom from deleterious material, water absorption and specific gravity, and mechanical performance assessed through crushing, impact and abrasion testing. Test methods sit in the IS 2386 series.

These are general standards and technical references. For current grading information or available documentation for the material being supplied, contact OM Group with the project requirement.

What Is 20mm Aggregate Used For?

The coarse aggregate for conventional RCC.

20mm aggregate is used mainly as the coarse aggregate in reinforced concrete, and in specified road and drainage applications. Where sections are thin or reinforcement is congested, 10mm aggregate is generally more appropriate.

RCC slabs, beams and columns

Commonly used as the coarse aggregate for conventional structural concrete, including standard grades such as M20, where member dimensions and reinforcement spacing permit it.

Foundations and footings

Standard footings and raft slabs where section depth accommodates 20mm.

Mass concrete and PCC

Used where placement is unrestricted and a larger size is either not permitted or not preferred by the specification.

Ready-mix concrete

RMC plants commonly use 20mm as the principal coarse aggregate, blended in proportion with 10mm to reach a designed combined grading.

Road and infrastructure works

May be used in specified road and infrastructure applications alongside products such as GSB and WMM, depending on the project specification. These are distinct materials with their own requirements and are not interchangeable with 20mm aggregate.

Drainage applications

Can be used in drainage such as French drains, soakaways and pipe surround where a clean, low-fines, open-graded aggregate is specified. Materials containing fines, such as GSB or crusher run, are not substitutes.

Choosing the Right Aggregate Size

Follow the specification first.

If your engineer or mix design has specified the aggregate size, follow that specification. Aggregate size is a design decision and nothing on this page substitutes for it.

For context, IS 456:2000 constrains the maximum nominal size of coarse aggregate against the member being cast. The limits generally applied are:

  • Not more than one quarter of the minimum dimension of the member
  • For heavily reinforced members, 5mm less than the minimum clear distance between main bars, or 5mm less than the minimum cover, whichever is smaller

20mm is commonly suitable for conventional reinforced-concrete members when member dimensions, reinforcement spacing, cover and the approved project specification permit it. Congested reinforcement and genuinely thin sections generally require a smaller size.

One practice worth naming: replacing a specified aggregate size with 40mm simply to reduce material cost may conflict with the project specification or applicable size limitations. Blending leftover sizes on site to extend a stockpile produces an untested gradation with unpredictable water demand and workability. Final selection should follow the approved design and mix requirements, and any deliberate blending belongs in a designed mix trial.

20mm vs 40mm Aggregate

Economy applies where the size is appropriate.

40mm aggregate is generally more economical per cubic metre than 20mm because larger stone requires less cement paste. That economy applies only where 40mm is appropriate for the member being cast and permitted by the specification.
