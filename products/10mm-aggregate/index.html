What Is 10mm Aggregate?

10mm aggregate is crushed stone graded to a nominal size of 10mm, used as the coarse aggregate component in concrete and related construction work.

Aggregate is the stone fraction of concrete and accounts for most of a mix by volume. Cement and water bind it; the aggregate carries the bulk and controls the cost. Aggregate divides into two categories by particle size: fine aggregate such as sand, which passes a 4.75mm sieve, and coarse aggregate, which is retained on it. 10mm aggregate is a coarse aggregate.

The figure refers to nominal size, not a guarantee that every particle measures 10mm. Nominal size describes the sieve the bulk of the material passes, with a defined distribution above and below. IS 383:2016, the Indian standard for aggregates in concrete, specifies coarse aggregate in nominal sizes of 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm, as either single-size or graded material.

Size matters for a practical reason. Smaller aggregate presents more surface area per unit volume, so it typically requires more cement paste to coat — meaning a 10mm mix generally carries a higher paste demand than an equivalent 20mm mix. What it gives back is placement: it moves into tighter spaces and around closer reinforcement.

In Indian construction trade usage, terms such as metal, blue metal or jelly are commonly used for crushed stone aggregate, and 10mm metal generally refers to this product. Terminology varies by region and application, so confirm the size specification rather than relying on trade names alone when ordering.

10mm Aggregate Size and Grading

Nominal size is the start; grading determines behaviour.

IS 383:2016 sets grading limits for coarse aggregate by sieve analysis, defining the percentage of material expected to pass each sieve for a given nominal size. Well-graded aggregate generally produces better workability and more consistent results than poorly graded material. Concrete mix designs commonly combine two nominal sizes — typically 20mm and 10mm in proportion — to achieve a combined grading curve that neither delivers alone. This is routine at RMC plants and is the main reason 10mm is ordered alongside 20mm rather than instead of it.

Beyond grading, coarse aggregate for structural concrete is generally assessed on particle shape (flakiness and elongation), cleanliness and freedom from deleterious material, water absorption, specific gravity, and mechanical performance through crushing, impact and abrasion testing, using the relevant IS 2386 methods.

What Is 10mm Aggregate Used For?

Used where stone size is the deciding factor.

10mm aggregate is used where stone size is the deciding factor — concrete that must reach into confined sections, place around dense reinforcement, or finish to a fine surface. 10mm is not a general substitute for 20mm: for standard slabs, beams, foundations and mass pours, 20mm is typically the more economical and appropriate choice. Many projects need both.

RCC with congested reinforcement

Smaller aggregate can improve placement through tight bar spacing where mix design and member geometry require it. IS 456:2000 limits maximum aggregate size relative to member dimension and bar spacing.

Thin structural sections

Slabs, screeds, lintels, sunshades and chajjas. Section thickness is conventionally kept to several times the maximum aggregate size, which commonly rules 20mm out of genuinely thin work.

Precast elements

Precast production frequently uses smaller aggregate for mould fill, edge definition and surface quality on faces that remain exposed.

Paver blocks and cast products

10mm and finer grades are commonly used in paver and block manufacture, suited to the compaction and finish requirements of cast products.

Blended grading in RMC

10mm may be included in a blended aggregate grading for ready-mix concrete, including for pumped concrete, depending on the mix design.

Concrete requiring a finer surface

Where the concrete face is the finished surface, smaller aggregate can support a finer finish where mix design and finishing method are appropriate.

10mm vs 20mm Aggregate

Most structural work uses both.

20mm is typically the default for standard RCC slabs, beams, foundations and mass concrete, and is generally more economical because it demands less cement paste. 10mm is specified when placement or finish is the constraint. Not Sure Which Size You Need?

Follow your engineer's specification first.

If your engineer or mix design has specified the aggregate size, follow that specification. Aggregate size is a design decision and nothing on this page substitutes for it.

If the requirement is still being assessed, these are the questions that usually determine it, and the ones worth putting to your engineer:

  • How thick is the member, and how tight is the reinforcement? Congested steel or thin sections generally point toward smaller aggregate.
  • Is this general structural concrete at volume? Standard slabs, beams and foundations commonly use 20mm.
  • Is the concrete face the finished surface? Finish requirements may favour smaller aggregate.
  • Is the concrete being pumped? The mix design will determine the blend.
  • Are sizes being blended? Most RCC work uses both in proportion, and both can be dispatched on one order.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions, answered directly.

What is 10mm aggregate?

10mm aggregate is crushed stone graded to a nominal size of 10mm, used as the coarse aggregate component in concrete. It is classified as coarse aggregate because it is retained on a 4.75mm sieve.

What is 10mm aggregate used for?

10mm aggregate is used in RCC with closely spaced reinforcement, thin structural sections, precast elements, paver and block manufacture, and blended with 20mm aggregate in concrete mix designs. It is selected where a smaller stone suits the section or the finish.

What size is 10mm aggregate?

10mm is the nominal size — the sieve size the bulk of the material passes. It does not mean every particle measures exactly 10mm. IS 383:2016 defines coarse aggregate nominal sizes of 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm.

Is 10mm aggregate used for concrete?

Yes. 10mm aggregate is a standard coarse aggregate for concrete, used alone in thin or detailed work and blended with 20mm in general structural mixes.

Is 10mm aggregate used for RCC?

Yes. 10mm aggregate is used in RCC, particularly in members with congested reinforcement or thin sections where larger aggregate is harder to place cleanly around the steel.

What is the difference between 10mm and 20mm aggregate?

20mm aggregate is typically the default for standard RCC slabs, beams and foundations and is generally more economical because it requires less cement paste. 10mm aggregate is used where placement or finish matters more — congested reinforcement, thin sections, precast and blended grading. Most structural work uses both, in proportions set by the mix design.

Is 10mm aggregate suitable for RMC?

Yes. RMC plants commonly blend 10mm with 20mm aggregate to achieve a target combined grading. Whether 10mm is included, and in what proportion, depends on the mix design.

Can 10mm aggregate be used for paver blocks?

Yes. 10mm and finer grades are commonly used in paver and block manufacture, suited to the compaction and finish requirements of cast products.
