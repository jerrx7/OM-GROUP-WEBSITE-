OM Group manufactures Plaster Sand, also supplied commercially as P-Sand, for internal, external and ceiling plastering across North and South Goa. Plastering requires a different sand grading than structural concrete work, and Plaster Sand is produced and classified against IS 1542:1992, the Indian Standard specific to sand for plaster.

What Is Plaster Sand? What Is P-Sand?

Plaster Sand is sand classified to meet IS 1542:1992's requirements for use in plaster mortar — distinct from concrete-grade sand governed by IS 383:2016.

OM Group manufactures and supplies it under the commercial name P-Sand, alongside its Plaster Sand designation.

IS 1542:1992 recognises natural sand, crushed stone sand and crushed gravel sand as eligible sources, provided the material meets the standard's grading and cleanliness requirements — meaning plaster sand is properly understood as a grading and cleanliness specification, not a single fixed raw material.

Why Plastering Needs a Different Grading

A thin, trowel-applied layer behaves differently.

Plastering is a thin, trowel-applied layer, and the sand's particle-size distribution has a direct effect on finish, workability and cracking risk in a way that matters less in a thick structural pour.

IS 1542:1992 requires sand for plaster to be hard, durable and clean, with combined clay, silt, dust and organic impurities not exceeding 5% by mass. Grading is established by sieve analysis under IS 2386 (Part 1), with a minimum fineness modulus of 1.4 for crushed stone or crushed gravel sand and 1.5 for natural sand. The limit on material passing the 150-micron sieve is relaxed to 20% specifically for crushed sources, since crushed rock fines behave differently from the clay-bearing fines typical of natural sand.

A controlled amount of fine material is expected, not eliminated. IS 1542 sets a minimum fineness modulus and a maximum fines limit — it does not require zero fines. Concrete-grade M-Sand is not automatically the same as plaster-grade sand; the two are classified against different targets.

Plaster Sand vs M-Sand

Same broad family, different targets.

Plaster Sand and M-Sand belong to the same broad material family — crushed stone fine aggregate — classified against two different targets: IS 383's Zone II for structural concrete, and IS 1542's finer, differently-fined envelope for plastering. OM Group manufactures both, produced and graded for their respective applications.

Using concrete-grade material as-is for plastering can produce a rougher finish and higher rebound loss, since it isn't classified to the specification plastering requires. For full technical detail on M-Sand and IS 383 grading, see M-Sand.

Plaster Sand vs Washed Sand

A process and a specification.

"Washed" describes a processing stage; "plaster-grade" describes a grading specification. Washed material may or may not meet IS 1542's grading requirements on its own — washing affects cleanliness and fines content, not particle-size distribution by itself. Washed Sand and Plaster Sand can therefore overlap in a given batch of material without being interchangeable terms. See Washed Sand for detail on OM Group's washing specification.

Plaster Sand vs Concrete Sand

Selected against different mix requirements.

Plaster Sand is selected for plastering and mortar requirements, while concrete sand is selected for concrete mix requirements. The two should not be treated as interchangeable simply because both are fine aggregates.

Plaster Sand vs Stone Dust

Not a substitute, under any circumstance.

Stone dust and quarry dust are the uncontrolled fine by-product of crushing, not classified to any grading standard, and not a substitute for Plaster Sand under any circumstance. Substituting dust for graded plaster sand produces unpredictable water demand, poor workability, and a compromised, often cracked finish — a costlier failure to redo than most structural grading errors, since it's visible on the finished wall.

What is IS 1542?

IS 1542:1992 is the Indian Standard specifying requirements for sand used in plaster mortar, covering natural, crushed stone and crushed gravel sand sources.

What grading does Plaster Sand require?

IS 1542:1992 sets grading limits via sieve analysis, a minimum fineness modulus of 1.4 for crushed sources and 1.5 for natural sand, and a combined fines limit of 5%.

What is fineness modulus?

Fineness modulus is a numerical index of a sand's average particle size, derived from sieve analysis, used to assess grading suitability.

What is the fineness modulus of OM Group's Plaster Sand?

OM Group does not publish a fixed fineness-modulus value as a permanent product property; current test data can be confirmed against the material being supplied.

Does Plaster Sand contain silt?

IS 1542 permits combined clay, silt, dust and organic impurities up to 5% by mass — a controlled amount, not zero.

Does Plaster Sand contain fines?

Yes — a controlled proportion of fines is normal and expected under IS 1542, contributing to workability.

Can M-Sand be used for plastering?

Concrete-grade M-Sand is generally coarser than material classified to IS 1542. Plaster-specific grading is what plastering specifications generally call for.

What is the difference between Plaster Sand and M-Sand?

Both are manufactured from crushed stone; Plaster Sand is classified to IS 1542 for plastering, while M-Sand is typically classified to IS 383 Zone II for concrete.

What is the difference between Plaster Sand and Washed Sand?

What is the difference between Plaster Sand and Concrete Sand?

Plaster Sand is selected for plastering and mortar requirements; concrete sand is selected for concrete mix requirements. Both are fine aggregates, but they are not interchangeable.

What is the difference between Plaster Sand and river sand?

River sand is naturally deposited; Plaster Sand is manufactured and classified to IS 1542. Both are eligible sources under the standard provided grading and cleanliness requirements are met.

What is the difference between Plaster Sand and stone dust?
