What Is 12.5mm Aggregate?

12.5mm aggregate is crushed stone graded to a nominal size of 12.5mm, used as coarse aggregate in concrete where an intermediate size between 10mm and 20mm is appropriate.

Aggregate is the stone fraction of concrete and accounts for most of a mix by volume. The 4.75mm sieve divides fine aggregate, such as sand, from coarse aggregate. 12.5mm is a coarse aggregate, and it is one of the nominal sizes IS 383:2016 tabulates — alongside 10, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm.

The figure refers to nominal size, not a guarantee that every particle measures 12.5mm. Nominal size describes the sieve the bulk of the material passes, with a defined distribution above and below.

12.5mm exists as a distinct grade for a practical reason. Larger aggregate generally requires less cement paste and tends to be more economical, but it has to suit the member being cast. Where member geometry, reinforcement spacing or cover make a 20mm nominal size unsuitable, moving straight to 10mm can be finer than the design requires. 12.5mm can provide an intermediate option in that situation.

Is 12mm Aggregate the Same as 12.5mm?

In trade usage, "12mm" is shorthand for the 12.5mm nominal size listed in IS 383:2016 — the grading should still be confirmed.

The two designations come from different places. IS 383:2016 sets out nominal coarse aggregate sizes of 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm — there is no 12mm nominal size in the standard. Suppliers, quotations, site vocabulary and search behaviour almost universally say 12mm.

Three things follow from that, and they matter commercially:

The trade name is a label, not a grading. Two suppliers both describing "12mm" may be screening to different sieve ranges. The name alone does not tell you what you are receiving.

A specification naming 12.5mm is naming the IS 383 nominal size, and the grading envelope attached to that designation is what applies.

Comparison between quotations needs the grading, not the label. If rate differs noticeably between suppliers quoting "12mm," the sieve range is the first thing worth checking.

If your specification requires a particular grading or sieve range, state it with your enquiry and it can be confirmed against the material being supplied.

Size, Grading and Applicable Standards

Why is there no 12mm size in IS 383?

There is no 12mm nominal size listed in IS 383:2016. The standard tabulates 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 40 and 63mm, and 12.5mm is the nominal designation commonly represented in trade as 12mm.

IS 383:2016 sets grading limits for coarse aggregate by sieve analysis, defining the percentage of material expected to pass each sieve for a given nominal size, with separate limit sets for single-size and graded material. The standard also covers particle shape including flakiness and elongation, cleanliness and freedom from deleterious material, water absorption and specific gravity, and mechanical performance assessed through crushing, impact and abrasion testing. Test methods sit in the IS 2386 series.

Crushing plants generally produce single-size grades, which are blended in proportion at mix design to reach the combined grading a concrete design requires. A 12.5mm product is normally one component of that blend rather than the whole coarse fraction.

Choosing Between 10mm, 12.5mm and 20mm

Follow the specification first.

If your engineer or approved mix design has specified the aggregate size, follow that specification. Aggregate size is a design decision and nothing on this page substitutes for it.

Where the size is still being worked out, IS 456:2000 constrains the maximum nominal size of coarse aggregate against the member being cast. The limits generally applied are:

  • Not more than one quarter of the minimum dimension of the member
  • For heavily reinforced members, 5mm less than the minimum clear distance between main bars, or 5mm less than the minimum cover, whichever is smaller

12.5mm may become relevant where the applicable member, reinforcement, cover and project requirements rule out a 20mm nominal size but do not require a smaller size. For example, where the applicable clear spacing limits the maximum nominal aggregate size below 20mm, 12.5mm may become an appropriate intermediate option, subject to the approved structural design and mix requirements.

The general trade-off: 20mm tends to be the most economical where it fits, 10mm is the most accommodating of tight placement, and 12.5mm can sit between the two. Share the member details and application with your enquiry and OM Group can discuss the material requirement and quantities with you.
