What Is Road Base Material?

Road base material is a broad construction term for graded granular materials used within the base or sub-base structure of a road pavement. Depending on the pavement design and project specification, the required material may be GSB, WMM or another specified graded aggregate.

It is a category term, not automatically one material. The pavement layer a project is building determines which material applies — the sub-base position calls for GSB, the base course above it calls for WMM — and the project specification determines the grading and physical properties required within either.

A supplier's job is to match the material to the specified application, not to sell whichever product is easiest to source. This page exists to make that match correctly: it explains the real distinction between GSB and WMM, and routes you to the page with the technical depth your project needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions, answered directly.

Definition

What is road base material?

Road base material is a broad construction term for graded granular materials used in the base or sub-base layers of a road pavement. Depending on the project specification, it may refer to GSB, WMM or another specified graded aggregate.

Is road base material one specific product?

No. It is a category term, not one manufactured product. The specific material required depends on the pavement layer and the project's specification.

What determines which road base material a project needs?

The pavement layer being constructed (sub-base or base course) and the project's contract specification together determine whether GSB, WMM or another graded aggregate applies.

What is the difference between road base material and GSB?

"Road base material" is a broad category term; GSB is one specific, defined material within it, governed by MoRTH Clause 401 and used at the sub-base position.

What is the difference between road base material and WMM?

"Road base material" is the broad category; WMM is one specific material within it, governed by MoRTH Clause 406 and used as the base course above GSB.

GSB

Is GSB a road base material?

Yes — GSB is the granular sub-base layer, one of the specific materials the broader term "road base material" can refer to.

What is GSB?

GSB (Granular Sub-Base) is a graded granular material placed and compacted over prepared subgrade to form the sub-base layer of a road pavement. Full detail is on the GSB page.

What is MoRTH Clause 401?

MoRTH Clause 401 is the principal Indian reference governing Granular Sub-Base material, grading and construction requirements.

Where is GSB used in a pavement?

GSB is generally used as the sub-base layer, above the subgrade and below the base course (typically WMM).

Does OM Group supply GSB?

Yes. OM Group supplies GSB across Goa — see the GSB page for grading, testing and specification detail.

WMM

Is WMM a road base material?

Yes — WMM is the base-course material above GSB, also within the broader "road base material" category.

What is WMM?

WMM (Wet Mix Macadam) is a pavement base-course material made by mixing clean, crushed, graded aggregate with controlled water and compacting it, per MoRTH Clause 406. Full detail is on the WMM page.

What is MoRTH Clause 406?

MoRTH Clause 406 is the principal Indian reference governing Wet Mix Macadam production, grading and construction requirements.

Where is WMM used in a pavement?

WMM is generally used as the base course, above the granular sub-base and below the bituminous or surface layer.

Does OM Group supply aggregate for WMM?

Yes. OM Group supplies the crushed, graded aggregate fractions used in WMM production — see the WMM page for the full aggregate-supply detail.

Comparison

What is the difference between GSB and WMM?

GSB is the sub-base layer, governed by MoRTH Clause 401; WMM is the base course above it, governed by Clause 406, more rigorously graded and plant-mixed with controlled moisture. They are not interchangeable.

Can WMM replace GSB?

No, not without engineering/specification approval — GSB and WMM serve different pavement positions with different grading and physical requirements.

Can GSB replace WMM?

No, for the same reason — the two materials occupy different, specified positions in the pavement structure.

Is crusher-run the same as road base material?

Not automatically. Crusher-run is an uncontrolled mixed-size crusher output; it must be blended to a specified grading (GSB's or WMM's) before it meets either specification.

What is road metal?

"Road metal" is a term with genuine regional and historical variation in Indian construction trade usage — it is not automatically equivalent to GSB or WMM.

Is recycled aggregate used as road base material?

Recycled aggregate is used in some jurisdictions and project specifications, where the applicable specification explicitly permits it. It is neither promoted nor dismissed here — acceptance is project- and specification-specific.

Technical

What material is used below WMM?

GSB (Granular Sub-Base) is generally used below WMM, forming the sub-base layer beneath the WMM base course.

What is used as a road sub-base?

GSB, governed by MoRTH Clause 401, is the standard material used as a road sub-base in Indian practice.

What is used as a road base course?

WMM, governed by MoRTH Clause 406, is generally used as the base course above the sub-base, subject to the project's pavement design.

What is CBR in road construction?

CBR (California Bearing Ratio) measures the load-bearing resistance of a compacted material relative to a reference crushed-stone standard, used to evaluate whether a pavement layer provides the support the design requires.

Why does grading matter for road base material?

Grading controls density, load distribution and drainage behaviour once a layer is compacted. Both GSB and WMM are specified to grading envelopes for this reason, not supplied as a single loose aggregate size.

How is road aggregate tested?

Common tests include sieve analysis (grading), CBR, Aggregate Impact Value, Los Angeles Abrasion, flakiness and elongation, plasticity index and moisture/compaction testing — which apply, and to what limits, depends on whether the material is GSB, WMM or another specified aggregate.
